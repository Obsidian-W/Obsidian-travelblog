This route runs north from Vientiane to Luang Prabang, the Nam Ou villages and Luang Namtha, then doubles back south for Thakhek, the Bolaven Plateau and Don Khon. It takes **17 days**, with the longest transfer between Luang Namtha and Thakhek.

## Day 1: Vientiane

Start with **Wat Sisaket** and **Ho Phra Keo**, then walk past the presidential palace and through the central streets towards **Patuxai**. Continue east to **Pha That Luang** and the surrounding temples before returning to the Mekong riverfront and night market.

**Pha That Luang** is around four kilometres east of the centre; the rest of the route links the central temples, Patuxai and riverfront on foot.

For **Luang Prabang**, take a morning **Laos–China Railway train**, around **two hours**. Vientiane station is outside the city: allow roughly **an hour** for the station bus or an accommodation-arranged transfer. Tickets are sold through **LCR Ticket**, station counters and local travel agencies.

---

## Day 2: Luang Prabang

Take the morning train to **Luang Prabang**, then the shared station minibus into town, around **0:30–0:45**. Walk along the peninsula to **Wat Xieng Thong** before taking the shared minivan to **Kuang Si Falls** and the Hmong village stop included in the excursion. Kuang Si drops over a tall limestone cascade into a series of pale-blue pools. The falls and village excursion cost **15$ per person in 2025**. Kuang Si is around **an hour by road** from the city. [Waterfall transfers and excursions can also be booked here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/kuang-si-falls-l84641/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

---

## Day 3: Luang Prabang

Take the morning boat up the Mekong to the **Pak Ou Caves**, where hundreds of small Buddha statues stand in two chambers above the river. The lower cave opens onto the Mekong; stairs climb to the darker upper cave. Back in Luang Prabang, visit the **Royal Palace Museum**, the temples along the peninsula and the old streets between the Mekong and Nam Khan, then climb **Mount Phousi** late in the afternoon.

[Pak Ou boat trips are available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/pak-ou-caves-l89468/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). The upstream boat journey takes around **two hours**, with a faster return downstream.

Buy the Nong Khiaw bus ticket in Luang Prabang. In 2025, departures were available at 09:00 and 11:00.

---

## Day 4: Nong Khiaw

Take the morning bus from Luang Prabang to Nong Khiaw. The journey takes around four hours. Cross the bridge over the **Nam Ou River** for the view of the village beneath the limestone cliffs, then climb one of the marked viewpoints above town. Buy the viewpoint ticket at the trail entrance and start early enough to descend before dark.

---

## Day 5: Muang Ngoi

Take the morning boat from Nong Khiaw to **Muang Ngoi**. Walk through the village, climb the nearby viewpoint and follow the inland path through the caves and rice fields towards **Ban Na village** before returning to the pier.

I treated Muang Ngoi as a day trip and slept again in Nong Khiaw. The return boat journey was around three hours in total and cost **6$ in 2025**.

---

## Day 6: Luang Namtha

Take the morning bus from **Nong Khiaw to Luang Namtha**, around **seven hours** through the mountain roads. After arrival, walk around the central market, the river and the town's temples, then arrange the following day's village circuit.

---

## Day 7: Luang Namtha

Spend the morning cycling to **Ban Nam Dee**, about **5,5 km outside Luang Namtha**, a Lanten village known for indigo-dyed textiles and bamboo-paper making. The hilltop **Luang Namtha stupa**, reached from the north end of town, overlooks the valley.

Leave for **Boten station** in the afternoon, roughly **an hour by road**, then take the train to **Vientiane**. Boten–Vientiane took around **four hours** and cost about **20$ in 2025**. Continue from Vientiane's **Southern Bus Station** on the overnight bus to **Thakhek**, around **six to seven hours**.

---

## Day 8: Thakhek

Arrive in **Thakhek** after the overnight bus, then take the local connection from the bus station into the riverside centre.

Walk through Thakhek's old centre, past the colonial-era shopfronts and Catholic church, then along the **Mekong riverfront**, facing Nakhon Phanom across the water. I booked the next three days locally instead of riding the loop independently.

---

## Days 9-11: Thakhek Loop

I booked these three days locally. The tour left Thakhek through the limestone landscape and caves around Mahaxai, including the **Tham Xang** and **Tham Nang Aen** area, then crossed the **Nakai Plateau** towards Thalang.

Around **Thalang**, flooded trees rise out of the reservoir beside the road. Farther west, the road descends towards the limestone valley around **Kong Lor**.

The final major stop was **Kong Lor Cave**, crossed by small boat along its underground river. Inside, the boat stops for a walk through illuminated rock formations before continuing through the mountain. The operator handled the vehicle and accommodation throughout the loop. After returning to Thakhek, take the night bus south to **Pakse**, roughly **eight hours**.

---

## Day 12: Pakse

Visit **Pakse's centre** and **Wat Luang** in the morning. In the afternoon, take an accommodation-arranged excursion to **Vat Phou**, around **45 km south of Pakse**, beyond Champasak. The path climbs from the large reservoirs past the lower stone buildings to the upper sanctuary beneath the cliff, overlooking the plain. Return to Pakse afterwards.

---

## Day 13: Bolaven Plateau

Begin the small Bolaven loop from **Pakse** with a guesthouse-arranged vehicle or rented scooter. Visit **Tad Fane**, whose twin waterfalls drop into a deep gorge, then **Tad Yuang**, with steps down to a closer viewpoint. Continue through the coffee-growing area to **Paksong** for the night.

---

## Day 14: Bolaven Plateau

Continue towards **Tad Lo**. Walk along the river between the village and the nearby **Tad Hang** waterfall, then return to Pakse on the western side of the small loop. Coffee farms along the plateau offer visits to the growing and processing areas; arrange one through the overnight guesthouse.

---

## Day 15: Don Khon

Take a morning minibus from Pakse to **Nakasong**, then the short passenger boat into the 4,000 Islands. Continue to Don Khon and walk through the main village and along the river. I chose Don Khon because it was quieter than Don Det.

Combined bus-and-boat tickets are sold in **Pakse**. Allow around **three to four hours** to **Nakasong**, then roughly **0:20–0:30** by boat to **Don Khon**.

---

## Day 16: Don Khon

Rent a bicycle and follow the old railway alignment from the bridge between Don Det and **Don Khon**. Stop at the preserved locomotive, then continue to **Li Phi Falls**, where the Mekong splits into rapids between the rocks.

---

## Day 17: Don Khon

Follow the former railway south to **Ban Hang Khon**. The old landing stage was part of the French-era route for moving cargo around the Mekong rapids; the river here breaks into channels around the islands. Return by boat to Nakasong, then use the northbound bus for Pakse and Vientiane or the southbound connection for the Cambodian border.

## With more days

### Luang Prabang: another night

Separate Kuang Si from the morning train arrival. The waterfall paths, swimming pools and bear-rescue centre fill an afternoon, while the peninsula temples and Royal Palace Museum then have their own day.

### Stay overnight in Muang Ngoi

Stay in **Muang Ngoi** after the Ban Na walk for the village evening. Return to **Nong Khiaw** the following day before the onward bus to Luang Namtha.

### A fourth day on the Thakhek Loop

Put the additional night near **Kong Lor**. This separates the cave boat trip from the return to Thakhek, before the overnight journey to Pakse.
