## Day 1: Manila

Start inside the walls of **Intramuros**. Walk from **Fort Santiago** through Plaza Roma to **Manila Cathedral**, then continue to **San Agustin Church** and Casa Manila. The walls, gates and damaged buildings make more sense as one continuous walk than as separate taxi stops.

From the airport, use the official airport transport desk or a ride booked in the app before leaving the terminal. Manila traffic can turn a short distance into the longest part of the arrival, so keep Intramuros as the only major area on Day 1.

I disliked most of Manila. The traffic, rubbish and visible sex-tourism scene made it an unpleasant start. I repeatedly saw older foreign men seeking very young company; that was my observation during this visit, and it heavily coloured my opinion of the city.

## Day 2: Manila

Use the museum quarter for the second day. The **National Museum of Anthropology** and **National Museum of Fine Arts** stand on opposite sides of Rizal Park, so they can be combined without another journey across Manila. Walk through **Rizal Park** afterwards and return to the accommodation before the evening traffic becomes worse.

In the evening, I went to see midget boxing. It was clearly unethical, yet I found the show fun at the time. Both parts belong in the account; pretending otherwise would make the anecdote cleaner than the experience was.

Take the night bus from Manila to Banaue. The buses leave from their own operator terminals rather than one central station, so confirm the exact terminal on the ticket.

## Day 3: Banaue

Arrive in Banaue in the morning and leave the bag at the accommodation before visiting the main rice-terrace viewpoints above town. The viewpoints follow the road north of Banaue; hire one registered tricycle for the circuit rather than negotiating a new ride at each stop. Walk around the town and market afterwards, once the overnight-bus fatigue catches up.

## Day 4: Banaue

Use a local guide for a longer walk through the terraces and villages around Banaue. Confirm the exact route before leaving: **Batad** involves a road transfer and a steeper walk, while the viewpoints immediately around Banaue are a much shorter outing.

One evening I ordered chicken adobo. The owner's children killed the chicken beside me after I ordered it. I felt bad afterwards; that is the entire point of the anecdote, without adding a lesson that was never there.

Take the night bus back to Manila for the flight south.

## Day 5: Cebu

Fly from Manila to Cebu. From Mactan-Cebu Airport, take the airport bus towards Cebu City and continue locally to the accommodation. Keep the day's walk in the old centre: **Basilica Minore del Santo Niño**, **Magellan's Cross**, Cebu Metropolitan Cathedral and **Fort San Pedro** are close enough to visit in sequence.

## Day 6: Cebu

Start at **Carbon Market** while it is active, then take a jeepney along the main road towards Lahug for the **Cebu Taoist Temple**. The final uphill section requires walking or a local connection. Continue towards one of the Busay viewpoints only if the weather is clear; otherwise return to the centre for the **Casa Gorordo Museum** and the heritage streets around Parian.

## Day 7: Panglao

Take a fast ferry from Cebu City's passenger port to **Tagbilaran**. From the port, use the public bus towards Panglao or transport arranged by the accommodation. Spend the remaining afternoon around **Alona Beach**, then walk farther along the coast once the day-trip boats have returned.

## Day 8: Panglao

Use a booked shared countryside trip for the **Chocolate Hills**, a recognised tarsier sanctuary and the Loboc area. These stops are spread across Bohol and do not fit sensibly into a public-bus day from Panglao with a fixed ferry or flight afterwards. Return to Panglao for the final evening, or go directly to Tagbilaran if leaving on the last ferry.
