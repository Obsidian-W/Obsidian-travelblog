> This was the end of my Himalaya trip in July 2024. I entered India overland from Nepal and had six days for Varanasi, Sarnath, Agra and Delhi.
>
> I used buses in India, not trains. For this short end-of-trip route, buses were simpler than dealing with Indian train booking as a foreigner without a local number.

## Day 1: Varanasi

From Lumbini, take the local connection to **Bhairahawa/Siddharthanagar** and continue to the **Sonauli** border. Immigration is completed on foot: leave Nepal at Belahiya, cross the frontier and enter India at Sonauli.

On the Indian side, take a bus from Sonauli to **Gorakhpur**. Change at the [main bus stand near Gorakhpur railway station](https://gorakhpur.nic.in/tourism/) for a bus to **Varanasi**. This sequence occupied almost the entire day of my trip.

Walk to **Dashashwamedh Ghat** and follow the river south. Return through the lanes behind the ghats.

## Day 2: Varanasi

Start at **Assi Ghat** and follow the river north on foot. This passes a long succession of smaller ghats before **Harishchandra Ghat**, the palaces above the water and **Dashashwamedh Ghat**. The lanes behind the river twist through the old city.

Use the middle of the day for the lanes around **Kashi Vishwanath Temple** and the markets behind the ghats. Return to Dashashwamedh for the evening **Ganga Aarti**. Watching from the steps costs nothing; boatmen also sell places on the water.

## Day 3: Sarnath

**Sarnath** is about [10 kilometres from Varanasi](https://varanasi.nic.in/tourist-place/sarnath/). For a direct journey, arrange a return auto-rickshaw from the accommodation to the archaeological area. Visit **Chaukhandi Stupa**, **Dhamek Stupa**, the excavated monastic remains, **Mulagandha Kuti Vihar** and the **Sarnath Archaeological Museum**. The museum holds the original **Lion Capital of Ashoka**, whose four lions became India's national emblem. The excavated site and Dhamek Stupa stand close to it; Chaukhandi lies farther south on the road from Varanasi.

Return to Varanasi in the afternoon. I took the overnight bus to **Agra**.

## Day 4: Agra

Visit the **Taj Mahal** first. The mausoleum stands at the far end of the garden beside the Yamuna, with carved marble screens and inlaid floral decoration inside. It is closed to sightseeing visits on Fridays. After the mausoleum, cross the centre to **Agra Fort** and follow the palaces along the eastern wall, where several rooms look back towards the Taj Mahal.

The old city around **Jama Masjid** is beside Agra Fort, if there is time before the bus to Delhi. For the onward connection, **Kashmiri Gate ISBT** and **Anand Vihar ISBT** both have metro stations; [Delhi's transport department lists the inter-state terminals](https://delhi.gov.in/page/bus-services-0).

## Day 5: Delhi

Take the metro to **Lal Quila** or **Jama Masjid** and spend the day in Old Delhi. Visit **Jama Masjid**, then walk through the market streets towards **Chandni Chowk**, the Sikh **Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib** and the lanes around the spice market. Allow several hours for the **Red Fort** and its museums.

Finish at **Raj Ghat**, southeast of the fort, before returning by metro. A cycle-rickshaw can cover the market section between Chandni Chowk and the spice shops of **Khari Baoli**.

## Day 6: Delhi

Start at **Humayun's Tomb**, then continue west to **Lodhi Garden** and the tombs inside it. From there, take the metro or a city bus towards **India Gate** and walk along Kartavya Path towards the government buildings on Raisina Hill.

My flight left shortly after midnight, so this remained a sightseeing day. The **Airport Express** links New Delhi metro station with the airport's Terminal 3.
