## Day 1: Varanasi

![The ghats beside the Ganges in Varanasi](images/blog/india4.jpg)

From Lumbini, take the local connection to **Bhairahawa/Siddharthanagar** and continue to the **Sonauli** border. Immigration is completed on foot: leave Nepal at Belahiya, cross the frontier and enter India at Sonauli.

On the Indian side, buses from **Sonauli to Gorakhpur** take roughly **three hours**. Change at the bus stand near **Gorakhpur railway station** for Varanasi. Allow **eight to ten hours from Sonauli to Varanasi**, including the change. With the Lumbini connection and border crossing, this sequence occupied almost the entire day of my trip.

Walk to **Dashashwamedh Ghat** and follow the river south. Return through the lanes behind the ghats.

## Day 2: Varanasi

Start at **Assi Ghat** and follow the river north on foot. This passes a long succession of smaller ghats before **Harishchandra Ghat**, the palaces above the water and **Dashashwamedh Ghat**. The lanes behind the river twist through the old city.

Use the middle of the day for the lanes around **Kashi Vishwanath Temple** and the markets behind the ghats. Return to Dashashwamedh for the evening **Ganga Aarti**. Watching from the steps costs nothing; boatmen also sell places on the water.

## Day 3: Sarnath

**Sarnath** is about **10 kilometres northeast of Varanasi**. Passenger trains connect Varanasi with Sarnath station; an accommodation-arranged excursion is another option. Visit **Chaukhandi Stupa**, **Dhamek Stupa**, the excavated monastic remains, **Mulagandha Kuti Vihar** and the **Sarnath Archaeological Museum**. The museum holds the original **Lion Capital of Ashoka**, whose four lions became India's national emblem. The excavated site and Dhamek Stupa stand close to it; Chaukhandi lies farther south on the road from Varanasi.

For a guided visit with transport from Varanasi, [this Sarnath excursion takes around four hours (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/varanasi-l32154/sarnath-tour-where-buddhism-originated-t685145/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

Return to Varanasi in the afternoon, then take the overnight bus to **Agra**, around **twelve hours**. Evening departures reach Agra the following morning.

## Day 4: Agra

![The Taj Mahal](images/blog/india.jpg)

![Courtyard inside Agra Fort](images/blog/india5.jpg)

Visit the **Taj Mahal** first. The mausoleum stands at the far end of the garden beside the Yamuna, with carved marble screens and inlaid floral decoration inside. It is closed to sightseeing visits on Fridays. After the mausoleum, cross the centre to **Agra Fort** and follow the palaces along the eastern wall, where several rooms look back towards the Taj Mahal.

[A guided Taj Mahal visit can be booked here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/agra-l824/agra-taj-mahal-mausoleum-skip-the-line-tour-with-options-t521131/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

The old city around **Jama Masjid** is beside Agra Fort. Afterwards, take an intercity bus to **Delhi**, roughly **four to five hours**. NueGo and other operators sell this route, with boarding around **Idgah, Agra Fort and the expressway approaches**, depending on the service. **Kashmiri Gate ISBT** connects with the Yellow Line towards New Delhi; **Anand Vihar ISBT** with the Blue Line towards central Delhi.

## Day 5: Delhi

Take the metro to **Lal Quila** or **Jama Masjid** and spend the day in Old Delhi. Visit **Jama Masjid**, then walk through the market streets towards **Chandni Chowk**, the Sikh **Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib** and the lanes around the spice market. Allow several hours for the **Red Fort** and its museums.

Finish at **Raj Ghat**, southeast of the fort, before returning by metro. A cycle-rickshaw can cover the market section between Chandni Chowk and the spice shops of **Khari Baoli**.

## Day 6: Delhi

![Qutb Minar in Delhi](images/blog/india2.jpg)

Start in Mehrauli at **Qutb Minar**. The tower is surrounded by the carved arcades of **Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque**, the Iron Pillar and Iltutmish's tomb. Allow around two hours for the complex. The Yellow Line serves Qutab Minar station, about two kilometres from the entrance; the last stretch is a walk or local-bus ride.

Continue north for **Humayun's Tomb** and its garden enclosure. Walk through the water-channel gardens to the red sandstone mausoleum, then visit the smaller tombs inside the enclosure. Finish at **Lodhi Garden**, where paths run between the tombs of the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties. For an evening walk, **India Gate** and Kartavya Path lie north of the gardens.

## With more days

### Another day in Agra

Stay a night in Agra for **Itimad-ud-Daulah**, the marble tomb across the Yamuna, and **Mehtab Bagh**, the garden facing the Taj Mahal from the opposite bank. Its riverfront gives a different view of the mausoleum towards sunset. An extra night also opens up the Taj Mahal's first visiting hours, instead of arriving after the bus from Varanasi.

### South Delhi

Spend another morning in **Mehrauli Archaeological Park**, beside the Qutb complex, for **Jamali Kamali Mosque** and **Rajon ki Baoli**, a stepwell enclosed by arcaded galleries. Continue east to the **Lotus Temple** for its white, petal-shaped prayer hall; **Kalkaji Mandir** on the Violet and Magenta lines is the nearby metro station.

### More days around varanasi

Add **Bodh Gaya**, east of Varanasi, for the **Mahabodhi Temple** and the Bodhi Tree beside it, marking the place associated with the Buddha's enlightenment. The tall brick temple rises above a sunken courtyard surrounded by smaller shrines and stupas. The town also has Thai, Tibetan and Japanese Buddhist temples and the Great Buddha statue.

Allow **two additional days**, including the journeys. Trains from **Varanasi Junction to Gaya Junction** take around **four to five hours**; **Bodh Gaya** is about 13 kilometres beyond Gaya, roughly **0:30–0:45 by road**. Stay in Bodh Gaya for the temple and monasteries, then return to Varanasi for the westbound journey to Agra. A transfer arranged by the accommodation covers the Gaya–Bodh Gaya road connection.

### Jaipur to finish the golden triangle

Put **Jaipur between Agra and Delhi** and add **two or three days**. Buses from **Agra's Idgah terminal to Jaipur's Sindhi Camp** take around **five hours**. After Jaipur, buses from **Sindhi Camp to Delhi** take roughly **five to six hours**; direct trains also link the three cities.

Spend the first day inside the Pink City's walls: **Hawa Mahal**, the **City Palace** and **Jantar Mantar** stand close together. Hawa Mahal's latticed windows face the bazaar; the palace has royal courtyards and museum collections, while Jantar Mantar's enormous stone instruments were built for astronomical observations. Continue through **Johari Bazaar** and **Bapu Bazaar** in the afternoon.

Use the second day for **Amber Palace**, its mirror-decorated **Sheesh Mahal** and the courtyards above the lake. Buses leave Ajmeri Gate and MI Road for Amber, around 0:40 from central Jaipur. Walk up from the road to the palace. With a third day, add **Albert Hall Museum** and the royal cenotaphs at **Gaitor** before the journey to Delhi.
