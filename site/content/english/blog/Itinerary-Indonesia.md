## Day 1: Ubud

Start at **Ubud Palace** and the market, then walk south through town to the **Sacred Monkey Forest**. These three stops fit on foot and show the centre before the second day's trip into the surrounding countryside.

I hated Bali. The parts I saw were dirty and overwhelmed by overtourism, foreign-owned shops, tattoo businesses, prostitution and drug dealing. The large foreign and Russian expat scene made Ubud feel detached from the rest of Indonesia I saw afterwards.

## Day 2: Ubud

Book one shared excursion or driver for **Tegallalang's rice terraces**, **Tirta Empul** and the temple countryside north of Ubud. They are spread along roads outside town and do not form a useful walking route.

Return to Ubud and buy the westbound shuttle or bus ticket for Gilimanuk. The following day includes Bali traffic, the ferry and another road connection after reaching Java.

## Day 3: Banyuwangi

Take a westbound bus or shuttle from Ubud to **Gilimanuk**, then board the public ferry to **Ketapang**. The crossing itself is short; the road across Bali consumes most of the day.

Continue into **Banyuwangi**, visit the market and central streets, then stay near the Ijen pickup agreed with the hostel. Confirm the departure before sleeping because shared vehicles leave in the middle of the night.

## Day 4: Ijen

Use a hostel-arranged shared vehicle to the **Ijen** trailhead before dawn. Climb through the forest to the crater rim and remain on the permitted route around the viewpoint. Volcanic gas can close sections of the trail with little notice.

Return to Banyuwangi after sunrise and sleep before the next connection.

Take the train from Banyuwangi towards Probolinggo after the hike if the connection leaves enough recovery time; otherwise stay one more night and take the first train the next day.

## Day 5: Bromo

From Probolinggo, take the local minibus or the transfer arranged by the accommodation up to **Cemoro Lawang**. The minibus normally waits for passengers, so an almost-empty vehicle means either waiting or sharing the remaining seats.

Walk along the caldera edge around the village in the afternoon and locate the path for the sunrise viewpoint before dark.

## Day 6: Bromo

Leave before dawn for the Bromo sunrise viewpoint. Return towards Cemoro Lawang, cross the **Sea of Sand** and climb the staircase to the crater rim. The volcano visit and the viewpoint are separate walks even when sold as one jeep excursion.

Descend to Probolinggo and take the westbound train towards Yogyakarta. Reserve the train before reaching Bromo; the mountain minibus does not operate around a fixed railway departure.

## Day 7: Yogyakarta

Visit the **Kraton** in the morning, then walk through the lanes to **Taman Sari** and its former bathing complex. Continue through the neighbourhood workshops towards the central market and **Malioboro** after dark.

Trans Jogja buses connect the railway area, Malioboro and the roads towards the temple terminals, although the final walk varies by stop.

## Day 8: Borobudur and Prambanan

Visit **Borobudur** in the morning and **Prambanan** later in the day on a shared excursion. They stand on opposite sides of Yogyakarta, so the road between them crosses the city and occupies a large part of the schedule.

Borobudur tickets distinguish access to the grounds from timed access onto the monument. Prambanan has a wider archaeological park beyond the main group, including Candi Sewu; leave enough time to walk or use the park shuttle.

## Day 9: Temanggung

Take a bus from Yogyakarta to **Magelang**, then change for **Temanggung**. Leave the bag and walk through the centre and traditional market before heading into the tobacco-growing countryside around town.

Temanggung was not part of my original sightseeing plan, but it sits directly on the route towards Semarang and deserves two or three days for a view of ordinary Central Java beyond its major temples.

## Day 10: Temanggung

Leave early for **Posong**, where the viewpoint sits between Mount Sumbing and Mount Sindoro. Continue through the nearby mountain villages and plantations before returning to Temanggung.

Covid kept me in Temanggung far longer than planned. During that period I sorted tobacco, helped harvest rice and taught English and French. Those were part of being stuck there, not activities for this itinerary.

## Day 11: Semarang

Take the northbound bus to Semarang and leave the bag before walking around **Kota Lama**, Gereja Blenduk and the old commercial streets. Continue to **Lawang Sewu**, then take a local bus or booked ride west to **Sam Poo Kong Temple**.

## Day 12: Jakarta

Take the direct train from Semarang to Jakarta. After arrival, use the commuter line or TransJakarta to reach **Kota Tua**, walk around Fatahillah Square and continue north to **Sunda Kelapa** harbour.

## Day 13: Jakarta

Visit the **National Museum** and **Monas** around Merdeka Square, then continue towards the old civic buildings and markets south of the monument. Commuter rail and TransJakarta buses cover the long city journeys; Jakarta is too large to connect these districts comfortably on foot.

## Day 14: Jakarta

Visit **Istiqlal Mosque** and **Jakarta Cathedral**, which face each other across the same central road. Use the remaining time for the National Gallery or another central stop missed on Day 13.

Take the airport rail link to Soekarno-Hatta Airport and allow time for the terminal transfer after the train. This was the intended end of my Bali-to-Jakarta route, although Covid kept me in Central Java much longer than planned.
