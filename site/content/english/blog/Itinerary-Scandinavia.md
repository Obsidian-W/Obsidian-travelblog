## Day 1: Stockholm

![Stockholm](images/blog/sweden1.jpg)

Begin in **Gamla Stan** around Stortorget, the Royal Palace and Stockholm Cathedral. Cross towards Riddarholmen, then follow the waterfront past City Hall and return through Norrmalm. **Gamla stan metro station** is on the western side of the island. Around **Stortorget**, the coloured merchant houses face the Nobel Prize Museum; the narrow lanes continue south towards **Mårten Trotzigs gränd**.

## Day 2: Stockholm

Take the metro to **Brommaplan** and change to a bus stopping at **Drottningholms slott**. Visit **Drottningholm Palace**, then walk through the formal gardens towards the theatre and the **Chinese Pavilion**.

Back in Stockholm, take the metro to **Slussen** and walk up to **Monteliusvägen** in Södermalm. The path overlooks City Hall, Riddarholmen and the water between the islands.

## Day 3: Stockholm

Take the tram or ferry to **Djurgården**. Visit the **Vasa Museum** first. The seventeenth-century warship fills the main hall, with galleries on several levels for its carvings, construction and recovery from the harbour.

The **Nordic Museum** is next door, with exhibitions about everyday life and traditions in Sweden. Farther along Djurgården, **Skansen** has relocated farmhouses, workshops and town buildings. [Skansen admission is available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/skansen-l3721/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Day 4: Uppsala

![Uppsala](images/blog/sweden2.jpg)

Take a regional train from **Stockholm Central to Uppsala Central**, around **0:40**. Walk from the station to **Uppsala Cathedral**, then cross to **Gustavianum** and the university buildings. Continue towards the castle hill and botanical garden. **Gamla Uppsala**, with its royal burial mounds and museum, is outside the centre and needs a separate local-bus journey. Return to **Uppsala Central** for the train to **Örebro Central**, around **2:40** on a direct Mälartåg service via Sala and Västerås. Stay in Örebro for the night.

## Day 5: Orebro

Walk around **Örebro Castle**, built on an island in the **Svartån**. Follow the river through **Stadsparken** to **Wadköping**, where old wooden houses, courtyards and workshops have been preserved as an open-air museum. Afterwards, take the direct train from **Örebro Central to Göteborg Central**, around **three hours**.

## Day 6: Gothenburg

![Gothenburg](images/blog/sweden3.jpg)

Walk from the central station along the canals to the city museum and the old streets around Kronhuset. Continue through Haga and climb Skansen Kronan, then return by tram through the centre. The climb to **Skansen Kronan** starts above Haga. The stone tower stands on a hill overlooking the old wooden houses, roofs and harbour.

## Day 7: Gothenburg archipelago

Take Tram 11 to **Saltholmen** and use the public ferries into the southern archipelago. **Styrsö and Donsö** are connected by a pedestrian bridge. Walk between the small harbours and wooden houses, then follow one of the coastal paths before returning to Saltholmen by ferry. The same local transport ticket covers the tram and the regular passenger boats.

## Day 8: Malmo

Take the direct train from **Göteborg Central to Malmö Central**, around **2:30–3 hours**. Walk from the station through Stortorget and Lilla Torg to Malmöhus Castle, then continue through the parks and along the waterfront towards the Turning Torso. Cross the Öresund by train to **København H** in the evening, roughly **0:40**.

## Day 9: Copenhagen

![Rosenborg Castle in Copenhagen](images/blog/denmark1.jpg)

Start at **Rosenborg Castle** for the royal rooms and treasury, including the crown jewels. Walk through the **King's Garden** to **Frederik's Church** and **Amalienborg**, whose four palaces face an octagonal square. Continue along the harbour to Nyhavn and cross the inner harbour for the evening around Christianshavn.

## Day 10: Copenhagen

![Central Copenhagen](images/blog/denmark2.jpg)
![Copenhagen from above](images/blog/denmark3.jpg)

Visit **Christiansborg Palace** on **Slotsholmen**. The royal reception rooms include the **Great Hall**, with tapestries depicting Danish history; beneath the palace are the ruins of earlier buildings. Visit the palace chapel and royal stables, then walk along the canals past the old stock-exchange site and continue to **City Hall Square**.

[This one-hour canal cruise leaves from Ved Stranden (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/copenhagen-l12/copenhagen-canal-boat-tour-from-gammel-strand-t37848/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher), beside Slotsholmen, and passes the harbour palaces and Christianshavn.

## With more days

### A second archipelago day in Gothenburg

Take the ferry from **Saltholmen** to **Brännö** for another day among the southern islands. Walk from the village lanes and wooden houses to the footbridge onto **Galterö**, whose paths cross open grazing land and rocky shoreline. Return to Brännö for the boat to Gothenburg.

### Another Copenhagen night

Spend a morning at the **National Museum**, with time for its prehistoric and Viking collections, then cross to **Christianshavn**. Climb the spiral spire of **Our Saviour's Church** for views over the harbour and follow the canals towards the waterfront. **Tivoli**, beside the central station, is another evening option during its opening seasons.
This is actually well needed.

### More time in Denmark

**Roskilde** makes a full day by train from **København H**, around **0:25**. Walk from the station to **Roskilde Cathedral**, the burial church of Denmark's monarchs, then down towards the fjord for the **Viking Ship Museum**. Its five excavated ships are displayed indoors, while the harbour workshops show traditional boatbuilding.

For a second day, take the coastal train from **Nørreport to Helsingør**, roughly **an hour**. Visit **Kronborg Castle**, including its royal rooms and casemates, then walk through the old town. The **M/S Maritime Museum** occupies the former dry dock beside the castle.

Another castle outing is **Frederiksborg in Hillerød**, with its palace rooms, chapel and formal gardens around the lake. Take an S-train from Copenhagen to **Hillerød**, then walk through the town centre to the castle.
