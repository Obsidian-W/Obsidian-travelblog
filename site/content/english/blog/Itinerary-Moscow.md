I visited Moscow during a 72-hour layover, using the Russian transit visa available for that stop at the time. This is the three-day route I fitted between the two flights.

That visa information belongs to my 2020 trip, not to a current application guide. Entry rules have changed since then.

> I also visited someone there, so my days were only half days.

# Itinerary done quick

## Moscow

| Day | Destination | Notes |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| Day 1 | [Red Square](#day-1-red-square) | Kremlin, GUM and Red Square after dark |
| Day 2 | [Central Moscow](#day-2-central-moscow) | Metro stations, Seven Sisters and riverfront |
| Day 3 | [Kolomenskoye](#day-3-kolomenskoye) | Wooden palace, estate and winter park |

## Day 1: Red Square

![The State Historical Museum beside Red Square](images/blog/moscow2.jpg)

Take the metro to **Okhotny Ryad** and enter Red Square beside the State Historical Museum. Walk the square clockwise past Lenin's Mausoleum, the Kremlin wall, St Basil's Cathedral and GUM.

The visitor entrance to the Kremlin museums is in **Alexander Garden**, on the opposite side of the wall from Red Square. Cathedral Square and the Armoury use timed tickets, so they have to be booked before fixing the rest of the day. Leave the Kremlin through Alexander Garden, cross Manezhnaya Square and enter Kitay-gorod along Nikolskaya Street.

Go inside **GUM**, even without any intention of shopping. The galleries are part of the visit, and the ice-cream kiosks are one of the cheaper things in the building. Return to Red Square after dark. The illuminated GUM façade and St Basil's are worth seeing twice.

## Day 2: Central Moscow

![Winter lights in Moscow](images/blog/moscow3.jpg)

Use the Koltsevaya Line for the metro interiors. A compact sequence is **Komsomolskaya**, **Novoslobodskaya**, **Kievskaya** and **Park Kultury**; the trains run in a loop, so there is no backtracking between them.

Several of the **Seven Sisters** sit near the same route: Hotel Leningradskaya is visible from Komsomolskaya, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs stands beside Smolenskaya, and Hotel Ukraina is across the river from Kievskaya.

Walk Old Arbat, then continue to the **Cathedral of Christ the Saviour** and the river embankment. For cheap food, I used the restaurant floors in shopping centres and the Central Asian kebab shops found across the city; both were far cheaper than eating beside Red Square.

## Day 3: Kolomenskoye

![Kolomenskoye in winter](images/blog/moscow1.jpg)

Take Metro Line 2 to **Kashirskaya** for the reconstructed wooden **Palace of Tsar Alexei Mikhailovich**. The palace stands at the southern end of Kolomenskoye, not beside Kolomenskaya station.

After the palace, walk north through the estate to the Church of the Ascension, the wooden buildings and the paths above the Moscow River. Exit at **Kolomenskaya** station instead of retracing the whole park. The route across the estate is around five kilometres. The park was particularly good under snow.


Winter light installations fill several central squares after sunset.

With a fourth day, take the suburban train from Rizhsky station towards Istra for the **New Jerusalem Monastery**. It is too far outside Moscow to squeeze into any of these three half-days.
