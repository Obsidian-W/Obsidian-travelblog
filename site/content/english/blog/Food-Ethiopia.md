## Injera and how meals work

![Injera in Ethiopia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Eating%20Injera%2C%20Ethiopia%20%2814683490675%29.jpg)

Most Ethiopian meals are built around **injera**, the sour flatbread that works as plate, starch and utensil. Stews, vegetables and meat are served on top or beside it. Pieces of injera are used to pick up the stews.

Injera is traditionally made with teff, though some recipes mix in wheat or barley. Those mixed versions contain gluten.
Honestly, I found Injera pretty disgusting, I can't stand sour tastes. So I suffered for my three weeks and a half I had in Ethiopia.

Hand-feeding someone a bite of injera is called **gursha**, a gesture of affection or hospitality. [Gursha restaurant in Addis explains the custom](https://www.gursha-restaurant.com/).

## Core stews and fasting plates

**Beyaynetu** is a mixed fasting plate. It usually brings several meat-free dishes on one injera: lentils, split peas, greens, potatoes, cabbage, beetroot or shiro.

| Food | What it is | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shiro | Chickpea or broad-bean stew, often smooth and spiced | Pretty simple and tasty, served along Injera|
| Misir wat | Red lentil stew with berbere | Nicely spiced, filled with fiber and proteins  |
| Kik alicha | Mild yellow split-pea stew | I don't remember that one |
| Gomen | Cooked greens, sometimes with onion and garlic | It's a popular side dish with Injera |
| Atkilt wat | Cabbage, potato and carrot stew | I don't remember that one either |

I usually ate the small stews served on top of the injera without keeping track of every name.

## Meat dishes

**Doro wat** is chicken stew with berbere, usually served with egg and injera.

| Food | What it is | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doro wat | Chicken stew with berbere and egg | Found everywhere, makes a good lunch|
| Tibs | Fried or sauteed meat, often beef or lamb | Very common for lunch or dinner |
| Kitfo | Minced beef with spice and clarified butter | Raw, lightly cooked or fully cooked |
| Key wat | Spicy red meat stew | Couldn't find it |
| Alicha wat | Milder meat stew | Couldn't find it |

Kitfo can be served raw, lightly cooked (**leb leb**) or fully cooked.

## Breakfast and snacks

**Firfir** is torn injera mixed with sauce, often spicy and filling. **Chechebsa** or **kita firfir** is torn flatbread mixed with spiced butter or sauce. **Genfo** is a thick porridge, usually eaten with spiced butter in the centre. **Ful** is common in cities and offers a breakfast without injera.

IMHO, breakfasts are the best part of Ethiopia's food culture, mostly because you do without the Injera.

For snacks, look for **sambusa**, fresh bread, simple cakes, fruit juice and tea. **Spris** is a layered fruit juice, often avocado, mango and papaya.

## Coffee and drinks

![Ethiopian coffee ceremony](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Ethiopian%20coffee%20ceremony%20-%20Addis%20Ababa.jpg)

A traditional ceremony takes time: roasting, grinding, brewing in a jebena and serving small cups. You can get coffee pretty much everywhere, even the side of the road

Tea is common. Fresh juices are easy in Addis and larger towns.

## Dietary notes

**Yetsom** means fasting food, which excludes meat and dairy under Orthodox fasting traditions. **Niter kibbeh** is spiced clarified butter, used in many other dishes.

**Alicha** stews are generally milder than those made with berbere.
