## Day 1: San Pedro de Atacama

The four-day Uyuni tour I took ended at the Bolivian border, where I got into a shared taxi to **San Pedro de Atacama**.

> Fun story, right away: I fell in the water near the border, while waiting for the transfer.
> The water was warm (hot springs), the air not. My clothes froze, obviously, they were wet and in -10°C temperatures. Then, I got sick almost instantly. And forgot my phone.
> I had to bribe the driver for him to drive back 5 whole minutes (maybe less, actually).
> What a mess.

Leave the luggage at the accommodation and walk around the adobe centre, **San Pedro Church** and the archaeological museum.

The transfer is quite long, so it won't leave time for more.

I booked an excursion for Valle de la Luna for D+2, right near my accomodation.

My accomodation was Casa Voyage Hostel, it still exists and my review on google maps can stil be found!

> Fun fact, I met a girl for the third time there, I met her the first time in Peru, possibly Arequipa, then met her a second time in Cusco, Peru. So the rule applied.

## Day 5: Punta Arenas

I flew from **Santiago to Punta Arenas**, around 3 hours 30 minutes in the air.
I transferred right away (I don't remember how, dropped my stuff) and spent the rest day walking.

Punta Arenas feels like the end of the world. It's windy, colourful, isolated and stretched along the Strait of Magellan.

I walked all over the city: **Plaza Muñoz Gamero**, the port, the waterfront sculptures and **Cerro de la Cruz** for the view over the rooftops and the strait.

The city isn't packed with must-sees, but the atmosphere does most of the work. It's really otherwordly in such an eerie way...
My favourite part was the signpost place, where you had signs to all major destinations and distance, that's when you understand that you're in bumfuckistan.

## Day 6: Puerto Natales

![New SF Char leaked](images/blog/chile3.jpg)

^Found this guy near the center, felt like a new Street Fighter Character.

In winter 2022, there was **one bus a day** from Punta Arenas to Puerto Natales. It left from the bus terminal in Punta Arenas and arrived at **Rodoviario Puerto Natales** around three to four hours later.

The road was really beautiful: empty land, distant mountains and that very Patagonian feeling that everything is much farther away than it looks.

My first stop was the **Museo Histórico Municipal**. From the museum, I walked north along the waterfront through **Monumento al Viento**, the newer waterfront and **Muelle Fiscal**, then **Muelle Histórico** and the ugly bronze Milodón sculpture near the northern entrance to town.

Afterwards, I booked the next two days with a local agency: one photography tour by car and one full-day hike to Base Torres. I got a good price because I booked both at once. The winter transfers to Torres del Paine were included, so Puerto Natales remained my base.

## Day 7: Torres del Paine

![Torres del Paine in winter](images/blog/patagonia3.jpg)
*Not a bad place for a photography tour.*

This was called a photography tour, but it was essentially a full-day road trip through **Torres del Paine National Park** in a small group.

We first went to **Cueva del Milodón**, then drove through the park and stopped at the nicest viewpoints: mountains, lakes, waterfalls and snow everywhere. It filled the whole day easily. The distances are huge, so most of the tour was short walks and photo stops between long bits in the car.

I didn't mind. The roads and the views from the car were part of the trip.

I booked locally. > Cueva del Milodón is around 25 kilometres north of Puerto Natales. The cave is interesting. The giant sculpture in Puerto Natales is still ugly.

## Day 8: Base Torres

![Base Torres covered in snow](images/blog/patagonia2.jpg)
*Base Torres in winter.*

The second tour was a full-day hike to **Base Torres**, booked with the same agency.

We left Puerto Natales early and started near **Hotel Las Torres**. The return hike is roughly **20 kilometres** and took most of the day. There weren't many people insane enough to do it in that weather, so our group was small.

The trail was covered in snow and it was really cold, especially whenever we stopped. The final climb was the slowest part, but the frozen lake and the towers above it were fucking beautiful.

For winter 2026, the park requires an accredited guide, microspikes, trekking poles, a headlamp and proper winter clothing for Base Torres. The rules change with trail conditions, so the [official Torres del Paine website](https://parquetorresdelpaine.cl/) is the place to check.

[This guided Base Torres day hike from Puerto Natales is the GetYourGuide alternative (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/puerto-natales-l32145/puerto-natales-base-of-the-towers-day-hike-t419260/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). I didn't use that operator. Winter dates and included equipment vary.

Cold, tiring, 100% worth it.

## Day 9: El Calafate

I took the morning international bus from **Rodoviario Puerto Natales** to the **Terminal de Ómnibus de El Calafate**, crossing into Argentina at **Cerro Castillo/Cancha Carrera**.

The bus is scheduled for around five hours. With immigration and winter conditions, five to seven hours is more realistic. Mine ran once a day, lasted 7 hours. There you go.

After arriving, I walked around central **El Calafate**, mainly along **Avenida del Libertador** and towards **Laguna Nimez**. 

It's a tourist base more than an exciting city, (So... barely anything to do but chill)
The best thing I did was walk in the historic center then along the Laguna Nimez.

> Actually, Lago Argentino was quite nice. Despite the freaking cold weather.

## Day 10: El Chalten

![El Chalten](images/blog/patagonia4.jpg)

^ See my picture of El Chalten, I had a shitty weather indeed

I booked a full-day trip from El Calafate to **El Chaltén**.

El Chaltén is around **220 kilometres from El Calafate**, close to three hours each way. Most of the day was spent driving, although the road was beautiful and we still had time to walk around town and hike nearby.

It was cold, rainy and the mountains spent a lot of time hiding behind clouds. The weather kinda ruined the trip, but I have no regrets. El Chaltén was still beautiful.

[This El Chaltén and Mount Fitz Roy day trip from El Calafate is a similar GetYourGuide option (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/el-calafate-l544/from-el-calafate-full-day-tour-to-el-chalten-t239467/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).
It isn't the operator I used.
Winter availability and walking time depend on the departure. It's always harder in the cold season (June to August, btw)

With more time, I would stay in El Chaltén for more days (At least one more).
Six hours in a car for a few hours of hiking
wasn't the best ratio...

## Day 11: Perito Moreno

![Perito Moreno Glacier](images/blog/patagonia.jpg)
*Perito Moreno, plus a bird that understood framing better than I did.*

Perito Moreno was much easier. Transport leaves from El Calafate, follows **Ruta Provincial 11** and reaches the glacier in around 1 hour 30 minutes. It's roughly 80 kilometres from town.

Once there, I spent the day walking between the viewpoints facing the ice. The paths are easy and there are enough angles to keep stopping every so often for pictures.

The [official Los Glaciares National Park page](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/parquesnacionales/patagonia-austral/parque-nacional-los-glaciares/horarios-como-llegar-0) lists different winter and summer access hours.

[This Perito Moreno trip from El Calafate includes the walkways and an optional boat ride (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/el-calafate-l544/el-calafate-perito-moreno-glacier-optional-boat-trip-t231537/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher)

I used local transportation, not that operator. The national-park entrance may be separate.

Perito Moreno was one of the highlights of Patagonia. In winter it was quiet, bright blue and surrounded by snow.
(Also pay attention to how noisy is the Glacier!!)

> Bonus: Lot of wildlife to be seen!

## Day 12: Punta Arenas

This was a complete transport day, just two buses back to back:

1. **El Calafate to Puerto Natales** in the morning, around five to seven hours including the border.
2. **Puerto Natales to Punta Arenas** in the afternoon, around three to four hours.

Both used the main bus terminals. I had enough time between them in Puerto Natales, but the border can make the first bus late. I flew out of Punta Arenas early the following morning, so... No other choice.

> It took me 12 hours including change between bus. I came back in the evening with barely time to do any activity.

From El Calafate, continuing through Argentina makes more sense. I had to return to Chile, so I couldn't do that.
(Flight from Santiago, hence why in the early parts of this itinerary)

## Day 13: Santiago

![Santiago](images/blog/chile2.jpg)

> I actually didn't get time to visit much, I walked the center, walked up a hill with a garden and that's it

Here's what one could do (it's AI, sorry):
Start at **La Moneda** and walk through the civic centre to the **Museum of Pre-Columbian Art**. Continue through Plaza de Armas and the **Central Market**, then cross the Mapocho towards **Bellavista**.

Take the funicular or walk up **Cerro San Cristóbal** for the view across Santiago. Return through Bellavista and **Lastarria**, with **Cerro Santa Lucía** as the final central stop if it is still open. The metro handles the long jump between the accommodation, centre and airport connection; the historical core itself is better covered on foot.
