I reached Busan by ferry from Fukuoka at the end of my [15-day Japan itinerary](/blog/itinerary-japan/). The ferry arrives at **Busan Port International Passenger Terminal**, about ten minutes on foot from Busan Station. The JR Beetle service no longer operates. [New Camellia](https://www.camellia-line.co.jp/newcamellia/) normally leaves Hakata at 12:30 and reaches Busan at 18:30, with occasional overnight departures listed in its [booking calendar](https://www.camellia-line.co.jp/rs/). Arrive the evening before Day 1 for a full first day in Busan.

{{< worth-o-meter score="7" >}}
Korea is not the most amazing country, far from that.
The rough culture, the hyper focus on fakeness and appearance, the idolization of celebrities, the too-fast-paced life... A few days is enough to feel it, which drops the score, the culture shock is real in a way.
However, the gentle rolling hills, the pretty mountains in the north, the amazingly located temples and the delicious food makes up for it, South korea rewards travellers with an experience that is delightfully unique.
It suffers from the comparison to countries such as Japan and Taiwan, but even as a standalone, SK is a trend to be witnessed.

## Day 1: Busan

Take Metro Line 1 from Busan Station to **Jagalchi Market**. The fish stalls extend along the waterfront as well as inside the market building. Cross BIFF Square into **Gukje Market** for its clothing, household-goods and food alleys, then follow Gwangbok-ro to the escalators for **Yongdusan Park** and Busan Tower.

## Day 2: Busan

![Gamcheon Culture Village](images/blog/korea1.jpg)

Take Metro Line 1 to **Toseong**, Exit 6, then village bus Saha 1-1, Seo-gu 2 or Seo-gu 2-2 to Gamjeong Elementary School. The entrance to **Gamcheon Culture Village** is five minutes from the stop.

Gamcheon's painted houses rise in terraces above the port. Follow the murals, stairways and viewpoints through the neighbourhood, including the **Little Prince sculpture** and the lanes overlooking the harbour.

Continue to Songdo by bus for the beach and coastal walk, then return across the harbour for the evening.

## Day 3: Busan

![Haedong Yonggungsa](images/blog/korea2.jpg)

Take Metro Line 2 to **Haeundae**, Exit 7, then bus 100, 181 or Haeundae 9 to **Haedong Yonggungsa**. The temple entrance is about ten minutes from the bus stop.

The approach to **Haedong Yonggungsa** passes zodiac statues before descending to the temple buildings on the rocks above the sea. Cross the little bridge for the view back towards the main hall.

Return by bus to Haeundae. Walk the beach from the old station side to Dongbaek Island, circle the coastal path past Nurimaru and exit near Dongbaek metro station.

## Day 4: Gyeongju

Take Metro Line 1 to **Nopo** for **Busan Central Bus Terminal**. Intercity buses reach **Gyeongju's central bus terminal** in around **0:50–1:10**. Walk east from the terminal to **Daereungwon**, where grass-covered royal burial mounds stand among the trees. **Cheonmachong** is open inside, with a reconstructed burial chamber and displays of the objects found there.

Continue to the stone astronomical tower at **Cheomseongdae**, then through Gyochon Village to **Woljeonggyo Bridge**. Finish at **Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond** after dark, when the reconstructed palace pavilions are reflected in the water.

## Day 5: Gyeongju

![Bulguksa](images/blog/korea3.jpg)

**Buses 10 and 11** link Gyeongju's terminals and centre with **Bulguksa**. From the Bulguksa car park, **bus 12** climbs to **Seokguram**.

At Bulguksa, the route climbs past Cheongungyo and Baegungyo bridges to Daeungjeon, Museoljeon and the upper halls. The courtyard contains the contrasting stone pagodas Dabotap and Seokgatap. At **Seokguram**, a seated stone Buddha faces the entrance from the circular chamber; visitors view it through glass rather than walking around the statue.

## Day 6: Gyeongju

![A traditional village near Gyeongju](images/blog/korea4.jpg)

Visit the **Gyeongju National Museum**, including the Silla gold crowns and the Divine Bell of King Seongdeok, then travel to **Yangdong Village** for the afternoon.

Take **bus 203 from Gyeongju's intercity terminal to Yangdong Folk Village**, about 20 kilometres north of the centre. The houses spread across several hills, with **Hyangdan** and **Mucheomdang** among the principal buildings. The same bus returns to Gyeongju.

## Day 7: Seoul

Take a local bus to Gyeongju railway station and the KTX to **Seoul**. Visit Gyeongbokgung Palace, walk through Bukchon Hanok Village and continue to Insadong and Jogyesa.

The **KTX journey takes about two hours**. Gyeongju's railway station is outside the historic centre, southwest of town.

At **Gyeongbokgung**, walk through the main throne-hall courtyards to the Gyeonghoeru pavilion. Bukchon's hanok streets lie east of the palace; continue south for Insadong's craft shops and the main Buddhist hall at **Jogyesa**.

[Guided Gyeongbokgung visits are available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/gyeongbokgung-l89492/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Day 8: Seoul

Walk through Gwangjang Market and along Cheonggyecheon, then take the metro to Myeongdong. Finish at **Namsan Seoul Tower**, reached by the hillside bus, cable car or walking path. Take the AREX train from Seoul Station or Hongik University to Incheon Airport.

Gwangjang's covered food alleys are known for mung-bean pancakes and small seaweed rice rolls. The path beside **Cheonggyecheon** runs below street level towards the centre. From Myeongdong, the walk up **Namsan** climbs through wooded paths to the tower and its city views.

## With more days

### Two more days in Seoul

Spend a day at **Changdeokgung**, including the **Secret Garden** on a reserved visit, then continue through **Ikseon-dong** to **Jongmyo**, the Confucian shrine of the Joseon kings.

Use another day for the **National Museum of Korea**, reached from **Ichon station**, with its Buddhist sculpture, ceramics and royal objects. Continue west to **Yeouido** for a walk along the Han River and, in spring, its cherry blossom.

### Another Busan night

Visit **Beomeosa**, in the wooded hills north of Busan. The temple's gates and prayer halls sit below **Geumjeongsan**, with trails continuing towards the old mountain fortress. Take Metro Line 1 to **Beomeosa station**, leave through Exit 5 or 7 and take **bus 90** up to the temple.

### Jeju

Add **three or four days** for Jeju, flying from Busan before Gyeongju or from Seoul at the end of the route. Use **Jeju City** for the northern and eastern coast, or **Seogwipo** for the southern waterfalls and coast paths.

On the east side, climb **Seongsan Ilchulbong**, the volcanic crater rising directly above the sea, then follow the coast around **Gwangchigi Beach**. Bus 201 links Jeju City with Seongsan, although the journey takes much of two hours each way. From Seogwipo, another day can link the harbour, **Jeongbang Waterfall** and **Cheonjiyeon Waterfall** on foot and by local bus.

Keep a full day for **Hallasan** if hiking. The **Seongpanak** and **Gwaneumsa** trails reach the summit crater; the shorter **Yeongsil** route climbs towards the **Witse Oreum** plateau. The summit trails require an advance reservation.

### DMZ

Add a day from Seoul for **Imjingak**, the **Third Infiltration Tunnel** and **Dora Observatory**, looking across the border towards North Korea. The tunnel visit descends underground into one of the passages discovered beneath the frontier; the observatory gives a very different view of the divided peninsula above ground.

The **DMZ Peace Tour** leaves from **Imjingak** and takes about **three hours**, plus the journeys from Seoul. Book ahead and bring the passport used for the reservation. [This DMZ coach tour from Seoul includes the tunnel and observatory (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/seoul-l197/seoul-dmz-the-3rd-tunnel-and-suspension-bridge-tour-t63309/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). The tunnel and observatory close on Mondays, and military restrictions can interrupt visits. **Panmunjom/JSA is a separate visit** from this route.
