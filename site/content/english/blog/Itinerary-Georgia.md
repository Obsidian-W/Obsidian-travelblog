## Day 1 Sighnaghi

In 2019 I took a bus from Sheki to the Azerbaijani border, then found a bus going through Sighnaghi and onward to Tbilisi. I simply got off where I wanted.

Walk the old town's cobbled streets and carved wooden balconies, then follow the accessible section of the **Sighnaghi Fortress wall**. From the towers you can see across the Alazani Valley to the Caucasus. Add **Bodbe Monastery** if your arrival leaves enough time; otherwise stay in town for the walls and viewpoints. I also heard that the Sighnaghi Museum is nice, although I didn't go.

Stay overnight or take a marshrutka to Tbilisi, a journey of about two and a half hours.

---

## Day 2 Tbilisi old city

Start at **Freedom Square** and walk into the old city through its steep lanes and wooden-balcony houses. Continue to the **Abanotubani sulphur-bath district**, where brick domes sit beside the small gorge and waterfall.

Climb to **Narikala Fortress** and the **Mother of Georgia** statue for the view over the roofs and river. Walk back down through the Botanical Garden side or towards **Metekhi Church**, then cross the river and finish around **Rike Park** and the **Bridge of Peace** after dark.

---

## Day 3 More Tbilisi

Use the second city day for the parts beyond the old town. Walk along **Rustaveli Avenue**, stopping at Parliament and whichever museum interests you. Cross to the left bank for **Fabrika** and its surrounding streets, then continue to the huge **Holy Trinity Cathedral**.

I stopped there because I had a date. If you don't, take Metro Line 1 to Ghrmaghele, then bus 360 from David Guramishvili Avenue. Get off at Cadet Corps and walk uphill for around 10 to 15 minutes to the **Chronicle of Georgia**. Check the current route locally before leaving.

---

## Day 4 Gori and Uplistsikhe

Visit Gori for its fortress and the Stalin Museum, built around Stalin's house, with an important side trip to the cave city of **Uplistsikhe**.

Marshrutkas leave from Didube Bus Station and take around two hours to reach Gori. Uplistsikhe requires an additional local bus; the return to Tbilisi can be harder. I hitchhiked back, so it really was a full-day trip.

## Day 6 Mtskheta

Visit Georgia's ancient capital, **Svetitskhoveli Cathedral** and the confluence of the two rivers, my personal highlight.

Take a Mtskheta-bound marshrutka from Didube. It takes around 45 minutes and drops you near the centre. This is not a full-day trip, leaving time for more Tbilisi or an onward connection. If you continue to Armenia that day, start around 7:00; otherwise stay another night in Tbilisi.

For a guided alternative, [this half-day Mtskheta tour visits Jvari and the Chronicle of Georgia (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/tbilisi-l1379/tbilisi-explore-mtskheta-the-ancient-capital-of-georgia-t165599/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). I went independently.

## More days? Planned vs Done

### Svaneti: my original plan

I originally planned to add **Svaneti**, but I got fewer paid-leave days than expected and had to shorten the whole Caucasus trip. With enough time, take a night bus from Tbilisi to Mestia and another night bus back. Give Svaneti at least three full days between those journeys: one to discover Mestia and two for hikes or nearby villages. In practice, add **three days plus two overnight journeys**; more is even better if you want a longer trek.

### Batumi: an alternative extension

**Batumi** is a common Georgian destination. My Chinese friends spent three days there and liked it. I did not include it in the core itinerary because I didn't go myself, but it works well as a separate two- or three-day extension.

You could walk Batumi Boulevard and the seafront, explore the old town and Europe Square, visit the Botanical Garden, see the Ali and Nino sculpture after dark, and use a final day for the Gonio Fortress or an excursion into the Adjara countryside. Choose Batumi for the Black Sea and city atmosphere; choose Svaneti for mountains and hiking.
