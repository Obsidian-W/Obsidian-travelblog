## Day 1: Hanoi

Start around **Hoan Kiem Lake** and cross the red bridge to **Ngoc Son Temple**. Walk north through the **Old Quarter**, using the old merchant streets to reach Đồng Xuân Market, then return past St Joseph's Cathedral.

In the afternoon, cross into the French Quarter for the Opera House and the streets around the National Museum of Vietnamese History. Return to the Old Quarter after dark for the food streets and, at weekends, the night market.

## Day 2: Hanoi

Visit the **Temple of Literature**, then continue to the **Ho Chi Minh complex**, One Pillar Pagoda and the grounds around the mausoleum. In the afternoon, choose either the Museum of Ethnology or the Vietnamese Women's Museum; they are in different parts of Hanoi.

I stayed at **Old Quarter View Hanoi Hostel** and booked the Ha Giang Loop, Ha Long Bay and Ninh Binh trips there.

Take the hostel-booked night bus from **Hanoi to Ha Giang**, roughly **six to seven hours**.

## Day 3: Ha Giang

My Ha Giang Loop was a motorcycle tour **with a driver**, booked through the hostel in Hanoi. I did not ride the mountain roads myself.

Meet the group in **Ha Giang** and head north over **Bắc Sum Pass** to **Quản Bạ**. Stop at **Heaven's Gate** for the view over Tam Sơn and the rounded Twin Mountains, then continue through the valleys and pine-covered slopes towards **Yên Minh** for the night.

The operator supplied the motorcycle, driver and accommodation. [This four-day Ha Giang trip also offers a driver option (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/ha-giang-l149376/hanoi-ha-giang-loop-4-days-tour-with-extra-night-pickup-t1053879/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Day 4: Ha Giang Loop

Continue from **Yên Minh** over **Thẩm Mã Pass** into the **Đồng Văn Karst Plateau**. The road winds between limestone peaks and villages built among the rocks. Visit the **Vương family palace at Sà Phìn**, with its stone walls and timber courtyards, then continue to **Đồng Văn** for its old market square and stone-and-timber houses.

## Day 5: Dong Van

Leave **Đồng Văn** for **Mã Pí Lèng Pass**, where the road follows the cliffs above the **Nho Quế River**. A boat outing on the river enters **Tu Sản Canyon**, beneath the high limestone walls seen from the pass. Continue through **Mèo Vạc** and the southern valleys towards **Du Già** for the night.

## Day 6: Ha Giang

Visit **Du Già waterfall**, then return to **Ha Giang** through the villages and terraced valleys of the southern loop. Take the hostel-booked night bus back to **Hanoi**, around **six to seven hours**.

## Day 7: Ha Long Bay

The hostel-booked trip included the road transfer from Hanoi and the cruise. The cruise passes between steep limestone islands, with caves and small beaches at the foot of the cliffs. The overnight boats continue beyond the harbour area for their afternoon stops.

The drive from **Hanoi to the Ha Long cruise ports** takes around **2:30–3:30**. Allow **three days and two nights aboard** for this bay visit. [Multi-day Ha Long cruises are listed here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/ha-long-l119790/multi-day-trips-tc282/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Day 8: Ha Long Bay

Spend the second day out on the bay. Ha Long cruises commonly include **Sửng Sốt Cave**, with its large chambers, and **Ti Tốp Island**, where a staircase climbs from the beach to a viewpoint above the islands. Kayaking routes vary by cruise.

## Day 9: Hanoi

Finish the final cruise activity and return to the harbour, then use the road transfer included with the booking back to Hanoi. Spend the remaining evening around **Trúc Bạch Lake**, the Old Quarter or any museum missed during the first two days.

## Day 10: Ninh Binh

**Hanoi to Ninh Binh/Tam Cốc** takes around **two to three hours** by shared minibus. The train reaches **Ninh Binh station**, followed by a local transfer to Tam Cốc.

The Ninh Binh trip was also booked through **Old Quarter View Hanoi Hostel**. The road transfer left Hanoi for the former capital at **Hoa Lư**, followed by the boat route through the limestone landscape at **Tràng An**.

At **Hoa Lư**, the remaining royal temples stand beneath limestone hills. At **Tràng An**, small rowing boats pass between flooded valleys through low caves. Stay around Tam Cốc or Ninh Binh for the following day.

## Day 11: Ninh Binh

Climb the long staircase at **Múa Cave** for the viewpoints over the karst fields, then visit **Bích Động Pagoda** and cycle through the roads around Tam Cốc.

Take a sleeper bus south to **Huế**, or the overnight train from **Ninh Binh Station**, around **eleven to thirteen hours**. Huế station is south of the **Perfume River**; the Imperial City is across the river to the north.

## Day 12: Hue

Give the **Imperial City** most of the morning and early afternoon, walking through the main gates, ceremonial courtyards and surviving palace compounds. Continue to **Đông Ba Market** and follow the Perfume River back towards the centre.

## Day 13: Hue

Use a shared minibus tour for the imperial tombs of **Minh Mạng** and **Khải Định**, which lie in different directions outside central Huế. Finish at **Thiên Mụ Pagoda** beside the Perfume River; some tours return to the centre by boat.

## Day 14: An Bang

Arrange a return excursion from Huế through the accommodation to **An Bàng village** on the coast. Its cemetery contains enormous family tombs decorated with painted dragons, tiled roofs and elaborate gateways. Continue to the beach and the nearby lagoon before returning to Huế.

This is An Bàng near Huế, not the beach of the same name beside Hội An.

## Day 15: Da Nang

Take the daylight train from **Huế to Đà Nẵng**, around **three hours**, across the **Hải Vân Pass** and sit on the left side leaving Huế for the coastal view. From Đà Nẵng station, visit the **Museum of Cham Sculpture**, Hàn Market and the riverfront around Dragon Bridge. Continue to **Mỹ Khê Beach** late in the day.

## Day 16: Da Nang

Take the local bus south to the **Marble Mountains** and climb through the caves, pagodas and viewpoints on Thủy Sơn. Return through Đà Nẵng, then take an accommodation-arranged transfer to **Linh Ứng Pagoda** on Sơn Trà Peninsula. Its large white Lady Buddha statue faces the bay; the temple courtyards overlook the coast.

## Day 17: Hoi An

For public transport from Đà Nẵng, take **bus 02 to Vietnam–Korea University**, then change to **LK02** towards Hội An and get off around Lý Thường Kiệt for the old town. Allow around **1:30–2 hours** for the two local buses; direct shuttles take around **an hour**. Walk through the **Ancient Town**, linking the old houses, Chinese assembly halls, market and **Japanese Covered Bridge** with the same old-town ticket. Return to the river after dark when the shopfronts and lanterns are lit.

## Day 18: Hoi An

Rent a bicycle for the rice fields and villages between Hội An and **An Bàng Beach**. Return through the lanes north of the old town.

Return to **Đà Nẵng Airport** for an evening flight to **Ho Chi Minh City**. The overland alternative is the train from **Đà Nẵng to Sài Gòn**, around **seventeen to twenty hours**, requiring an additional travel day.

## Day 19: Ho Chi Minh City

Visit the **War Remnants Museum** and **Independence Palace**, then walk through the central streets to the post office, Notre-Dame Cathedral area and the old civic buildings around Nguyễn Huệ.

Use a city bus to reach **Chợ Lớn** for Bình Tây Market and Thiên Hậu Temple, then return to District 1 for the evening.

## Day 20: Mekong Delta

Take a full-day organised trip from Ho Chi Minh City into the **Mekong Delta**. A standard Mỹ Tho or Bến Tre route combines the main river crossing, smaller canals, village workshops and lunch before returning to the city.

## Day 21: Cu Chi

Take a day trip from **Ho Chi Minh City to the Củ Chi tunnels**. The visit covers the preserved tunnel area, entrances, traps and wartime interpretation before the return journey. [This small-group excursion visits the Bến Dược section and includes return transport (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/ho-chi-minh-city-l272/hcmc-cu-chi-ben-duoc-tunnel-authenticless-touristy-max-10-t619144/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

Independently, take **bus 13 to Củ Chi bus station**, then **bus 79 to Bến Dược**. This reaches the **Bến Dược** tunnel site; **Bến Đình** is a different visitor area used by many organised tours.

## Day 22: Cao Dai Temple

Travel to **Tây Ninh** for the main **Cao Đài temple** and one of its daily ceremonies. A booked day trip handles the timing and the road transfer. Independently, take an intercity bus to Tây Ninh and continue locally to the Holy See compound.

## Day 23: Phu Quoc

Fly from Ho Chi Minh City to Phú Quốc and use the airport bus or accommodation transfer to **Dương Đông**. Visit Dương Đông Market and **Dinh Cậu Temple**, then continue along the west coast for the beach and sunset.

## Day 24: Phu Quoc

Use the final day for the southern island: the fishing port around **An Thới**, one south-coast beach and the roads between the pepper farms and villages. Local buses cover the main north-south road, while a scooter or booked island vehicle is needed for several separate coastal stops.

Flights connect Phú Quốc directly with Ho Chi Minh City for the return journey.

## With more days

### Another night in Ninh Binh

Take a day trip to **Cúc Phương National Park**, west of Ninh Binh, for its forest trails, caves and **Endangered Primate Rescue Center**. Trips leave from Ninh Binh and Tam Cốc; allow around **1:30 by road each way**, with the rest of the day inside the park.

### More time in Huế

Add **Tự Đức's tomb**, with its lake, wooden pavilions and walled burial grounds, then the nearby **Từ Hiếu Pagoda** in the pine woods. Both lie southwest of central Huế and can be combined in a half-day excursion. Use the afternoon for the smaller streets and garden houses near **Kim Long**, west along the Perfume River.

### Hội An before the long journey south

Visit **Mỹ Sơn Sanctuary**, around **an hour by road from Hội An**. The surviving brick temple towers stand in a wooded valley and were built by the rulers of **Champa**. Small-group morning excursions return to Hội An in the afternoon. For the onward train to Ho Chi Minh City, add a travel day: the journey from Đà Nẵng takes roughly **seventeen to twenty hours**.

### Sapa and the northern valleys

Add **three or four days from Hanoi** for **Sapa** and the **Mường Hoa Valley**. Buses reach Sapa in around **six hours**; the overnight train reaches **Lào Cai** in around **eight hours**, followed by another **hour uphill by bus**. Spend two days walking between the terraced fields and villages around **Lao Chải** and **Tả Van**, with a night in the valley if taking a guided overnight walk. Return to Hanoi before continuing to Ha Long Bay.

### Đà Lạt between Hội An and Ho Chi Minh City

Add **two or three days in Đà Lạt**, plus the journeys. The city has an old railway station, **Linh Phước Pagoda**, the **Crazy House** and cafés using the coffee grown on the surrounding plateau. A countryside day can combine coffee farms and waterfalls outside town. From Hội An, return to Đà Nẵng for a flight to **Liên Khương**, or travel overland via **Nha Trang**; the Nha Trang–Đà Lạt bus takes around **four hours**. Continue to Ho Chi Minh City by bus, roughly **seven to eight hours**.

## With fewer days

For **three weeks**, finish in **Ho Chi Minh City after Củ Chi**, leaving Tây Ninh and Phú Quốc for another trip. That keeps the four-day Ha Giang Loop, the two-night bay cruise and the central Vietnam stops.

For **two weeks**, choose the northern route through **Hanoi, Ha Giang, Ha Long Bay and Ninh Binh**, or continue from Hanoi straight to **Huế and Hội An** after Ninh Binh, omitting the loop and cruise.
