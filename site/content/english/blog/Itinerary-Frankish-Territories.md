## Day 1: Brussels

From **Brussels-Central**, walk down to **Grand-Place** for the guildhalls, town hall and Maison du Roi. Continue through the covered **Galeries Royales Saint-Hubert**, then return through the streets around the Bourse and **Manneken Pis**.

Continue through the Sablon to the Palace of Justice viewpoint and descend by lift into the Marolles. Return through the comic-book murals and the lanes around Sainte-Catherine.

## Day 2: Brussels

Take the metro to **Schuman** for the **European Quarter**. Walk around the Parliament buildings and **Parc Léopold**, then continue to **Parc du Cinquantenaire**, where the triple arch stands between the museums.

For the Art Nouveau part, travel separately to **Saint-Gilles**: it is southwest of the centre, not on the walk back from Cinquantenaire. The streets around **Rue Américaine** include **Victor Horta's house and studio**, now the Horta Museum.

The House of European History and Parliamentarium are the main visits around the European Quarter. From Cinquantenaire, Metro Line 1 returns directly towards the centre.

## Day 3: Ghent

![Central Ghent](images/blog/belgium1.jpg)

![The old streets of Ghent](images/blog/belgium2.jpg)

Take a direct **SNCB train from Brussels to Gent-Sint-Pieters**, around **0:35**, then **tram 1 or 3 to Kouter** and walk north into the centre.

Begin at **St Bavo's Cathedral**, where the *Ghent Altarpiece* is displayed, then walk past the **Belfry** and **St Nicholas' Church** to **St Michael's Bridge**. Below it are the **Graslei and Korenlei** quays, lined with former guildhalls. Follow the canal north to **Gravensteen**, the stone castle in the middle of town.

Walk through the Patershol streets behind Gravensteen and return along the canals towards St Michael's Bridge. Return to Kouter for the tram to Gent-Sint-Pieters.

## Day 4: Bruges

Take a direct train from **Gent-Sint-Pieters to Brugge**, around **0:25**. Walk through the beguinage and Minnewater to the Markt, then continue around Burg Square and the canals.

Climb the Belfry if the timed entry is available, then walk beyond the busiest streets to the windmills along Kruisvest before returning to the station. [Guided Bruges walks, including combinations with a canal ride, are listed here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/bruges-l854/walking-tours-tc3/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

Take the direct train from **Brugge back to Brussels**, around **an hour**, after the visit.

## Day 5: Aachen

![Aachen Cathedral](images/blog/germany.jpg)

Take the international train from Brussels to **Aachen**. Walk from Aachen Hauptbahnhof to the cathedral, treasury, Rathaus and old streets.

Direct high-speed trains from **Bruxelles-Midi / Brussel-Zuid to Aachen Hauptbahnhof** take around **1:10**.

The cathedral and treasury need the main part of the afternoon. Finish around the Elisenbrunnen and the small lanes inside the former city walls.

## Day 6: Aachen

Visit **Centre Charlemagne** for Aachen's history, then the **Couven Museum**, set inside an eighteenth-century townhouse with furnished rooms and an old pharmacy. Walk north through the old streets to **Ponttor**, one of the surviving medieval city gates.

Continue uphill to **Lousberg**, on the northern edge of the centre. Paths wind through the wooded park to viewpoints over Aachen. Return through the centre for the thermal-water fountains around **Elisenbrunnen**.

## Day 7: Trier

![A street in Trier](images/blog/germany1.jpg)

![Trier Cathedral](images/blog/germany2.jpg)

Take a morning train from **Aachen Hauptbahnhof to Trier Hauptbahnhof**, around **four hours including changes**. Connections run via **Cologne and Koblenz**, or via **Euskirchen**.

Walk from the **Porta Nigra** through **Hauptmarkt** to **Trier Cathedral** and the **Liebfrauenkirche** beside it. Continue south to the **Aula Palatina**, the enormous Roman throne hall, then the **Imperial Baths**. Finish at the **Roman Bridge** and return north along the Moselle.

## Day 8: Manternach

![The forest trail near Manternach](images/blog/luxembourg2.jpg)

From **Trier**, take the Luxembourg-bound regional train through **Wasserbillig** to **Manternach**.

The **Traumschleife Manternacher Fiels** starts at **A Wiewesch nature centre**, on **Syrdallstrooss**. The **8,2 km loop** takes around **2:30**, passing rock faces, moss-covered slopes and the **Syre stream** before climbing back towards the village. There are no food stops along the forest trail. After the walk, continue by train from **Manternach to Luxembourg City**.

## Day 9: Luxembourg

![Luxembourg City](images/blog/luxembourg.jpg)

Walk from the upper city to the Chemin de la Corniche and descend into **Grund**, returning by lift. Continue around the cathedral and Place d'Armes.

Cross the Alzette below the old fortifications, then take the public lift back to the upper city for Place Guillaume II and the Grand Ducal Palace.

## Day 10: Luxembourg

![The fortifications of Luxembourg](images/blog/luxembourg1.jpg)

Visit the **Bock promontory and casemates**, the passages cut into the city's old defences. The openings look over the lower town and the Alzette valley.

Walk to **Hamilius** for the tram towards **Kirchberg**, getting off at **Philharmonie–Mudam**. **Mudam** occupies a modern building beside **Fort Thüngen**; the fort houses the **Musée Dräi Eechelen**, and the park has views back towards the old city. Buses, trams and second-class trains are free within Luxembourg; the German part of the Trier journey still needs a ticket.

## With more days

### Ghent and Bruges after the day visitors leave

In **Ghent**, visit the **Museum of Fine Arts (MSK)** beside **Citadelpark** for its collection spanning six centuries of painting and sculpture. Return to the centre for the illuminated quays along **Graslei** and **Korenlei**.

In **Bruges**, visit the **Groeningemuseum** for paintings by **Jan van Eyck** and **Hans Memling**, then walk through the smaller lanes around **Dijver** and **Boniface Bridge**. The museum closes for refurbishment from **11 January 2027**; its core collection moves to **BRUSK from March 2027**.

### Brussels: Art Nouveau as a separate day

Spend longer around **Saint-Gilles** and **Ixelles** for the Art Nouveau houses. Start at the **Horta Museum** on Rue Américaine and continue through the streets around **Avenue Louise** and the **Ixelles ponds**. [Art Nouveau walks are available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/horta-museum-l6371/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

### Another Luxembourg walking day

Take a bus from Luxembourg City to **Echternach**, then follow the **E1 walking circuit** from the basilica. The **13,5 km loop** crosses the narrow **Wollefsschlucht** gorge, the Aesbach valley and the **Huel Lee** rock caves near Berdorf. Allow around **four hours** for the walk, plus the bus journeys and a look around Echternach's abbey and old centre.
