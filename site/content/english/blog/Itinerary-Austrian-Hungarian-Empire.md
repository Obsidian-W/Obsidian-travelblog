## Day 1: Budapest

![Hungarian Parliament](images/blog/hungary1.jpg)

Start at the **Hungarian Parliament** and Kossuth Square, then walk along the Danube to the **Shoes memorial**. From the river, head inland to **St Stephen's Basilica**, whose dome overlooks Pest. Continue through **Deák Ferenc tér** to the **Great Synagogue** on Dohány Street and the Jewish Quarter around Kazinczy Street.

The Parliament interior is a guided visit, separate from the walk around the building. Book through the Parliament's own ticket service if you want to see the main staircase and the hall containing the Hungarian crown. **Tram 2** follows the Pest embankment, with views across to Castle Hill.

## Day 2: Budapest

![Vajdahunyad Castle](images/blog/hungary2.jpg)

Take **bus 16 from Deák Ferenc tér** to **Castle Hill**. Visit **Matthias Church** and the arcades of **Fisherman's Bastion**, then walk south through the old streets to **Buda Castle** and its terraces above the Danube.

Return by bus 16 to Deák Ferenc tér, then take **Metro Line 1 to Hősök tere** for **Heroes' Square**, **Vajdahunyad Castle** and **City Park**. The castle combines several architectural styles around a courtyard and lake. **Széchenyi Baths** are beside the park, with indoor thermal rooms and large outdoor pools.

Gellért is not an alternative at present: the [bath's official website](https://www.gellertbath.hu/) lists it as closed for renovation since October 2025.

## Day 3: Danube Bend

Take the morning train from Budapest-Nyugati to **Esztergom**. Visit the basilica, treasury and castle museum, then walk down to the Danube and across the Maria Valeria Bridge for the view back from Slovakia.

The [**880–889 bus corridor**](https://www.mavcsoport.hu/mav-szemelyszallitas/belfoldi-utazas/vonali-menetrendek/2920) follows the river from **Esztergom** through **Visegrád** and **Szentendre**. In Visegrád, leave the riverside road for the **Upper Castle**, high above the bend in the Danube. This is an uphill walk; the castle is not beside the bus stop.

Continue by bus to **Szentendre**. Walk between **Fő tér**, the Serbian churches and the riverside promenade, then take **HÉV H5** back to **Batthyány tér** in Budapest.

## Day 4: Kosice and Poprad

![Central Kosice](images/blog/slovakia.jpg)
![A church in Kosice](images/blog/slovakia1.jpg)

Take the morning train from Budapest-Keleti to **Košice**. I used it as a stop rather than a full day: leave the bag at the station, walk along Hlavná to St Elisabeth Cathedral, the Urban Tower, singing fountain and State Theatre, then return for the train to **Poprad-Tatry**.

I continued to Poprad and used it as the base for both mountain days. You could stay in Košice instead and travel to Poprad the following day, adding a night before the hikes.

## Day 5: Mount Rysy

![Strbske Pleso in the High Tatras](images/blog/slovakia2.jpg)

Take the Tatra Electric Railway from Poprad-Tatry to **Štrbské Pleso** and follow the marked trail towards Popradské pleso and Chata pod Rysmi. The return hike to **Mount Rysy** fills the day and includes chains on the steeper upper section. The upper path passes **Chata pod Rysmi** and climbs to the border ridge, with views into Poland. The [seasonal closure](https://www.tatry.sk/en/information-center/tatra-national-park/visiting-rules/winter-closure-of-marked-hiking-trails/) runs from November through May; this is a summer hiking day.

## Day 6: High Tatras

![A trail in the High Tatras](images/blog/slovakia3.jpg)

Take the electric railway to **Starý Smokovec**, then the funicular to Hrebienok. Walk between Rainerova Hut, the waterfalls and Zamkovský Hut before returning to Hrebienok.

I walked all the way down instead of taking the funicular. The descent was steep and not especially interesting. The funicular is the better return to Starý Smokovec.

Board the evening train towards Bratislava.

## Day 7: Bratislava

![The Blue Church in Bratislava](images/blog/slovakia4.jpg)

Walk through Michael's Gate, the main square and the old streets below **Bratislava Castle**. Descend to the **Danube promenade**, then walk east to the **Blue Church** on Bezručova Street. Its pale-blue façade, roof and tiled details are quite different from the buildings around the main square.

Take the direct regional train from Bratislava hlavná stanica to Vienna.

## Day 8: Vienna

![Karlskirche in Vienna](images/blog/vienna1.jpg)
![Belvedere Palace](images/blog/vienna2.jpg)

Walk the **Ringstrasse** from the Opera past the **Hofburg**, Parliament and Rathaus. Turn into the centre for **St Stephen's Cathedral**, then follow Graben and Kohlmarkt back towards the Hofburg courtyards.

Take **tram D to Schloss Belvedere** for the palace gardens and the Upper Belvedere collection, including Klimt's *The Kiss*. **Karlskirche**, with its two carved columns and green dome, is west of the palace on the walk back towards Karlsplatz.

## Day 9: Vienna

Take the U4 to **Schönbrunn** for the palace rooms, gardens and Gloriette. Return on the same line to **Kettenbrückengasse** for **Naschmarkt**. Walk past the **Secession Building**, with its gold-leaf dome, to the **MuseumsQuartier**. The **Leopold Museum** has works by Schiele and Klimt; across Maria-Theresien-Platz, the **Kunsthistorisches Museum** has the larger old-master collection.

Schönbrunn and one of these museums take most of the day, with the market between them.

Vienna bored me more than the other stops, even with two days there.
