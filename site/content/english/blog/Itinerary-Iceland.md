> I spent six days in Iceland in August 2021. Reykjavik was my base: two days in the city, then four day trips that I had booked online.
>
> The last one was for the volcanic eruption near Fagradalsfjall. The 2021 eruption ended, so that day cannot be repeated now.

All four excursions left Reykjavik and returned there in the evening. I kept the same accommodation for the whole trip. Travelling alone, the tours also cost less than renting a car for six days and gave me four very different parts of Iceland without moving my luggage every morning.

## Day 1: Reykjavik

Keflavik Airport is outside Reykjavik. Airport coaches run to **BSÍ**, just south of the centre. The cheaper public option is [Strætó route 55](https://www.straeto.is/en/route-planner/bus-kefairport), although only some departures continue as far as BSÍ.

Begin at **Hallgrimskirkja** and go up the tower for the view over the corrugated roofs. Walk downhill along **Skólavörðustígur**, the street painted in rainbow colours, then continue through **Laugavegur** and the compact centre around **Austurvöllur**, Parliament and **Tjörnin**.

From there, follow the water to **Harpa**, the **Sun Voyager** and the old harbour.

## Day 2: Reykjavik

Start with the **National Museum of Iceland** on Suðurgata. Its permanent exhibition runs from the settlement of Iceland to the modern country.

Walk back around Tjörnin to **Aðalstræti** and the **Settlement Exhibition**, built around the remains of a tenth-century longhouse.

Continue to the old harbour and Grandi for the **Reykjavik Maritime Museum**, then finish in one of the municipal geothermal pools. **Sundhöllin** is close to Hallgrimskirkja; **Vesturbæjarlaug** is west of the centre. Both have swimming pools and several hot tubs. The [official pool list](https://visitreykjavik.is/service-category/public-pool) has the current opening hours.

## Day 3: South Coast

![The path behind Seljalandsfoss](images/blog/iceland2.jpg)

![Ice floating across Jökulsárlón](images/blog/iceland1.jpg)


I booked this trip online from Reykjavik. It was the longest road day of the week: roughly fourteen hours along Route 1 through the waterfalls and black-sand coast before reaching **Jökulsárlón**. A normal South Coast tour turns around near Vík; the booking must explicitly include the glacier lagoon.

The first major stop was **Seljalandsfoss**. In summer, the path can continue behind the waterfall, which also means getting wet even when it is not raining.


At **Skógafoss**, see the waterfall from below and climb the staircase for the view from the top. The route then continues past Vík to **Reynisfjara**, with its basalt columns, cave and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore.

Reynisfjara has killed visitors. Stay well back from the water: sneaker waves travel much farther across the sand than the previous ones. Coastal erosion can also narrow the usable beach; check the [Icelandic Tourist Board's latest Reynisfjara notice](https://www.ferdamalastofa.is/en/news/category/1/reynisfjara-recent-changes-due-to-coastal-erosion) before leaving Reykjavik.

The trip also went all the way to **Jökulsárlón**, where pieces of ice drift across the lagoon below Breiðamerkurjökull. Reaching it from Reykjavik makes this roughly a fourteen-hour excursion.


For the same long version, look for a tour that explicitly lists both Reynisfjara and Jökulsárlón; the journey takes around fourteen hours. [This current Jökulsárlón tour](https://city-sightseeing.com/en/49/reykjavik/2527/jokulsarlon-glacier-lagoon-tour) follows that route. The shorter [EastWest South Coast tour](https://eastwest.is/south-coast) turns back after Reynisfjara and Sólheimajökull and takes around ten hours.

## Day 4: Landmannalaugar

![The rhyolite mountains around Landmannalaugar](images/blog/iceland4.jpg)

![Volcanic terrain on the road through the Icelandic Highlands](images/blog/iceland3.jpg)

I booked Landmannalaugar online as another day trip from Reykjavik. The final section enters the Highlands, so the excursion uses a high-clearance bus or Super Jeep.

The road crosses bare volcanic ground before reaching the colourful rhyolite mountains at **Landmannalaugar**. Short trails run through the black **Laugahraun** lava field and towards **Grænagil**. Longer routes climb **Brennisteinsalda** or **Bláhnúkur**.



The access road is seasonal and bad weather can cancel the departure. [This current Landmannalaugar Super Jeep tour](https://www.landmannalaugartours.com/landmannalaugar-safari-super-jeep-day-tour) leaves Reykjavik for a twelve-hour day in summer. For trail and reserve information, use the [official Fjallabak page](https://ust.is/english/visiting-iceland/protected-areas/south/fjallabak-nature-reserve/outdoor-activities-and-hiking-trails/).

## Day 5: Golden Circle

![Gullfoss](images/blog/iceland5.jpg)

The Golden Circle day trip covered **Thingvellir**, **Geysir**, **Gullfoss** and **Kerið**.

At **Thingvellir**, walk through Almannagjá to the old parliament site and continue towards Öxarárfoss if it is included in the stop.

At the **Geysir geothermal area**, Geysir itself is mostly dormant. Strokkur is the one erupting every few minutes. The walking paths also pass steaming ground, smaller hot springs and bubbling pools.

**Gullfoss** comes next. The upper viewpoint shows both stages of the Hvítá river; the lower path descends close to the spray when it is open.


My route also stopped at **Kerið**, the red volcanic crater used as the cover photograph for this article. Walk along the rim first, then take the stairs down to the lake.

[Reykjavik Excursions still runs the basic Golden Circle route](https://www.re.is/tour/golden-circle-direct/). Check the stops before booking because Kerið is not included in every version.

## Day 6: Fagradalsfjall

My final excursion went to the eruption near **Fagradalsfjall** on the Reykjanes Peninsula. The vehicle only handled the transfer from Reykjavik; reaching the viewpoint meant hiking across exposed volcanic ground.

This was the 2021 eruption. Lava later covered parts of the original paths, new eruptions opened elsewhere on Reykjanes and access rules changed repeatedly. The route I walked is no longer a current eruption trail.

For any future eruption, the destination can change from one day to the next. [SafeTravel Iceland](https://safetravel.is/) publishes the open routes and closure notices.
