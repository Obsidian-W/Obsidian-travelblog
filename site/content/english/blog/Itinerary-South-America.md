# Peru

## Day 1: Lima

Begin with a walking tour around **Plaza Mayor**, the cathedral, Archbishop's Palace and the **Monastery of San Francisco**. Walk through the pedestrian centre to Plaza San Martín, then take the Metropolitano south to **Barranco** for the Bridge of Sighs and the Pacific cliffs.

Take the night bus from **Lima to Huaraz**, around **eight hours**. Services leave **La Victoria** or **Plaza Norte**, depending on the operator.

## Day 2: Huaraz

Keep the first day in **Huaraz** for acclimatisation after the night bus from Lima. Walk around Plaza de Armas, the Regional Museum of Ancash and its stone garden, then climb to a viewpoint above town.

I booked the next three Cordillera Blanca day trips through the hostel for about **10€ each**, returning to Huaraz each evening.

## Day 3: Pastoruri Glacier

![Pastoruri Glacier in the Cordillera Blanca](images/blog/peru1.jpg)

Take the hostel minibus south for **Pastoruri Glacier**. The drive crosses the high plateau and stops among the giant **puya raimondii** plants before the final walk from the car park. The glacier trail rises above **5 000 m**. The full excursion takes around **eight to nine hours**, including the road stops and return to Huaraz.

## Day 4: Laguna 69

![Laguna 69 in the Cordillera Blanca](images/blog/peru2.jpg)

Leave before dawn in the hostel minibus for the **Laguna 69** trailhead in Huascarán National Park. The path climbs past waterfalls and a broad glacial valley before the final steep section to the turquoise lake. Allow **five to six hours for the 14 km hike**, plus roughly **three hours on the road each way**. [A Laguna 69 day trip from Huaraz is available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/huaraz-l360/huaraz-full-day-laguna-69-views-of-laguna-llanganuco-t829310/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Day 5: Laguna Paron

Take the third hostel excursion to **Laguna Parón**. Walk beside the lake and climb the rocky viewpoint above it for the full view towards the glaciers. Return to Huaraz, collect the main bag and take the night bus north to **Trujillo**, roughly **eight hours**.

## Day 6: Trujillo

![Historic central Trujillo](images/blog/peru3.jpg)

After arriving in **Trujillo**, walk around **Plaza de Armas**, the cathedral and coloured colonial façades. In the afternoon, take a combi towards **Moche** or a local tour to the **Huacas del Sol y de la Luna**. Allow around **two to three hours** for the museum and archaeological visit, plus the road connection. The painted walls inside Huaca de la Luna are the main reason to come.

## Day 7: Chan Chan and Huanchaco

Take a westbound combi or shared tour to **Chan Chan**, then continue to **Huanchaco** on the same side of Trujillo. Visit the beach and the *caballitos de totora*; I also went surfing here. Return to Trujillo, collect the bag and take the night bus south to **Lima**, around **ten to eleven hours**.

## Day 8: Lima to Paracas

After the night bus, spend the morning at **Museo Larco** in **Pueblo Libre**. Its galleries cover pre-Columbian ceramics, textiles and metalwork; allow around **two hours**. Take an afternoon bus from **La Victoria or Atocongo to Paracas/El Chaco**, around **four hours**, then walk around the harbour.

## Day 9: Paracas

![The coast of Paracas National Reserve](images/blog/peru4.jpg)

Take the morning boat to the **Ballestas Islands**, then join a van through **Paracas National Reserve** for the peninsula cliffs, beaches and desert coast. [This Ballestas and reserve excursion combines the boat and road visits (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/paracas-l32470/paracas-ballestas-islands-and-paracas-national-reserve-tour-t855974/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). The boat leaves **Muelle El Chaco** and takes around **two hours**; the reserve circuit adds roughly **three to four hours**.

## Day 10: Ica and Huacachina

Take a bus from **Paracas to Ica**, around **1:10**, then continue to **Huacachina** by an accommodation-arranged transfer. Walk around the oasis and join a late-afternoon dune-buggy and sandboarding trip. Return to **Ica** for the evening bus to **Nazca**, around **2:30**.

## Day 11: Nazca

![The Nazca Lines from the air](images/blog/peru5.jpg)
![A wrong turn through the outskirts of Nazca](images/blog/peruExtra.jpg)

I took a flight over the **Nazca Lines**, then visited the **Cantalloc aqueducts**. Add the Antonini Archaeological Museum, the Chauchilla cemetery and the roadside observation tower if the flight time leaves enough of the day.

I got lost walking through the poorer outskirts of Nazca; that is where the second photograph comes from.

Take the night bus from **Nazca to Arequipa**, around **nine hours**.

## Day 12: Arequipa

![Santa Catalina Monastery in Arequipa](images/blog/peru6.jpg)

Arrive in Arequipa after the night bus from Nazca. Walk around the white-stone **Plaza de Armas**, cathedral and **Church of the Company**, then spend the afternoon inside **Santa Catalina Monastery**.

## Day 13: Colca Canyon

![The landscape outside Arequipa](images/blog/peru7.jpg)

Leave Arequipa early on the booked two-day trip. The agency vehicle handles the road through Chivay and the stop at **Cruz del Cóndor**, then the hiking part begins with the long descent into **Colca Canyon**. The trail drops towards the villages and the oasis at the bottom, where the group sleeps.

## Day 14: Colca Canyon

Start climbing before sunrise. The ascent to **Cabanaconde** takes around **two to four hours**. Continue by road through **Chivay** and the high plateau to **Arequipa**, usually returning around **18:00**. Night buses from **Terminal Terrestre or Terrapuerto to Cusco** take around **ten hours**.

## Day 15: Cusco

Walk around **Plaza de Armas**, **Qorikancha**, **San Blas** and the Inca walls along **Hatun Rumiyoc**. From **Mercado Rosaspata**, take a **Cristo Blanco** or **Señor de Huerto** bus uphill to **Q'enqo**, then walk towards **Sacsayhuamán**. Descend through **San Cristóbal** to the centre. Allow around **four hours** for the bus, archaeological stops and return walk.

## Day 16: Rainbow Mountain

Leave before dawn on a shared minibus excursion to **Rainbow Mountain**. The road transfer takes most of the early morning before the walk even starts. The trail begins at extreme altitude and climbs towards the striped ridge, with Ausangate visible beyond it. Return to Cusco for the evening cultural performance.

## Day 17: Machu Picchu hike

Begin the **five-day booked trek**. My package included the Machu Picchu reservation, transport, food and the guide. The road transfer from **Cusco through Mollepata towards Soraypampa** takes around **three to four hours**. The first day passed **Humantay Lake** before camp; the uphill lake walk takes roughly **1:30–2 hours**, followed by the descent.

## Day 18: Machu Picchu hike

Cross **Salkantay Pass**, around **4 630 m**, then descend through **Wayracmachay** towards the warmer valley around **Chaullay**. This is the longest stage on a conventional five-day route, around **eight to ten hours on foot**.

## Day 19: Machu Picchu hike

Continue into the cloud forest towards **La Playa**, passing streams, cultivated slopes and small farms. The walking stage usually takes around **five to six hours**, followed by the overnight stop around **Santa Teresa or Lucmabamba**.

## Day 20: Machu Picchu hike

The final approach reaches **Hidroeléctrica**, then follows the railway beside the **Urubamba River** towards **Aguas Calientes**. This last section is around **10 km and three hours on foot**. Routes through **Llactapata** add a forest climb and descent before Hidroeléctrica.

## Day 21: Machu Picchu

![Machu Picchu](images/blog/peru8.jpg)

Enter **Machu Picchu** using the reservation included in the five-day package. The stone terraces and roofless buildings fill the ridge above the Urubamba, with the higher mountain paths climbing above the citadel. After the visit, the road-return option involves walking back to **Hidroeléctrica** for the agency minibus to **Cusco**. Train returns run from **Aguas Calientes to Ollantaytambo**.

## Day 22: Ollantaytambo

Take a full-day **Sacred Valley excursion from Cusco**, linking **Chinchero**, **Moray**, **Maras**, **Ollantaytambo** and **Pisac**. At Ollantaytambo, climb the fortress terraces towards the **Temple of the Sun**, then walk through the old Inca streets and their stone water channels. The complete circuit takes around **twelve hours** and returns to Cusco.

## Day 23: Cusco

Start at **San Pedro Market**, then walk through **Plaza San Francisco** to the **Regional Historical Museum** and the **Museum of Contemporary Art** near Plaza Regocijo. Continue south along Avenida El Sol to the **Pachacutec Monument** for its small historical display and viewpoint.

Take a night bus from **Cusco's Terminal Terrestre to Puerto Maldonado**, around **nine to eleven hours**.

## Day 24: Puerto Maldonado

![Travelling by boat outside Puerto Maldonado](images/blog/peru9.jpg)

My **three-day Amazon trip** was booked online. The operator collected the group in Puerto Maldonado and transferred us by road and boat to the lodge. The first afternoon included **Monkey Island**, followed by a night walk to look for insects, frogs and tarantulas.

## Day 25: Amazon

This was the complete day based at the lodge. We navigated the river and smaller waterways to spot wildlife, then continued on foot through the forest with the guide. The programme included another walk after dark.

## Day 26: Puerto Maldonado

Finish the final lodge activity and return by boat and road to **Puerto Maldonado**. The overnight bus towards **Juliaca and Puno** takes around **thirteen hours** to Puno.

## Day 27: Amantani

![Lake Titicaca](images/blog/peru10.jpg)

Continue from Juliaca to Puno and take the boat across Lake Titicaca to **Amantani**. I had met a Peruvian family during the Amazon trip and they invited me to their home on the island. It became one of the best side trips of the entire journey.

Walk through the villages and climb towards the Pachamama and Pachatata viewpoints before staying with the family. Continue to Copacabana the following day for the start of the Bolivia itinerary.

# Bolivia

## Day 28: Copacabana

Return to **Puno** on an early boat from Amantani, around **2:30**. Continue by bus towards **Copacabana**, with exit and entry formalities at the Peru–Bolivia border. Visit the **Basilica of Our Lady of Copacabana**, the market streets and the lakefront, then climb **Cerro Calvario** above the town.

Take a late-afternoon bus from **Copacabana to La Paz**, around **four hours**. At the **Tiquina Strait**, passengers cross in a small launch while the bus travels separately on a barge. Reboard on the opposite shore for the final road into the city.

## Day 29: La Paz

![A market in La Paz](images/blog/bolivia1.jpg)

Start at **Plaza Murillo**, then walk through Calle Jaén to San Francisco and the **Witches' Market**. Ride Mi Teleférico from the centre towards El Alto and change lines for the broad view across the valley. The cable-car network is also the easiest way to cross La Paz without spending the afternoon in traffic.

## Day 30: Tiwanaku and La Paz

Take a morning bus or shared tour west to **Tiwanaku**, the country's most important pre-Columbian archaeological site. Visit the Kalasasaya complex, the Gate of the Sun, the Semi-Subterranean Temple and the two site museums before returning to La Paz. Minibuses leave the **Cementerio General** area and take around **1:30–2:00 each way**. [A Tiwanaku guided trip from La Paz is available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/la-paz-l4674/from-la-paz-tiwanaku-tour-bolivia-t836709/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

In the evening, go to **Cholitas Wrestling** in El Alto. The performances mix lucha libre with the bowler hats and layered skirts worn by the cholitas. Shows do not run every evening, so move the Tiwanaku day within the La Paz stay if necessary.

Take the night bus from **La Paz's main bus terminal to Cochabamba**, around **eight hours**.

## Day 31: Torotoro

I spent around three hours in **Cochabamba** before continuing to Torotoro. Minibuses leave from **Avenida República near Avenida Barrientos**, on the La Cancha side of Cochabamba, and depart when full. The drive takes around **six hours**.

In **Torotoro**, register at the **national-park office**, where guides and groups are organised for the protected routes. Walk through the village afterwards.

## Day 32: Torotoro

![The landscape around Torotoro](images/blog/bolivia2.jpg)

Join the guided **dinosaur-footprint and Vergel Canyon circuit**, around **five hours**. The route crosses exposed rock with fossilised tracks, follows the canyon rim, then descends towards the **El Vergel waterfalls** before climbing back out.

## Day 33: Torotoro

Take the guided **Umajalanta Cave** excursion, around **seven hours including the surrounding rock formations and road stops**. The park supplies a helmet and light. Inside, the route squeezes through low passages, passes rock formations and reaches the pools with blind fish. Stay in Torotoro after the excursion.

## Day 34: Sucre

![Central Sucre](images/blog/bolivia3.jpg)

Leave Torotoro early for the **six-hour minibus to Cochabamba**, then continue towards **Sucre**. I took an afternoon minibus that left around **14:00** and reached Sucre around **21:00**. Larger overnight buses leave Cochabamba's **Terminal de Buses** around **21:00–21:30** and take roughly **eight to nine hours**.

## Day 35: Sucre

Walk around **Plaza 25 de Mayo**, visit **Casa de la Libertad** and continue through the white centre to **San Felipe Neri**, whose rooftop overlooks the church towers and tiled roofs. After the central market, visit the **Museum of Indigenous Art** and climb through the old streets to **La Recoleta** for the view over Sucre.

## Day 36: Potosi

Take a morning bus from **Sucre's main terminal to Potosí's Nueva Terminal**, roughly **2:30–3:00**. Local buses connect the new terminal with the historic centre. Start around Plaza 10 de Noviembre and the cathedral, then spend the afternoon in **Casa Nacional de la Moneda** among the colonial minting machinery and coin galleries.

## Day 37: Potosi

![Inside a mine in Potosi](images/blog/bolivia4.jpg)

I joined a tour into the **Cerro Rico** mines. The agencies provide protective clothing and stop at the miners' market before driving up the mountain. Conditions underground are cramped, dusty and difficult, with miners working around the visiting groups.

Back in the centre, visit the **Santa Teresa Convent Museum** if the tour returns early enough. Take an evening bus from **Potosí's Nueva Terminal to Uyuni**, around **four hours**, and stay in town before the four-day tour.

## Day 38: Uyuni tour

My **four-day Uyuni trip** was booked online. The first day began at the train cemetery and **Colchani**, then continued onto the Salar de Uyuni. The vehicle, driver and remote accommodation were included.

## Day 39: Uyuni tour

![Incahuasi during the Uyuni tour](images/blog/bolivia5.jpg)

Continue across the salar in the tour vehicle towards **Incahuasi Island**, when water and seasonal conditions allow access. The cactus-covered island, salt polygons and distant volcanoes occupy most of the day before another remote overnight stop.

## Day 40: Uyuni tour

![A coloured lagoon on the Uyuni tour](images/blog/bolivia6.jpg)

Cross the high-altitude desert in the same 4x4 through the stone formations and coloured lagoons of the Eduardo Avaroa reserve. This is the longest driving section, with stops for flamingos and the volcanic landscape between the lagoons.

## Day 41: Uyuni tour

Leave before sunrise for the Sol de Mañana geysers and hot springs, then drive to the border above **San Pedro de Atacama**. Complete the Bolivian exit formalities and change into the Chilean minibus supplied with the cross-border booking.

# Chile and Patagonia

## Day 42: San Pedro de Atacama

The four-day Uyuni tour ended at the Bolivian border, where I changed into the Chilean minibus for **San Pedro de Atacama**. The transfer stops for Chilean immigration before reaching town.

Leave the luggage at the accommodation and walk around the adobe centre, **San Pedro Church** and the archaeological museum. In the afternoon, take an excursion to **Valle de la Luna**, walking between the salt formations and ridges before the sunset viewpoint. Tour agencies leave from central San Pedro; public buses do not cover the valley circuit.

## Day 43: Atacama Desert

![The Atacama Desert](images/blog/chile1.jpg)

![A stop during the Atacama cycling trip](images/blog/chileExtra.jpg)

Leave before dawn by tour bus or minibus for **El Tatio**. The geyser field is more than 4,000 metres above sea level and the tours arrive during the coldest part of the morning, when the steam is most visible. Stops on the return vary between operators and may include the wetlands and small villages north of San Pedro.

Return to town for the afternoon. The Altiplanic Lagoons and Salar de Atacama require a separate excursion day.

## Day 44: Santiago

![Santiago](images/blog/chile2.jpg)

Take the booked shuttle from San Pedro to **Calama Airport**, then fly to Santiago. At Santiago airport, use an airport bus to **Pajaritos** or **Los Héroes** and continue by metro to the accommodation.

Walk around **Plaza de Armas**, the cathedral and the streets towards the former National Congress if daylight remains. Keep Bellavista and the hills for the complete Santiago day.

## Day 45: Santiago

Start at **La Moneda** and walk through the civic centre to the **Museum of Pre-Columbian Art**. Continue through Plaza de Armas and the **Central Market**, then cross the Mapocho towards **Bellavista**.

Take the funicular or walk up **Cerro San Cristóbal** for the view across Santiago. Return through Bellavista and **Lastarria**, with **Cerro Santa Lucía** as the final central stop if it is still open. The metro handles the long jump between the accommodation, centre and airport connection; the historical core itself is better covered on foot.

## Day 46: Punta Arenas

I flew from **Santiago to Punta Arenas**, around 3 hours 30 minutes in the air. This was the beginning of Patagonia for me.

Punta Arenas feels like the end of the world. It's windy, colourful, isolated and stretched along the Strait of Magellan.

I walked all over the city: **Plaza Muñoz Gamero**, the port, the waterfront sculptures and **Cerro de la Cruz** for the view over the rooftops and the strait. The city isn't packed with must-sees, but the atmosphere does most of the work. Ships in the distance, empty streets, harsh light... Otherworldly is the word.

The airport is outside the city. I used the transfer into the centre, dropped my stuff and spent the rest of the day walking.

## Day 47: Puerto Natales

In winter 2022, there was **one bus a day** from Punta Arenas to Puerto Natales. It left from the bus terminal in Punta Arenas and arrived at **Rodoviario Puerto Natales** around three to four hours later.

The road was really beautiful: empty land, distant mountains and that very Patagonian feeling that everything is much farther away than it looks.

My first stop was the **Museo Histórico Municipal**. From the museum, I walked north along the waterfront through **Monumento al Viento**, the newer waterfront and **Muelle Fiscal**, then **Muelle Histórico** and the ugly bronze Milodón sculpture near the northern entrance to town.

Afterwards, I booked the next two days with a local agency: one photography tour by car and one full-day hike to Base Torres. I got a good price because I booked both at once. The winter transfers to Torres del Paine were included, so Puerto Natales remained my base.

## Day 48: Torres del Paine

![Torres del Paine in winter](images/blog/patagonia3.jpg)
*Not a bad place for a photography tour.*

This was called a photography tour, but it was essentially a full-day road trip through **Torres del Paine National Park** in a small group.

We first went to **Cueva del Milodón**, then drove through the park and stopped at the nicest viewpoints: mountains, lakes, waterfalls and snow everywhere. It filled the whole day easily. The distances are huge, so most of the tour was short walks and photo stops between long bits in the car.

I didn't mind. The roads and the views from the car were part of the trip.

I booked locally. [GetYourGuide also lists Torres del Paine photography tours, including a comparable full-day photo safari (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/torres-del-paine-l177394/photography-tours-tc19/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). It isn't the agency I used, and the listings don't all include Puerto Natales pickup or Cueva del Milodón.

> Cueva del Milodón is around 25 kilometres north of Puerto Natales. The cave is interesting. The giant sculpture in Puerto Natales is still ugly.

## Day 49: Base Torres

![Base Torres covered in snow](images/blog/patagonia2.jpg)
*Base Torres in winter.*

The second tour was a full-day hike to **Base Torres**, booked with the same agency. The agency transfer left Puerto Natales early and brought the group back after the hike.

We left Puerto Natales early and started near **Hotel Las Torres**. The return hike is roughly **20 kilometres** and took most of the day. There weren't many people insane enough to do it in that weather, so our group was small.

The trail was covered in snow and it was really cold, especially whenever we stopped. The final climb was the slowest part, but the frozen lake and the towers above it were fucking beautiful.

For winter 2026, the park requires an accredited guide, microspikes, trekking poles, a headlamp and proper winter clothing for Base Torres. The rules change with trail conditions, so the [official Torres del Paine website](https://parquetorresdelpaine.cl/) is the place to check.

[Guided hiking departures from Puerto Natales are listed here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/puerto-natales-l32145/hiking-tc71/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

Cold, tiring, 100% worth it.

## Day 50: El Calafate

I took the morning international bus from **Rodoviario Puerto Natales** to the **Terminal de Ómnibus de El Calafate**, crossing into Argentina at **Cerro Castillo/Cancha Carrera**.

The bus is scheduled for around five hours. With immigration and winter conditions, five to seven hours is more realistic. Mine ran once a day.

After arriving, I walked around central **El Calafate**, mainly along **Avenida del Libertador** and towards **Laguna Nimez**. It's a tourist base more than an exciting city, but everything needed for El Chaltén and Perito Moreno is there.

## Day 51: El Chalten

I booked a full-day trip from El Calafate to **El Chaltén**.

El Chaltén is around **220 kilometres from El Calafate**, close to three hours each way. Most of the day was spent driving, although the road was beautiful and we still had time to walk around town and hike nearby.

It was cold, rainy and the mountains spent a lot of time hiding behind clouds. The weather kinda ruined the trip, but I have no regrets. El Chaltén was still beautiful.

[This El Chaltén day trip from El Calafate includes the Cóndores viewpoint and Chorrillo del Salto (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/el-calafate-l544/from-el-calafate-full-day-hiking-tour-to-el-chalten-t233572/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

With more time, I would stay in El Chaltén for several days. Six hours in a car for a few hours of hiking isn't the best ratio.

## Day 52: Perito Moreno

![Perito Moreno Glacier](images/blog/patagonia.jpg)
*Perito Moreno, plus a bird that understood framing better than I did.*

Perito Moreno was much easier. Transport leaves from El Calafate, follows **Ruta Provincial 11** and reaches the glacier in around 1 hour 30 minutes. It's roughly 80 kilometres from town.

Once there, I spent the day walking between the viewpoints facing the ice. The paths are easy and there are enough angles to keep stopping every five minutes for another picture.

The [official Los Glaciares National Park page](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/parquesnacionales/patagonia-austral/parque-nacional-los-glaciares/horarios-como-llegar-0) lists different winter and summer access hours.

[This Perito Moreno trip from El Calafate includes the walkways and an optional boat ride (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/el-calafate-l544/el-calafate-perito-moreno-glacier-optional-boat-trip-t231537/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher). I used local transportation, not that operator. The national-park entrance may be separate.

Perito Moreno was one of the highlights. In winter it was quiet, bright blue and surrounded by snow. Even the bird in my picture knew where to pose.

## Day 53: Punta Arenas

This was a complete transport day with two buses back to back:

1. **El Calafate to Puerto Natales** in the morning, around five to seven hours including the border.
2. **Puerto Natales to Punta Arenas** in the afternoon, around three to four hours.

Both used the main bus terminals. I had enough time between them in Puerto Natales, but the border can make the first bus late. I flew out of Punta Arenas early the following morning, so the timing mattered.

From El Calafate, continuing through Argentina makes more sense. I had to return to Chile, so I couldn't do that.

## With more days

### La Paz and El Alto before Torotoro

Take the **Red cable-car line to 16 de Julio in El Alto** for the market, largest on **Thursdays and Sundays**, then continue towards the colourful **cholet** buildings. A guided architecture walk can include interiors that are private venues. Back in La Paz, add **Calle Jaén's small museums** and the walks around **Sopocachi** and **Parque del Montículo**.

### Sucre without the immediate Potosí transfer

Visit **La Glorieta Castle**, five kilometres outside Sucre on the Potosí road. **Bus 4** leaves from **Mercado Central or Avenida Hernando Siles** and terminates at the castle. The rooms, towers and gardens took around **4:30 including transport** on my trip.

Another excursion reaches **Cal Orcko and the Cretaceous Park**, where a near-vertical rock face preserves thousands of dinosaur tracks. The park's **Dino Truck** leaves from **Plaza 25 de Mayo** for the site outside the city.

### El Chaltén as an overnight base

Stay in **El Chaltén** for the **Laguna Torre** and **Laguna de los Tres** trails, both starting from town. Laguna Torre is around **18 km return**, roughly **six to eight hours**; Laguna de los Tres is around **20 km return**, roughly **eight to nine hours**, with a steep final climb below Fitz Roy. In summer, these fill separate hiking days. In winter, snow and ice change access and walking times.
