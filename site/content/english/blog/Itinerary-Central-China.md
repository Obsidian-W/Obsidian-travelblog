## Why this part of China?

The variety. **Shaanxi** gives this route its historical weight: Xi'an, the Terracotta Army, the old walls and a food culture built around wheat, lamb and noodles.

Then the train crosses the Qinling Mountains into **Sichuan**, where Chengdu's teahouses sit within reach of Buddhist mountains and the Tibetan landscapes around Jiuzhaigou, where you will be awed by the fairy pools (Huanglong / "Yellow dragon") and the incredible colors of the Park (see the cover picture!).
On the other side of **Sichuan**, **Chongqing** adds the huge city: towers, bridges, rivers and neighbourhoods spread over hills, with a mix of old and new.
The 8D cyberpunk city might be exaggerated online and overrated, but it packs a punch.

**Hunan** changes the scenery again. Fenghuang follows the river, with its stilt houses; Furong sits above a waterfall and its stone building contrasts wildly with other towns, and Zhangjiajie's pillars rise out of the jungle (The city is nice as well), the architecture changes, and the mix of nature continues impressing visitors. 

And below Hunan, **Tongren** in **Guizhou** is the base for Fanjingshan and is an underrated place in the region, overshadowed by Guilin.

## Days 1–3: Xi'an

### Day 1 - The inner city, including the Muslim quarter

![Courtyards at the Great Mosque in Xi'an](images/blog/shaanxi1.jpg)
^*The courtyards of Xi'an's Great Mosque*

> Alright, so I arrived at noon on my day 1, if you arrive in the evening, please consider that as day 0. Xi'an requires some time!

Stay inside or near the walls (inside is better), around **Zhonglou/Bell Tower** or **Yongningmen**, for evening walks and metro access.

From **Xi'an Xianyang Airport**, take the airport metro connection straight into the city, it's fast and efficient (And very cheap).
Allow **1–1:30** after leaving the terminal, enjoy the views of the city developing.
Also, see how cities are planned, here the subway is planned long before the city, so you have stops to random places in the country side.

The first stop I recommend is the city center with the Drum tower, a big roundabout that lights up at night (So you have to visit twice), it's one of the city's main landmark.
From there, just go west, see the bell tower then even more west to head to the **Muslim Quarter**, with noodle shops, grilled meat and sweet stalls it's a nice place to explore and get food, but it can get crowded. The **Great Mosque** has courtyards and Chinese-style pavilions within the neighbourhood;
The entrance is free for Muslims, alhamdulillah.

Then head even more west towards the closest city wall. Yup, we going up.
**Xi'an City walls** are beautiful, the gates even more so. You can rent a bike on it to go around, I definitely recommend doing that.
Once up, go to the north west corner to see the beautiful Tibetan temple. Then you continue around the wall as much as you can. Near the northern gate, you have nice viewpoints of the city, near the eastern gate, there is a pretty temple/guild thing.
All around the walls are some expo/museums, not worth much of a stop.

At night, because sun set rather early, you can see the same areas near the center this time beautifully illuminated.

Do the first day this way and you get a rather chill arrival day. You could technically rush it after a day arrival, but any thing you didn't do can be moved on day 2 or 3 worry-free, Xi'an is not that big.

### Day 2 - Terracotta Army

![Terracotta Army in Xi'an](images/blog/shaanxi2.jpg)
^*The Terracotta Army*

> What's the main point of interest of Xi'an ? The **Terracotta Army**, out in **Lintong**.
> Not to be mixed up with the delicious Panna cotta army.

**Pit 1** contains the largest formation of infantry and horses. **Pit 2** brings together different military units, including archers and cavalry, while **Pit 3** is interpreted as a command post. Allow **3 or 4 hours** for the exhibition halls and pits, plus the journey.
It can also be done as a tour, which I recommend, it's easier.

For public transport, take the metro to **Huaqingchi on Line 9**, then the connecting local bus to the museum. Allow roughly **1:30–2 hours each way** from central Xi'an and **15–20 RMB (1,90–2,50€)** each way. A direct tourist coach is another option, listed around **30 RMB (3,75€)** each way.

For a guide, this [Terracotta Army group tour — affiliate link](https://www.getyourguide.com/xi-an-l270/xi-an-exclusive-terracotta-army-tour-with-local-family-lunch-t905081/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher) is the tour I did.
With a really nice lunch too.

I really like the Terracotta army despite the crowds, it was just like I imagined it to be, until the exit, where it's some kind of Disneyland full of tourist shops. Meh.

I returned in town around 3:30, even if you do it by yourself, you will probably be back before 4.
This mean time for an evening activity.

I recommend going to the Wild Goose Pagoda and then Tang Paradise. They are next to each others and are iconic.
Big day, great activities.

> Tang Paradise is supposed to have a lot of performances, when I went, it was dead quiet. I was a bit disappointed, but I liked the night views.
> Of course everything inside is more expensive and, IIRC, some performances are additional fees. The area around it is actually quite nice (and lively) and worth the trip.

### Day 3 - Outside the walls

![Xi'an Bell Tower illuminated at night](images/blog/shaanxi3.jpg)
^*Xi'an's Bell Tower at night*

Spend day 3 outside the **city walls**. Essentially a park day. Nature within the city and some pagodas. It's nice to see another side of a city.

Start northwest at **Hanchenghu / Hancheng Lake**, for a walk along the water and the Han-themed park. Then head east to **Daming Palace National Heritage Park**, north of the city walls. Allow roughly **1 and a half hour** at the lake and **2 hours** for the palace park.

DiDi are the best way to quickly go to Daming, but you can also take the subway back, desptie it being quite long due to how uncomfortable that part is
(Rather long way to the the subway, then Loop line into another one...).

**Daming Palace** was the Tang emperors' royal palace. Today, the visit centres on the archaeological site: **Danfeng Gate**, the foundations of **Hanyuan Hall**, the palace museum and **Taiye Pond**. The grounds are huge, with open lawns between the remains. The museum is a nice quick visit.
Honest review: It's a bit expensive for what it is. I would rather have just seen the Daming gate, the park where people fly kites, the view from far and then to the next destination right away.

For the afternoon, continue northeast to **Xi'an Expo Park**, the former horticultural exhibition grounds. Walk around **Jinxiu Lake**, the themed gardens and **Chang'an Tower**. Budget another **2 hours** for a walk.
I liked how empty and quiet it was. Almost eerie, totally different from the inner parts of Xi'an.

For more, add **Small Wild Goose Pagoda** and **Xi'an Museum**, which share a site south of the walls. (This is a separate pagoda from the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda, but it looks similar). I didn't go because I didn't know about the place, despite largely having the time for it...

## Day 4: Chengdu

![Temple courtyard and pagoda in Chengdu](images/blog/chengdu1.jpg)
^*The Wenshu monastery, it's almost empty*

Book **Xi'an North / Xi'anbei → Chengdu East / Chengdudong**. Trains take roughly **3:30**. Budget **220 RMB (27€)** in second class.
Try departing early, or leaving late the previous day.
[Rail route and fare range](https://www.travelchinaguide.com/china-trains/high-speed/xian-chengdu.htm).

After dropping your luggage, start at **Wenshu Monastery / Wenshu Yuan**, then **People's Park**, followed **Kuanzhai / Wide and Narrow Alleys**.
Entry to all three is free.

**Wenshu** has temple courtyards, gardens and teahouses, it's really quiet in the evening (More centered around tea and calligraphy), I loved it.
It's calm, colorful, surprisingly tidy... 
Not far, you can find the main square of Chengdu, with all the high rise buildings and Mao Zedong's statue, worth a quick stop, essentialy to catch line 1.

At **People's Park**, the main stop is a cup of tea at **Heming Teahouse**, with outdoor seating around the park. **Kuanzhai** combines restored courtyard buildings with shops and restaurants (But it's overly touristy).

You can also see the Wuhou shrine, if you're not tired of temples. But honestly, just walking around the lively districts of Chengdu is part of the city experience.

I'm not the biggest fan of Chengdu as a tourist, feels like a city where you want to live in, but not for visiting, weird isn't it?

## Day 5: Leshan side trip

![Leshan Giant Buddha carved into the cliff](images/blog/leshan.jpg)
^*The Giant Buddha of Leshan, carved into the cliff*

### Getting to the Buddha

Take a morning train from **Chengdu East to Leshan**, usually around **45 minutes–1:15**. Budget **50–80 RMB (6,25–10€)** in second class. Allow another **45–60 minutes** by public bus, with **5–10 RMB (0,60–1,25€)** set aside for local rides. Bus 3 connects Leshan station with **Leshan Giant Buddha / 乐山大佛**.

On foot, you see the head, temples and cliff paths; The boat gives a frontal view from the river, but depends on river conditions. Published prices are **80 RMB (10€)** for land admission and **70 RMB (8,75€)** for the boat, separately.

The sleeping buddha is an additional fee, but it's dumb, because you see it from the road, it's gigantic. Otherwise, if you head inside, you can see some recent buddhas and have another path to the Giant Buddha of Leshan.

## Day 6: Emei

![Anshun Bridge illuminated at night in Chengdu](images/blog/chengdu2.jpg)
^*Anshun Bridge lit up at night*

> Other travellers and my Chinese friends recommended **Emei Shan**. I couldn't do it because I had slightly less than three weeks. This is the extra day I'd add after Leshan.
> So, this is not very "My trip" but I searched all the info for you, dear reader!

Spend the day on **Emei Shan**, with the **Golden Summit** as the main stop. At about **3 077 m**, the summit brings together the enormous **golden Samantabhadra statue**, **Huazang Temple** and viewpoints over the surrounding mountains.
Walk around the statue, visit the temple halls, then follow the viewing terraces along the cliffs.
On a clear day, the ridges stretch into the distance; cloud can sit below the summit or swallow the views entirely. (yay, another foggy place! Wait I spoiled you the rest of the article!!)

Start early from the **Baoguo/Huangwan** area. The mountain bus climbs through the forest to **Leidongping**, taking roughly **2 hours**. From there, walk uphill for **20–40 minutes** to **Jieyin Hall**, then take the cable car to the summit. Allow roughly **1–2 hours at the top** as a planning estimate, before returning to Leidongping for lunch and the bus down. [Summit route and day plan](https://www.chinahighlights.com/leshan/attraction/mount-emei.htm).

For forest trails and smaller temples instead, choose **Wannian Temple and Qingyin Pavilion** as an alternative to the summit. Combining both routes needs another day.

Stay near the mountain entrance after Leshan: the **Leshan → Emeishan** train takes roughly **15–25 minutes**, followed by a local bus to Baoguo/Huangwan. After the mountain visit, return to **Chengdu** by train, allowing roughly **1–2 hours** and an estimated **35–80 RMB (4,40–10€)** for the rail leg. [Rail connections](https://www.travelchinaguide.com/china-trains/high-speed/chengdu-leshan-emeishan.htm).

**October 2026 summit costs:** **160 RMB (20€)** admission, **90 RMB (11,25€)** for the return mountain bus and **120 RMB (15€)** for the return Golden Summit cable car: **370 RMB (46,25€)** in total, before meals and trains. [Official tariffs](https://www.leshan.gov.cn/lsswszf/zzzqgsgg/760644150124613.html). Renovation work is limiting summit admission and transport capacity; reserve the summit entry, bus and cable car ahead. [Official access notice](https://www.leshan.gov.cn/lsswszf/zzzqgsgg/808058894643269.html).

### Late afternoon at the Panda Base

Add the **Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding** if you can reach its entrance around **15:30–16:00**.
Apparently some Emei tours return a bit too late, some do include the Panda Base afterwards, but they start early.
For an early start, you can stay around Leshan.

Walk between the panda enclosures and bamboo-lined paths; the optional sightseeing bus covers the longer stretches. Admission is **55 RMB (6,90€)**, plus **30 RMB (3,75€)** for that bus. ### Day 7: Huanglong and on to Jiuzhaigou

Take the cable car up and walk towards the **Five Colour Pool**.
This is where you get the view of turquoise pools stacked down the mountainside, with the forest behind them.
Walk past Huanglong Temple, then follow the boardwalk downhill, along the smaller pools, terraces and waterfalls, to the Welcoming Pools near the entrance.
The way down is easy and straightforward.

Count around 4 hours for the visit.
Note: The upper pools are at about 3500m, and even with the cable car, there's some uphill walking. My friend wasn't well at this altitude.

Entry costs 170 RMB (21,25€), plus 80 RMB (10€) for the cable car up: 250 RMB (31,25€) together.

Afterwards, take the tourist coach to **Jiuzhaigou**, roughly 2:30–3 hours away. (Or go back with your tour, you lazy ass SoB)
Budget around 60–100 RMB (7,50–12,50€) for this leg.

Stay near the park entrance for the next two nights! With my tour, we had very high quality hotel rooms. Really 120€ for three days including trains, accomodation, entrance tickets and a guide. Wow! Awesome value.

### Day 8: Jiuzhaigou National Park

![Jiuzhaigou](images/blog/jiuzhaigou3.jpg)
^ Beside a clear blue lake in Jiuzhaigou, rare pic of me, unedited, as you can see I'm fugly on it

A full day for the lakes, waterfalls and the general area!
There are three valleys to explore, with buses between them and boardwalks for the walks along the water.

Start with **Rize Valley**, **Five Flower Lake** and **Pearl Shoal Waterfall**.
The water is clear enough to see the fallen trees below the surface. Walk between the lakes and the waterfall, taking the bus for the longer stretches.

Then head to **Long Lake** in Zechawa Valley, and walk down to the much smaller **Five Colour Pond**.
Return to Nuorilang for lunch and the waterfall nearby.
The morning bus may take you to Long Lake first; in that case, do these two valleys in the opposite order.

Keep **Shuzheng Valley** for the way back: Rhinoceros Lake, Shuzheng Waterfall, the lakes and the village.
Walk the lakeside paths, stop in Shuzheng Village, then take the bus back to the entrance.

The [park's route guide](https://www.jiuzhai.com/intelligent-service/way-of-play) shows the three valleys.

Entry is 190 RMB (23,75€), plus 90 RMB (11,25€) for the bus (well needed): 280 RMB (35€) for the day.
From 16 November to 31 March, the total drops to 160 RMB (20€).
Book with your passport and bring it with you, they use it at the gate.
[Official tickets](https://www.jiuzhai.com/intelligent-service/tickets).

> Note that you should bring lunch, food options aren't great.

## Day 10: Central Chongqing and the riverfront

![Hongyadong and Qiansimen Bridge at night in Chongqing](images/blog/chongqing1.jpg)
^*Hongyadong and Qiansimen Bridge at night*

Alright folks, today's walk makes a loop through the centre, out to Chaotianmen and back along the river to Hongyadong after dark.

> Be prepared, for a lot of stairs, you gonna climb. Up and down. Again and again, welcome to Chongqing!

Start at **Jiefangbei Pedestrian Street**, around the Liberation Monument. This is the Times Square side of Chongqing: huge shopping centres, screens and towers above a busy pedestrian district.
Walk around the monument and the shopping streets, with a stop at nearby **Bayi food street** for breakfast or snacks.
Personally, I found Bayi far too loud and busy. 

Note: **Linjiangmen, on Line 2**, is a convenient metro stop for the start.

Then walk northwest to **Kuixinglou / Kuixing Building**.
Cross the raised walkways and look down: what feels like ground level is several floors above the streets below. There are views towards the Jialing River and the buildings stacked along the hillside. 
Head east along the upper streets to the **Chongqing Art Museum at Guotai Arts Center**, the red building made of interlocking beams near Hongyadong.
Stop for the building itself and the exhibitions inside. Entry is free; it opens 9:00–17:00, with last entry at 16:30, and normally closes on Mondays. 

> I didn't go inside, but this could be nice for a rainy day

Continue east towards Xiaoshizi and **Luohan Temple**.
Its courtyards and Buddhist halls sit between the high-rises, with rock carvings along the entrance passage and a hall full of clay arhats, each with a different expression. Give it time for a proper walk through the halls.
The entrance fee is cheap but the exterior parts are the best, I really liked seeing all the Taoist cosplays around.

From there, head downhill towards **Huguang Guild Hall**, near Dongshuimen on the Yangtze side. (There is an elevator for free, or long steps around it, the view from above is much better from what's below)
Explore the yellow-walled courtyards, carved wooden opera stages and **Yuwang Palace**, the temple dedicated to Yu the Great. The complex tells the story of the migrants and merchant communities who settled in Chongqing. It's quite too expensive for what it is, but you do you.

After lunch, follow the Yangtze riverfront towards **Chaotianmen and Raffles City**.
The towers stand at the tip of the peninsula, where the Yangtze and Jialing rivers meet. Walk out to the square and river viewpoints;
You can also head all the way up, if you got the money!

The view is splendid, the Raffles City is a trip within itself, with so many colourful shops. Perfect for gifts.

Round the tip and follow the **Jialing riverfront west to Hongyadong**.
This brings you to the lower levels of the hillside complex. Walk through its stairs, balconies and shops, then come back out for the illuminated buildings after dark.
Finish on the pedestrian side of **Qiansimen Bridge** for the view back at Hongyadong. Cross farther towards **Jiangbeizui** if you want the skyline from the opposite bank.

> Walking around Chongqing in the dark is something else too. Well "In the dark" not really, since everything is so bright!

I slept in a Lavande Hotel, rather cheap and of very nice quality.

## Day 11: Liziba, Ciqikou and Shibati

![Traditional-style buildings on a Chongqing street](images/blog/chongqing2.jpg)
^*The Luohan Temple amidst modern buildings*

Start with **Shibati**, then visit the **People's Auditorium**, **Liziba** and finally **Ciqikou**.
The first stop is near the centre; the rest follow the metro west across the city.

Walk to **Shibati / Eighteen Steps** from **Jiaochangkou station, on Lines 1 and 2**.
Follow the stone steps down through the restored lanes, stopping in the smaller side streets between the shops and teahouses. The change in level is part of the visit: the stairs link the upper city to the lower neighbourhood.
Loop back towards the upper entrance and Jiaochangkou when you're done.

Take **Line 2 from Jiaochangkou to Zengjiayan**, then walk to **People's Square and the People's Auditorium / Great Hall of the People**.
The auditorium's green-tiled dome and red colonnades face the square, with a design inspired by Beijing's Temple of Heaven. Walk across the square for the full view of the building. Also, you can head inside for 10 RMB, it's a quick visit inside, to see the actual auditorium.

The **Three Gorges Museum** is opposite, if you want to add a museum visit, I didn't do it, seemed boring.

Return to **Zengjiayan** and continue on **Line 2 towards Yudong**, getting off at **Liziba**.

Step down from the train, go down where everyone is screaming with their mouths open (yes everyone is pretending the train is going in their mouth, or their ass, which is much funnier.) and enjoy this famous station.
You can also have a different view from the inside of the station, with my friend, we waited inside for some time to catch it.

Back on **Line 2**, continue to **Daping** and change to **Line 1 towards Bishan**. Get off at **Ciqikou** and walk into the old town.
Allow roughly 45 minutes for this leg, including the change and walk, as a planning estimate.

Spend the afternoon in **Ciqikou**: the main shopping lane, the narrower streets branching off it, teahouses and the Jialing waterfront.
Head towards **Baolun Temple** for its halls and stone steps, then work your way back through the lanes. There are plenty of places for a late lunch or an early dinner (But street vendors make you taste food, so you can just walk around and get food that way)

Take **Line 1 back to Jiaochangkou** for the evening in the centre.
For Guanyinqiao's shopping streets instead, change at **Lianglukou to Line 3 towards Jiangbei Airport** and get off at **Guanyinqiao**.
I went to Guanyinqiao, it's nice for people watching, but not much more, far too crowded to my liking.

## Day 12: Wulong day trip

![Courtyard beneath the cliffs at Wulong](images/blog/wulong.jpg)
^*The Tianmen Post below the cliffs of Wulong*

Visit **Three Natural Bridges**, **Longshuixia Fissure Gorge** and **Fairy Mountain**.
Either by yourself or by tour. My tour included all three.; if going by yourself, drop the fairy mountain, it's not worth it.

Here are some researches about going there alone:

### Mount Fanjing / Fanjingshan

Use the **East Gate in Jiangkou** for the standard sightseeing-bus and cable-car route. From Tongren, allow around **1:30–2 hours** by scheduled tourist coach and **60 RMB (7,5€)** each way. They start from the Tourist bus station, opposite of the train station (And a bit farther, it's really the opposite road).

#### Booking park admission

Book a **dated East Gate entry slot**, with the **sightseeing bus and return cable car**, through an authorised booking platform. No other way work for foreigners, Chinese national use a WeChat program for it.

(Note that the **West Gate in Yinjiang** is a different approach, based around a longer hike and with a bus that is less common to find)

Reserve as soon as your date becomes available in the booking calendar, particularly for weekends and holidays. Ideally one week in advance if it's a busy season. Tickets are released in three waves (7 days - 3 days - Same day), with 9000 total tickets available for any day (+ 1000 for west route)

East Gate admission is **100 RMB (12,50€)**. Budget **20 RMB (2,50€)** for the internal bus and **140 RMB (17,50€)** for the return cable car: approximately **260 RMB (32,50€)** before travel and food.

#### Booking the climb to Red Clouds Golden Summit

**Red Clouds Golden Summit / 红云金顶 has a second, free reservation on the mountain.** This is a numbered queue for the final climb, separate from the dated ticket used to enter the park. The park assigns numbers in reservation order; they are valid that day, and a missed number requires a new reservation. [Official summit reservation rules](https://fanjingshan.gz.cn/?p=8846).

After the cable car and the walk towards **Pudu Square**, look for the summit-reservation signs and staff. **Foreign visitors should ask a park ranger or member of staff to help register for the summit queue.** Show your passport and park ticket, and ask for **Red Clouds Golden Summit reservation**. Foreigners have no proper way to book it like locals, so you need to cheat a bit. Shitty system means that you can take advantage of it. There won't be many foreigners anyway.

--- Actual visit ---

From the East Gate, the sightseeing bus takes you to the cable-car station. Above the cable car, a stepped forest path leads into the upper sightseeing area. Allow **5–6 hours** for the bus, cable car, viewpoints and summit climbs; the Red Clouds queue can extend that.
The walk up is quite long and not always feasible, when I went, the weather was dreadful, heavy rains made the ascent over the thousands of stairs difficult.

**Red Clouds Golden Summit** is the split rock pinnacle with **two temples linked by a small bridge**. Its final ascent follows steep, narrow steps and sections with handrails or chains, with an organised flow of visitors. **Mushroom Stone** is the broad rock balanced above a narrow base. **Old Golden Summit / 老金顶** is a separate climb, with views back towards the temple-topped pinnacle and surrounding ridges.

Wind, thunderstorms, ice or crowd controls can close the final summit section even while the wider park remains open. Check the last downhill cable-car time with staff on arrival and leave enough time for the stepped path back to its upper station.

### Cableways, escalators and tickets

**October 2026 update:** the long cableway from downtown closed for renovation on **13 October**, suspending **Routes A and B**. **Route C**, using the **Tianmen Cave Express Cableway** in both directions, remains the operating route. No reopening date has been confirmed. [Cableway closure and route changes](https://www.zhangjiajieguide.com/travel-notices/tianmen-mountain-cableway-suspension.html).
When I went, it was still working, but a lot of construction was starting to happen.

The current journey is:

1. Take the scenic shuttle from the downtown cableway station to the **Mountain Gate**.
2. Ride the **Express Cableway** uphill, then follow the connecting path to the square beneath Heaven's Gate.
3. Climb the **999 steps**, or use the lower escalator, to reach the cave.
4. Take the **seven-stage through-mountain escalators** above the cave to the summit.
5. Walk the summit circuits, then return through the escalators and Express Cableway.

Book a dated entry slot with your passport. The standard adult package costs **288 RMB (36€)**, including the return Express Cableway and upper escalators. ## Practical notes and total budget

**Trains:** use [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/index.html) for the actual date, station and fare. Stations along the route: **Xi'an North, Chengdu East, Huanglongjiuzhai, Tongren versus Tongren South, Fenghuanggucheng, Furongzhen and Zhangjiajie West**. Rail budget estimates above don't include booking-agent fees. Carry the passport used for the reservation, it's used as a ticket.
You can also book with Trip.com, but you have to pay a fee for every booking.

**Sleeping:** for ordinary dates, budget **150–200 RMB (18–24€)** for a simple private room, or **50–100 RMB (6–12€)** for a hostel bed where available. Golden Week can push them much higher, particularly around Fenghuang and Wulingyuan.

**Food:** allow **15–40 RMB (1,8–5€)** per person per meal for simple meals with simple drinks.
I ate for around 60 RMB a day (7.5€) including snacks and drinks.

The [food guide](/blog/food-central-china/) breaks down the choices.

For the full **20 days in China**, budget **8 000–12 000 RMB (1 000–1 500€)**, excluding international flights, shopping and privately guided tours. That assumes two people sharing simple rooms, public transport, the main paid sights, Emei, and the conditional panda visit.

## With more days

### One more day in Chengdu

Give the **Panda Base** its own morning, then spend the afternoon around **Wuhou Shrine and Jinli** or return to the teahouses. The Panda Base costs **55 RMB (6,90€)**, plus **30 RMB (3,75€)** for its optional internal bus.

### Sanxingdui: one day from Chengdu

**Sanxingdui Museum**, near Guanghan, displays the large bronze masks, standing figures, gold objects and ritual bronzes excavated from the ancient Shu sites.

Take a train from **Chengdu East to Guanghan North**, around **30 minutes**, then **bus 13** to the museum. Direct tourist coaches also leave from central Chengdu. Admission is **72 RMB (9€)**; allow **150–220 RMB (18,75–27,50€)** for the outing with public transport and lunch, excluding accommodation. Reserve a dated museum ticket in advance. [Museum access and admission](https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/sichuan/chengdu/sanxingdui.htm).

### Dujiangyan: one day from Chengdu

**Dujiangyan** combines the ancient irrigation works, river channels, suspension bridges and **Erwang Temple**.

Take the metro to **Xipu**, then a train to **Lidui Park / Liduigongyuan**, about **20–30 minutes** for the rail leg. The scenic-area entrance is roughly **10 minutes on foot** from the station. Admission is **80 RMB (10€)**; trains are listed from **10 RMB (1,25€)** each way. Budget **130–180 RMB (16,25–22,50€)** with local transport and lunch. [Dujiangyan access and prices](https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/sichuan/chengdu/dujiangyan.htm).

### Dazu: one day from Chongqing

At the **Dazu Rock Carvings**, **Baodingshan** has Buddhist scenes carved along the cliffs, a reclining Buddha and the Thousand-Armed Guanyin. **Beishan** is a separate site with more carvings.

Take a coach to **Dazu town**, around **2 hours**, then a local bus to Baodingshan. Allow a full day from Chongqing. Admission is **115 RMB (14,40€)** for Baodingshan or **140 RMB (17,50€)** for the Baodingshan–Beishan combination; budget approximately **230–350 RMB (28,75–43,75€)** for a public-transport day with lunch, depending on the sites and local connections. [Dazu access and tickets](https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/chongqing/dazu.htm).

With **one more day at Wulingyuan**, spread the upper viewpoints across two days and give **Golden Whip Stream** a separate day. **Yangjiajie** adds its long sandstone walls to the pillar landscapes around Yuanjiajie and Tianzi Mountain.
