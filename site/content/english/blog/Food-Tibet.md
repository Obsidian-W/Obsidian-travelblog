## Barley, dairy and meat

![Tibetan butter tea](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Tibetan%20butter%20tea.jpg)

Roasted barley, butter tea, dumplings, noodle soups and yak or mutton form much of the Tibetan menu. In Lhasa, these sit alongside Sichuan dishes and other Chinese cooking; the smaller towns on the Everest road have a narrower choice.

## Lhasa tea houses

**Po cha**, or butter tea, is tea blended with butter and salt. It tastes savoury, almost like a light soup, and has a rich, oily texture. Tea houses serve it by the cup or flask.

**Sweet tea** is quite different: black tea with milk and sugar, closer to South Asian milk tea. Both are served alongside simple meals such as dumplings and noodle soup.

## Tsampa

**Tsampa** is roasted barley flour, mixed with butter tea and kneaded in the bowl into a dense dough. The flavour is toasted and nutty; sugar or dried cheese can also be added.

## Noodles, dumplings and simple meals

- **Thukpa** is noodle soup with vegetables, meat or both.
- **Thenthuk** has pieces of dough pulled and torn directly into the broth, making thicker, irregular noodles.
- **Momos** are filled dumplings, steamed or fried, with meat or vegetable fillings.
- **Tingmo** is soft, coiled steamed bread without a filling, served with stew.

## Yak, mutton and dairy

Yak, beef and mutton appear in hotpots, stews and dumplings. Dried yak meat is sold in strips and can be very firm and chewy.

Tibetan yoghurt is thick and sharply sour, often served with sugar. Butter and dried cheese also feature in the food, as well as in tea and tsampa.
