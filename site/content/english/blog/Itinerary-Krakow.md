## Day 1: Krakow

![Central Krakow](images/blog/krakow3.jpg)

Walk from the **Barbican** through **St Florian's Gate** and Floriańska Street to **Rynek Główny**. **St Mary's Basilica** faces the square, with its unequal towers and the carved wooden altarpiece inside. The **Cloth Hall** runs across the middle of the square, with shops beneath the arcades. Cross the square to Grodzka and follow the old royal route past the churches of Saints Peter and Paul and Saint Andrew to **Wawel Castle**.

Spend the afternoon on **Wawel Hill**. Visit the cathedral, then the castle's arcaded courtyard and one of its interior collections. The **State Rooms** have royal tapestries and decorated ceilings; the **Crown Treasury** contains the coronation sword **Szczerbiec**. These are separate exhibitions in the castle's ticket system.

Descend towards the dragon statue and follow the Vistula embankment before returning through **Planty**, the park around the Old Town.

## Day 2: Krakow

Start in **Kazimierz** at **Szeroka Street**, between the **Old Synagogue** and **Remuh Synagogue**. The Old Synagogue houses displays about Jewish life and religious traditions; Remuh has a small historic cemetery behind it. Continue through **Józefa Street** and **Plac Nowy**, then walk south past **Corpus Christi Church** to the river.

Cross the **Father Bernatek footbridge** into **Podgórze**. Walk from **St Joseph's Church** to **Ghetto Heroes Square**, where the empty-chair memorial stands on the former ghetto's assembly square. The **Eagle Pharmacy** faces the square and has an exhibition about the ghetto.

Continue to **Oskar Schindler's Factory** on Lipowa Street. Its main exhibition covers **Krakow under German occupation**, with reconstructed interiors, photographs and testimony. Allow around two hours inside. Return to Kazimierz for the evening.

## Day 3: Auschwitz

![Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial](images/blog/krakow2.jpg)

Take a morning train from **Kraków Główny** to **Oświęcim**. The train takes around **1:05–1:30**. The memorial entrance at 55 Więźniów Oświęcimia Street is about 20 minutes on foot from the railway station; local buses also stop outside it.

Reserve the named entry pass through [the official Auschwitz-Birkenau booking site](https://visit.auschwitz.org/) before the trip. The standard guided visit lasts about **3:30** and covers both **Auschwitz I** and **Auschwitz II-Birkenau**. Auschwitz I contains the museum exhibitions in the former prison blocks. Birkenau is the much larger site, with the railway entrance, surviving barracks and remains of the crematoria. A free museum shuttle links the two sites. Bags larger than 35 × 25 × 15 cm are not admitted.

Return to Krakow by train or direct bus after the visit.

## Day 4: Wieliczka

![Wieliczka Salt Mine](images/blog/krakow1.jpg)


Take the **SKA1 suburban train** from **Kraków Główny** to **Wieliczka Rynek-Kopalnia**, roughly **0:25**. The Daniłowicz Shaft entrance is approximately ten minutes on foot from the terminus. [Wieliczka visits with transport from Krakow are also available (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/wieliczka-salt-mine-l4139/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

Book the Tourist Route in the required language through [the mine's official site](https://www.wieliczka-saltmine.com/individual-tourist/tourist-route). The guided visit lasts **two to three hours** and includes **St Kinga's Chapel**, whose reliefs, statues and chandeliers were made using rock salt. The underground route also passes chambers, mining equipment and saline lakes. The route descends about 380 steps, contains more than 800 steps in total and returns to the surface by lift.

Return to **Kraków Główny** on the same suburban train after the mine.

## With more days

### Rynek Underground and the Czartoryski Museum

Go underground beneath the Main Square at **Rynek Underground**, where excavated medieval streets sit below the modern market, then visit the **Czartoryski Museum** for Leonardo da Vinci's *Lady with an Ermine*.

### A longer Wieliczka afternoon

After the mine, walk to the **Saltworks Castle**, the former headquarters of the salt administration. Its courtyards and museum collections continue the salt story above ground, including an extensive collection of decorated saltcellars. The town square is nearby, and the return train leaves from **Wieliczka Rynek-Kopalnia**.
