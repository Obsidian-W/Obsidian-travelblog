A short trip into Nepal, mostly around Kathmandu. This itinerary is ideal for anyone wishing for a short stay in nepal, wether it's transit or visiting from a close country (Or good when combined with Northern India/Tibet, like I did).
I hope that anyone doing the Tibet Crossing and heading to India can find value in this trip.
I'll try to include useful border and transport info, since Lumbini and Sonauli were poorly documented online. 

> I entered Nepal from Tibet at Gyirong in July 2024. Five days later I crossed into India at Sonauli, it was a transit country for me, but I included an itinerary for anyone wishing to spend less than a week there.

{{< worth-o-meter score="8" >}}
Hard to rate Nepal at it was a short stay around Kathmandu.
But it's a pretty country, with a unique architectural style, a rather unique blend of culture and ethnicities (Shared, in a way, with some Indian states, but less opressive for women).
Kathmandu's valley is packed, the backdrop is phenomenal, and the softer monsoon season (Compared to the rest of the country) makes it easy to visit even during the summer.

## Day 1: Kathmandu

![The old streets of Kathmandu](images/blog/nepal.jpg)

*Preface: My first day was actually spent going from Gyirong to Kathmandu, which I count as day 0 here, most of the day was spent on the road, stopping along viewpoints near Langtang National Park.*
*(The minibus driver (Carrying mostly tourists) stopped at every scenic spot for 10 minutes or so, sometimes stopping twice in a hour span, this was quite great as driving muddy mountain roads is exhausting beyond beliefs and it's much safer for the driver to stop and rest often during those times.)*

Spend the morning around **Kathmandu Durbar Square**. Start at **Kasthamandap**, then cross Basantapur towards **Kumari Ghar**, whose courtyard has finely carved wooden windows. Continue past the Shiva-Parvati and Jagannath temples to the large **Kal Bhairav** figure. **Taleju Temple** rises above the square, although its interior only opens during Dashain.

Go inside **Hanuman Dhoka Palace** for **Nasal Chowk** and the museum galleries about Nepal's monarchy.

After lunch, walk north through **Indra Chowk** to **Asan Bazaar**. The lanes between them are full of textile shops, brassware and food stalls; Asan's Annapurna Temple stands above the junction of the market streets. Continue through the smaller courtyards towards **Thamel**.

Finish at the **Garden of Dreams**, just east of Thamel, for its ponds, pavilions and enclosed gardens, then return through Thamel's restaurant streets for dinner.

## Day 2: Bhaktapur

![A brick courtyard in the Kathmandu Valley](images/blog/nepal2.jpg)

Take a Bhaktapur-bound local bus from **Bagbazar**, east of Ratna Park in central Kathmandu. The journey takes around **an hour**.

Walk from **Bhaktapur Durbar Square** to **Taumadhi Square**, the five-storey Nyatapola Temple, the pottery area and **Dattatreya Square**. Around Durbar Square, the **Golden Gate** opens towards the royal palace courtyards.

Taumadhi's Nyatapola stands above a steep stairway guarded by pairs of stone figures; the lanes towards Dattatreya pass carved wooden windows and small shrines.

Not far from Bhaktapur, there is a nice valley popular for walks, I did it with a guide, same as the city (valley in the morning and the city in the afternoon), it fills the time with a chance to see some wildlife. And weed.
Bhaktapur's valley is seemingly a stoner's paradise, not my thing, but a fun sight actually.

> Drugs are bad, kids.

For the following morning's **Nagarkot** sunrise, take the local bus from **Kamalbinayak**, northeast of Bhaktapur's old town, and spend the night on the ridge. The bus up to Nagarkot takes roughly **an hour**.

## Day 3: Kathmandu and Nagarkot

![Hills and fields in the Kathmandu Valley](images/blog/nepal4.jpg)

Walk to a Nagarkot viewpoint before sunrise, then take the local bus back through Bhaktapur to Kathmandu. On a clear morning, the Himalayan panorama appears beyond the Kathmandu Valley.

Allow **two to three hours** for the two buses back to Kathmandu, including the change at Kamalbinayak. For a sunrise outing that starts and ends in Kathmandu, [this Nagarkot excursion includes return transport and an optional morning hike (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/kathmandu-l101/nagarkot-sunrise-view-day-hike-with-lunch-privategroup-t488678/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

In Kathmandu, visit **Boudhanath** and follow the prayer-wheel circuit around the enormous white stupa. The surrounding square has monasteries, shops and rooftop restaurants looking towards the painted eyes and gilded spire.

Walk about two kilometres southwest to **Pashupatinath**. The temple complex extends along both sides of the Bagmati River, with stone shrines above the eastern bank and steps descending to the cremation ghats. Non-Hindus cannot enter the main temple; the paths and viewpoints across the river form the visit.

## Day 4: Patan

![Temples in the Kathmandu Valley](images/blog/nepal3.jpg)

From **Ratna Park bus stop**, take a microbus marked **Patan Dhoka**. From **Patan Dhoka**, walk through the old town to **Patan Durbar Square**, around a kilometre away.

Visit the royal square, including the stone **Krishna Mandir**, and **Patan Museum** in the former palace for its Buddhist and Hindu sculpture. Continue through the lanes to the **Golden Temple**, with its metal-clad courtyard, and **Kumbheshwar Temple**. Return to central Kathmandu by the same microbus route and cross to the west side for **Swayambhunath**. The staircase, stupa and viewpoints take around two hours.


## Day 5: Lumbini

![A golden Buddha statue at Lumbini](images/blog/nepal5.jpg)

Take a **morning bus from Kathmandu to Bhairahawa/Siddharthanagar**, the transport hub for Lumbini. Allow roughly **ten to twelve hours including the Lumbini connection**. Long-distance services leave **Gongabu New Bus Park**; tourist-bus departure points depend on the operator.

> Note: When I went in 2024, a lot of people died on one of those bus just weeks before I went. They might be a bit dangerous. I actually took a bus during the day when I saw the news, especially since the road was in a poor state, it was better.
> As a result the trip took much longer than it was advertised. No complaints, people fucking died.

From **Bhairahawa**, take the local bus west to **Lumbini Bazaar**, beside the entrance to the temple area. The 22-kilometre journey takes around **1:00–1:30** by local bus.

> If you cannot find a bus, don't worry, there are plenty of minibus and bajaj willing to go there, it's slightly more expensive, but more convenient.

There are plenty of hostels in Lumbini, so staying is fairly easy.


## Day 6: Lumbini and Sonauli

Visit the **Maya Devi Temple** and the archaeological remains surrounding the marker stone, then walk past the **Ashoka Pillar** and sacred pond. The wider Lumbini site extends far beyond the temple enclosure.
Note: Apparently you can rent a bicycle for the eastern and western monastic zones on either side of the central canal, I went on food as I couldn't find the place to rent a bycyle, but saw many people riding them.

The best part of Lumbini is this monastic zone where you can find temples of all styles, made by different bouddhists associations.
They are pretty but it takes time to visit them all, if you arrive late during the day/afternoon, you might wish for an additional morning to see them.

Also, good to know, but apparently you can stay at the Korean monastery, I didn't as I prefer booking stuff in advance, but I heard good things about it.

Leave for **Sonauli the following morning** if continuing to Varanasi: the journey from Lumbini to Varanasi took almost a whole day on my trip.

Take the local bus from **Lumbini Bazaar** back to **Bhairahawa/Siddharthanagar**, then a southbound local bus to **Belahiya**, the Nepali side of the Sonauli crossing. Complete the exit formalities at the Belahiya immigration office, cross on foot and go through Indian immigration at **Sonauli**. From there, buses run to **Gorakhpur**, with an onward connection to Varanasi in the [northern India itinerary](/blog/itinerary-india/).

## With more days

### Hike farther with a multi day hike from Kathmandu

The **Sundarijal–Chisapani–Nagarkot** walk takes three days on the hills around the Kathmandu Valley. On the first day, travel to **Sundarijal** and climb through Shivapuri's forest and the village of **Mulkharka** to **Chisapani**. Spend the second day walking east along the ridge to **Nagarkot**, then descend towards **Changu Narayan** on the third day and continue to Bhaktapur by bus.

This can replace the direct journey to Bhaktapur and the Nagarkot overnight in Days 2–3. There are lodges at Chisapani and Nagarkot, and the route combines forest, terraced farmland and Himalayan viewpoints.

### Spend time in the Langtang national park

Coming from **Gyirong**, stop at **Syabrubesi** before continuing to Kathmandu. A short detour takes **three days**: walk up the lower **Langtang Khola** valley to **Lama Hotel**, spend the night there, return to Syabrubesi the next day and take the bus to Kathmandu on the third. The trail follows the river through forest, with suspension bridges and small lodges along the way.

The full valley trek continues through **Langtang village** to **Kyanjin Gompa**, below the surrounding peaks. That is a longer addition: allow roughly **a week or more**, including the road journeys from Kathmandu. Langtang trekking requires a park permit and, under the current TIMS rules, a licensed guide and agency-issued TIMS card.


### Visit Pokhara for a couple of days

If my leaves for one more week were approved for this trip, I would have added two days in Pokhara (My initial plan for Nepal included that plus one more day in Lumbini and one transit day (And one more day in Yunnan, two more in India; take that as you will)).

Put **Pokhara between Kathmandu and Lumbini**. Tourist buses leave **Sorhakhutte in Kathmandu** for **Pokhara Tourist Bus Park**, around **eight hours**. Spend two days around the city and lake, then take a morning bus onwards to **Bhairahawa/Lumbini**, around **seven hours**.

Spend one day around **Phewa Lake**: take a boat across to the southern shore, walk up to the **World Peace Pagoda** and return along the lakeside. The hilltop pagoda looks across the lake towards the Annapurna range. Use the second morning for **Sarangkot**, then visit the **International Mountain Museum** and walk through the old bazaar. Mountain views are clearest in the dry seasons; in July, cloud can cover the range.


### More time in Lumbini 

Give the monastic zone its own day by bicycle, separate from Maya Devi Temple. The eastern and western zones are spread along the canal, visiting all the temples takes time, given that the night bus is dangerous, if you take a morning bus to Lumbini, you might require this extra time.

The **Royal Thai Monastery** and **Myanmar Golden Temple** are in the eastern monastic zone; **Zhong Hua Chinese Buddhist Monastery** and the **Korean monastery** lie in the western zone. Continue north along the canal to the **World Peace Pagoda**.
