## Rice noodles, tea and mushrooms around Kunming

![Cross-bridge rice noodles](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Crossing%20the%20Bridge%20Noodle%20at%20Qian%20Xiang%20Yuan.jpg)

Kunming is the easiest place to start with Yunnan food. The dish to try first is **cross-bridge rice noodles**: hot broth, rice noodles and separate toppings added at the table. The official Yunnan tourism site describes it as a Yunnan dish from **Mengzi**, made with soup, meat or fish slices, vegetables and rice noodles.

Kunming is also where you can eat across the province without changing hotels every night. Use it for rice noodles, mushrooms, Dai or Southeast Yunnan restaurants, Pu'er tea shops and bakeries. The useful neighbourhoods are not one single food street: look around busy malls for noodle chains, around markets for breakfast and around university or residential areas for cheaper local restaurants.

| Food | What it is | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-bridge rice noodles | Broth, rice noodles and toppings added separately | Kunming noodle chains and local restaurants |
| Small-pot rice noodles | Rice noodles cooked in a small pot with meat and pickles | Breakfast and lunch noodle shops |
| Flower cakes | Flaky pastry with rose filling | Bakeries and airport/station shops |
| Pu'er tea | Fermented tea from Yunnan | Tea shops and markets |
| Steam-pot chicken | Chicken soup cooked in a ceramic steam pot | Yunnan restaurants, especially in Kunming |
| Wild mushrooms | Seasonal mushrooms, usually stir-fried or in hotpot | Proper restaurants during mushroom season |

Kunming is also a practical place to try Yunnan mushrooms, but be careful with wild mushrooms. Eat them in proper restaurants during mushroom season, not from random sellers.

Do not treat mushroom season casually. Yunnan has a serious wild-mushroom poisoning problem every year. Order from restaurants that know the product and cook it thoroughly.

## Bai food around Dali and Xizhou

![Yunnan goat cheese](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Rubing%20Goats%20Cheese%20of%20Yunnan%20China.jpg)

Dali is good for Bai-style food, simple grills, lake fish and **rubing**, the Yunnan goat cheese usually served fried or grilled. Xizhou is useful for snacks, especially the local stuffed flatbread often sold around the old town area.

| Food | What it is | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rubing | Goat cheese, often fried or grilled | Bai restaurants |
| Xizhou baba | Stuffed flatbread, sweet or savoury | Xizhou snack stalls |
| Erhai fish | Lake fish dishes | Simple restaurants around the lake |
| Bai-style meals | Local vegetable, pork and pickle dishes | Dali old town and villages |
| Rushan | Thin sheets of milk cheese, often grilled | Snack stalls and Bai restaurants |
| Sour-and-spicy fish | Fish cooked with sour pickles or local seasoning | Lake-area restaurants |

Dali has many cafes, but do not let that become the whole food plan. Use cafes for breaks, then eat in local restaurants away from the most obvious old-town streets.

Xizhou is useful because snacks fit naturally into a lake day. Go in the morning or around lunch; late afternoon visits can mean less choice.

## Naxi food around Lijiang

Lijiang's old town is tourist-heavy, so food quality varies. The useful approach is to eat simple: noodles, grilled snacks, Naxi-style dishes and hotpot-style meals. If a restaurant is selling every Chinese regional cuisine at once, skip it.

| Food | What it is | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naxi-style grilled fish | Fish with local seasoning | Restaurants outside the busiest lanes |
| Chickpea jelly | Cold or fried bean-starch dish | Snack shops |
| Rice noodles | Breakfast or quick lunch | Local noodle shops |
| Yak or mushroom hotpot | Shared meal for colder evenings | Restaurants on quieter streets |
| Lijiang baba | Local griddled flatbread | Snack shops and breakfast counters |
| Cured pork rib hotpot | Pork-rib hotpot, often salty and filling | Local restaurants in colder weather |

Baisha and Shuhe can be easier for a slower meal than the busiest part of Lijiang Old Town.

If you are hiking Tiger Leaping Gorge from Lijiang, eat properly before leaving and carry snacks. Guesthouse meals in the gorge are practical, but you should not count on wide choice.

## Tibetan plateau food around Shangri-La

Shangri-La food shifts towards Tibetan plateau food. Expect barley, yak, dairy, butter tea, hotpot and simple noodle soups. It is a good place to try Tibetan food without the logistics of the Tibet Autonomous Region.

| Food or drink | What it is | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yak hotpot | Shared hotpot with yak meat | Good in cold weather |
| Tsampa | Roasted barley flour | Usually mixed with tea or butter tea |
| Butter tea | Salty tea with butter | Try a small cup first |
| Barley products | Bread, alcohol or porridge-style dishes | Common in Tibetan areas |
| Momos | Dumplings with meat or vegetables | Simple restaurants and guesthouses |
| Thukpa | Noodle soup | Reliable on cold or high-altitude days |

Altitude can affect appetite. Eat simple meals on the first evening and drink water rather than planning a heavy food tour immediately after arrival.

## Practical notes

Yunnan food can be spicy, sour and herb-heavy, but it changes by region. Vegetarian travellers can do fairly well in Kunming and Dali, but should still ask about broth and cooking oil. In Tibetan areas, vegetarian options exist but menus are more meat and dairy oriented.

If you cannot read Chinese, use translation apps for menus. Photo menus help, but they also correlate with tourist restaurants, so balance convenience with common sense.

For prices, avoid using old blog figures. Menu inflation and platform pricing vary by city. As a rough current pattern checked against traveller-facing sources in 2026, simple noodle bowls and snacks remain the budget end, mushroom hotpot and yak meals are mid-range or higher, and old-town cafes in Dali or Lijiang can cost more than local restaurants serving better Yunnan food.
