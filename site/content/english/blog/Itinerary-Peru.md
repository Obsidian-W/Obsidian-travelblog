## Day 1: Lima

> I technically arrived the day before, at night. I'll assume you did too, to have a full day.
> With an additional afternoon, you can find more districts to walk to.

Ok, so to start directly. You reached Lima, want to get a feel of the city right away?
Walk the cliffs! It's the prettiest part. 
Additionnaly, you'll be close to **Barranco**, worth it for the street art.
See Barranco’s plaza, walk over the **Bridge of Sighs**, down **Bajada de Baños** and back along the cliff viewpoints. The lanes around the bridge have murals, old houses and small galleries.

After a morning walk along the cliffs, I recommend to continue to the **Plaza Mayor**, with the cathedral and the wooden balconies of the Archbishop’s Palace facing the square. Walk to **San Francisco**, then follow Jirón de la Unión past La Merced to **Plaza San Martín**. This is a roughly **3 km** circuit, allow two hours minimum, perhaps three with stops along the way.

The guided visit inside **San Francisco Monastery** takes about an hour, through the cloisters, religious art and catacombs. Adult admission is 20 soles (5,10€).

> It's worth it, especially for the catacombs!

For the later parts of the evening, I personally went to the archelogical sight:  Pucllana.
It's not open often, but if it does, it will be your first ruin in Peru!

Then, if you don't want to waste time, head for a loop north to the Cordillera Blanca, one of the prettiest area in Peru.

Night buses to **Huaraz** leave Lima between 21:30 and 23:00. The journey takes about eight hours and tickets start at 40 soles (10,30€). Buses depart from La Victoria or Plaza Norte, depending on the operator.

For my previous night, I stayed in Waikiki Hostel in Miraflores, the best district of Lima. It has a rooftop and I received a lot of recommendations from travelers on their way out there.

## Day 2: Huaraz

Keep the first day around **Huaraz**, at roughly 3050 m of altitute, you may need to acclimatize.

> Note: I don't, I discovered I had the genetics for altitude resistance for up to 5000m. But if you follow my itinerary, I'm going to push you to more than 5.2km of altitude.

A note for Huaraz, an earthquake and a landslide destroyed most of it, it used to be a very pretty town. Used to.

Alright, let's visit.

Begin at Plaza de Armas and perhaps the **Regional Archaeological Museum of Áncash** (I didn't visit, i'm a bit tired of museums), then walk through the market streets towards the **La Soledad** neighbourhood.
Once the city seen, go back to the **Plaza de Armas** to head to a small hike in the Cordillera Negra: **Laguna Wilcacocha**.
It is a good acclimatization hike with views across to the **Cordillera Blanca**. Take a **number 10 minibus (colectivo)** from the main road near in Huaraz** to **Puente Santa Cruz in Chiwipampa**.
The hike took me a little less than 3:30 both ways, including a lunch breaks. The path is easy to follow (And you may encounter people preparing the Santa Cruz Trekk here)

**There are lots of dogs along the trail, especially at the top.** 
he ones I encountered weren't as aggresive as the reviews said, just hungry.
I came prepared and had cheese and ham. So I got surrounded by friendly dogs quickly (It's a bit reckless, I guess, but we (dogs and I) had a great time).

> I booked the next three Cordillera Blanca day trips through the hostel for about **10€ each**, returning to the same bed in Huaraz each evening. Convenient.

I stayed at Krusty Hostel B&B, it was cute, nice and well located (And have a variety of room types!)

## Day 3: Pastoruri Glacier

![Pastoruri Glacier in the Cordillera Blanca](images/blog/peru1.jpg)

So, I booked this one through the hostel, as said before. It was super convenient!

> Note: You'll need to buy the admission to the park, an additional fee.
> The current entry fee for **Huascarán National Park** is 30 soles (7,70€) for one day or 60 soles (15,40€) for two to three days for foreign visitors.
> The longer ticket covers both Pastoruri and Laguna 69.

The bus picks you up at your place around **08:30–09:00** and returns to Huaraz around **17:00–18:00**.
(It was a fairly large bus, the town was filled with hikers at this time of the year!), and a fair lot of driving, almost 4 hours spent on the road.

Hence, the excursion went south from Huaraz towards **Cátac**, then into the high plateau below **Pastoruri**. The road stops include the huge **puya raimondii** plants and the mineral springs around **Pumapampa**. Then, you head up to 5000m for an hour long walk around the Glacier. THat's when altitude sickness starts to hit.

## Day 4: Laguna 69

![Laguna 69 in the Cordillera Blanca](images/blog/peru2.jpg)

Hotel pickup is at 04:30–05:00 Return to Huaraz is around 18:00–19:00.
Almost 6 hours of driving in total (Not because it's far, just that the roads are difficult, I recommend sleeping on at least one way)

The trail begins around 3900 m, follows a stream through a broad valley and passes waterfalls before the first sustained climb.
After a flatter basin, the final zigzags reach **Laguna 69**, at about 4600 m, below the ice and rock of Chacraraju.
The complete walk is roughly **14 km**, with around **700 m of ascent**. Expect 5 or 6 hours of hiking, plus some time at the Laguna.

> The multi-day Huascarán ticket from Pastoruri also covers this hike. A separate day ticket costs 30 soles (7,70€).

## Day 5: Laguna Paron

Let's go for the last excursion: **Laguna Parón**, at 4185 m.
Pickup was around **06:00AM** And returns around **6:00PM**

After an almost 3 hours drive, walk along the shore, then climb the rocky path to the **western viewpoint**, an 1.5 hours hike.
From there, the turquoise lake stretches towards the snowy **Pirámide de Garcilaso**.
There are others small hikes around and there were a few stops along the way, but this was my least interesting day in a way, while having the prettiest scenery.

> Since, I don't like wasting time, straight away I continued north for the rest of my small loop.
> If you don't want to see Trujillo, I recommend going back to Lima for an extra day.

You can take a night bus to **Trujillo**, they departs around 10PM and the journey takes around 8 hours, with tickets from 55 soles (14,10€). They all departs from pretty much the same place (where you arrived, normally)

## Day 6: Trujillo

![Historic central Trujillo](images/blog/peru3.jpg)

Spend the morning in the colonial centre: **Plaza de Armas**, the yellow cathedral, **Jirón Pizarro** and the façades and courtyards around **Casa Urquiaga** and **Palacio Iturregui**.
The centre is compact enough for a **2–3 km walking loop**. Its painted houses and balconies makes it a good visit if you like colonial architecture.

In the afternoon, go south to the **Huacas del Sol y de la Luna**. Ideally with a tour, to make transport convenient and have a guide, they are in small groups, can be easily found in the city, even the same day and aren't expensive.

> I like using local operators for tours, and not to cheap out on them, having a guide is a nice thing, and a lot of families depends on this kind of income.

Start with the **Museo Santiago Uceda Castillo**, then join the visit into **Huaca de la Luna**, where excavated temple levels reveal painted reliefs, repeated animal motifs and ceremonial spaces. **Huaca del Sol** is seen from outside. 
It's 2–3 hours for the museum and archaeological visit, plus an hour or so of transport. SO half a day.

Return to Trujillo for the night, well needed after so man night buses (and buses, lol)

## Day 7 & 8: Chan Chan & Huanchaco

Go west from Trujillo to **Chan Chan**, the adobe capital of the Chimú kingdom. Begin with the **site museum** for the model of the city and its ceramics, then continue to the **Nik An palace complex**.
The tall earthen walls hide ceremonial plazas, corridors with fish and bird reliefs, lattice-like friezes and a large water reservoir.

Allow 3–4 hours for the museum, transfer and palace walk.

Take a bus towards **Huanchaco** on **Avenida Mansiche** and get off at **Chan Chan**. The ride takes 20–30 minutes and costs around 3–5 soles (0,80–1,30€).

After Chan Chan, continue to **Huanchaco** for the night. Take a Huanchaco-bound bus or combi from the main road towards the coast (I just asked around and got one quickly, the combi I took in Huaraz was actually headed to Huanchanco).

I then spent the evening walking around Huanchaco, past the pointed **caballitos de totora** (The reed fishing craft pulled up on the beach) and along the sea.

The next day, I spent the morning learning how to surf, but you may very well just enjoy the beach and the weather.

Then my afternoon was spent going up the hill and seeing the old parts of town. Not really interesting, but I needed to rest a bit.
If you like surfing, don't hesitate to enjoy more days.

I stayed at Totora Surf Hostel, they provide surf lessons (Extra cost, obviously)

## Day 8: Huanchaco

> I went surfing in Huanchaco before returning to Trujillo for the night bus south.

At **Ola Surf House**, a group lesson lasts **2:00** and costs 90 soles (23,10€), including board and wetsuit. An individual lesson is 120 soles (30,80€), and daily board-and-wetsuit rental is 45 soles (11,50€).

Return to Trujillo for a bus to **Lima**. The local bus from Huanchaco takes **0:30–0:45**; budget 3–5 soles (0,80–1,30€). Evening departures run roughly **19:00–23:15**, taking **9:30–11:00**; advertised fares start at 40 soles (10,30€), with more spacious seats costing more. Services use **Terrapuerto Trujillo** or private terminals such as those around **Avenida América Sur**.

## Day 9: Lima to Paracas

After the overnight bus, use the morning for **Museo Larco** in **Pueblo Libre**. Its galleries cover pre-Columbian ceramics, textiles and metalwork; the visible storage collection makes clear how much material sits beyond the main exhibition. Allow **2:00**, including the garden.

The museum is at **Avenida Simón Bolívar 1515**, open daily **09:00–19:00**. Adult admission is **45 soles (11,50€) online** or **50 soles (12,80€) at the door**. The museum lists buses **1013, 1253, 1234 and 1236** from **La Doce**, following Avenida Bolívar. Get off at **Aragón / Juan Valer Sandoval**, then walk to the entrance on **Calle Navarra**.

Continue by bus to **Paracas**, about **4:00** south. Advertised fares start at 45 soles (11,50€). PerúBus lists departures up to **12:45**; Jaksa also has afternoon and evening services. Boarding locations include **La Victoria/Nicolás Arriola** and **Atocongo**. The destination is **Paracas/El Chaco**; some other services stop on the highway near Pisco.

On arrival, walk the **El Chaco waterfront** between the harbour, fishing boats and restaurants.

I slept in Paracas Backpackers Hourse, one of the two big hostel. It didn't leave me any memories, but I gave it a really good rating online, so there's that.

## Day 10: Paracas

![The coast of Paracas National Reserve](images/blog/peru4.jpg)

Take the morning boat from **Muelle El Chaco** to the **Ballestas Islands**. Typical departures are **08:00 and 10:00** and the outing lasts around **2:00**. The boat passes the huge **Candelabro** geoglyph on the peninsula, then approaches the rocky arches and ledges of the islands, where sea lions, seabirds and Humboldt penguins can be seen. Passengers remain aboard.

A local operator advertises the boat at 40 soles (10,30€). The **SERNANP combined Ballestas–Paracas ticket is 17 soles (4,40€)**; a port charge is additional. The boat operator quotes 16 soles (4,10€) for its island-only admission and port charges together.

Afterwards, join a van circuit through **Paracas National Reserve**, normally 3–4 hours from El Chaco. Stops commonly include the **La Catedral viewpoint**, **Playa Roja** and the fishing settlement of **Lagunillas**. Walk the designated cliff viewpoints and look down towards the red sand and the meeting of bare desert with the Pacific. The reserve’s published visiting hours are **09:00–16:00**.

## Days 14 and 15: Colca Canyon

![The landscape outside Arequipa](images/blog/peru7.jpg)

I did Colca as one two-day trip from Arequipa, spending the night at the oasis on the canyon floor.

Absolutely worth it, if you wondered. And yes, I say that before we even started. If you go to Arequipa, it's probably for the Colca Canyon. It's hard to photograph due to the harsh light, but is a sight for your eyes.
You like nature? Condors? Plants? Hikes? Altitude?
Well, it's even more for you.
Tours are between 1 and 3 days (1 day means not going inside the canyon and spending like 9 hours driving, 3 days means an extra day in the hot spring. Now you know why I did two.)

Anyway, for the practical info: Trekking tours usually pick up from central hostels and hotels in Arequipa around **03:00–03:30** (yikes).
The drive goes through **Chivay** towards **Cruz del Cóndor**, around **5–6 hours including breakfast and stops**.
At the lookout, scan the canyon walls for condors riding the rising air, then continue towards **Cabanaconde** and the trailhead.

The common two-day route descends through the canyon villages to **Sangalle oasis**. It's a long walk, much of it steep descent, with an initial drop of about 1000 m.

Expect it to last the whole day. You need to earn the rest in Sangalle!

The next morning, start before sunrise, normally around **04:00–05:00**, for the sustained climb to **Cabanaconde**. Allow 2 to 4 hours for the ascent (Did it in two, but it's not a competition), followed by breakfast.
The return drive goes through Chivay and the high plateau; many programmes offer a hot-spring stop before reaching Arequipa around **18:00**.

Expect a total of **100€** for two days, bumped in price by the **20€ Colca Entrance Ticket**.

[This two-day Colca Canyon trekking tour from Arequipa (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/arequipa-l2859/from-arequipa-2-day-colca-canyon-trekking-tour-t674298/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher) follows the descent and Sangalle overnight. It starts and finishes in Arequipa.

Back in Arequipa, I took the night bus to **Cusco**. Services from **Terminal Terrestre or Terrapuerto** include **20:30–21:00** departures.
The journey takes 10 hours, with fares starting from 50 soles (12€).

## Day 16: Cusco

> Cusco is my favourite place in Peru. It's absolutely worth the hype and is best kept last because it's an highlught. Some visitors in Peru only do the Cusco region, you'll get to understand why.
> Cusco (The town, not the disney character) is beautiful, filled with indigineous culture.
> Seriously it should have been Peru's capital since it's such a cultural powerhouse and so much more memorable than Lima.

Begin in **Plaza de Armas**, then walk down to **Qorikancha**, where Inca stonework forms the base of the Santo Domingo complex. Continue via **Hatun Rumiyoc**, the twelve-angled stone and the uphill lanes of **San Blas**. Allow 2–3 hours for the streets and squares, with additional time for an interior visit to Qorikancha.

The **integral Cusco tourist ticket** costs 130 soles (33,30€) for a foreign adult, valid **ten days**. Starting it on Day 16 covers Sacsayhuamán today, the cultural performance, the Sacred Valley on Day 23 and the included museums on Day 24. Tickets are sold in person at **Avenida El Sol 103**, normally **07:30–18:00**, and at most included archaeological sites. The Qorikancha *site museum* is included; admission to the temple/Santo Domingo complex is separate.

For the afternoon, take a **Cristo Blanco** or **Señor de Huerto** bus from **Mercado Rosaspata** to **Q’enqo**, then walk towards **Sacsayhuamán**. At Q’enqo, follow the permitted route around the carved limestone and underground chamber. Sacsayhuamán has massive zigzag walls and broad views across Cusco; allow **1:30–2:00** there.

From Sacsayhuamán, descend on foot through **San Cristóbal** to the centre, around **0:30–0:45**. Visiting Q’enqo first avoids walking uphill again after Sacsayhuamán. The archaeological entries are already covered by the integral ticket.

I stayed in **Secret Garden Hostel**. Fun times, great place.

## Day 17: Rainbow Mountain

Leave Cusco before dawn for **Vinicunca, Rainbow Mountain**. Shared tours generally collect from central hotels around **04:00–05:00**, drive 3–4 hours with a breakfast stop, and reach a high trailhead for the walk to the striped ridge. The viewpoint is approximately 5 000 m, with Ausangate beyond it on a clear day.

Allow **1:30–2:00 uphill**, time at the viewpoint and **1:00–1:30 back**, depending on the road access used. The walk crosses open grazing country before the final steeper climb. A **Red Valley** extension adds walking time and may carry a separate community charge.

Allow an additional 30 soles (7,70€) as an entrance budget unless the selected option includes it; the community charges depend on the access route. Typical return is **17:00–18:00**.

## Days 18 to 22: Salkantay trek and Machu Picchu

> My five-day Salkantay booking included transport from Cusco, food, a guide and the Machu Picchu reservation.

The first day of my trek passed **Humantay Lake** before the first camp. Typical five-day departures collect in Cusco around **04:00–05:00**, with a drive of 3–4 hours through Mollepata towards Soraypampa. From the valley, the lake walk climbs for roughly **1:30–2:00**, reaching turquoise water below a steep glacier face at about 4 200 m. Descend to the valley camp afterwards; the precise campsite varies by operator.

The next day crosses **Salkantay Pass**, the highest and coldest part of the trek. The ascent leads into a bare basin below Salkantay, then reaches the pass at approximately 4 630 m. Beyond it, the route descends through high grassland towards **Wayracmachay** and warmer vegetation around **Chaullay**. Expect roughly **8–10 hours on foot** on a conventional itinerary, with substantial descent after the pass.

Below the pass, the landscape changes to cloud forest and cultivated slopes. Follow the lower valley paths towards **La Playa**, passing streams, small farms and increasingly dense vegetation. Typical programmes spend **5–6 hours walking** and overnight around **Santa Teresa or Lucmabamba**, sometimes using a short vehicle transfer between sections.

The final approach to **Aguas Calientes** varies between five-day routes. An itinerary through **Llactapata** climbs an old stone path through forest to ruins with a distant view towards Machu Picchu, then descends to **Hidroeléctrica**. That adds several hours before the final **10 km walk**, taking roughly **3:00** beside the railway towards **Aguas Calientes**. Some packages instead transfer towards Hidroeléctrica and omit Llactapata.

The return train costs extra on the cheapest option. The listing also identifies a 20 soles (5,10€) Mollepata/Humantay charge.

![Machu Picchu](images/blog/peru8.jpg)

The following morning, I entered **Machu Picchu** with the reservation included in my trek package, then returned by bus and train towards the Sacred Valley.

Buses leave **Aguas Calientes** for the entrance and take about **0:25–0:30**; walking up the steps takes approximately **1:30–2:00**. The visit follows the circuit and entry time printed on the ticket. Depending on that circuit, it combines views across the terraces with permitted paths through the Inca buildings and ceremonial areas. Allow 2–3 hours for a standard guided visit. The panoramic terrace and the lower urban routes require different tickets.

The **return train to Ollantaytambo** takes about **1:30–2:00**, followed by a road transfer to **Cusco** of another **1:30–2:00**. Packages differ in whether they include Machu Picchu entry, the uphill bus and the return train. A road return via Hidroeléctrica involves more walking and a longer journey. The current **SERNANP sanctuary charge is 11 soles (2,80€)** for foreign visitors, separate from the archaeological ticket.

## Day 23: Sacred Valley and Ollantaytambo

The Sacred Valley day includes **Chinchero → Moray → Maras salt mines → Ollantaytambo → Pisac**, with lunch in or near **Urubamba**. Leave Cusco around **06:30–07:00** on a shared tour.

At **Chinchero**, walk through the old village to the Inca terraces and plaza, then visit a weaving workshop to see how wool is spun and dyed. Allow about **0:45–1:00**. The drive from Cusco takes roughly **0:45–1:00**, then another **0:30–0:40** leads towards Moray.

At **Moray**, follow the permitted paths around the enormous circular terraces, descending in concentric rings into the ground. Allow **0:30–0:45** for the viewpoints and walk. Continue **0:20–0:30** to the **Maras salt mines**, where thousands of shallow white and brown evaporation pools step down the hillside. The visitor route stays at the overlooks and access paths above the working salt pans.

After lunch, continue to **Ollantaytambo**. Climb the broad fortress terraces towards the **Temple of the Sun**, then explore the water channels and ceremonial fountains below. The old town below has narrow Inca streets, with water still running in stone channels beside the houses.

**Chinchero, Moray, Ollantaytambo and Pisac are covered by the integral ticket bought on Day 16.** Without that pass, the Sacred Valley partial ticket costs 70 soles (17,90€) and lasts two days. **Maras is separate: 20 soles (5,10€)** in the current tour listing.

Continue through the valley to **Pisac** for the archaeological site above town, with its broad agricultural terraces and stone buildings overlooking the valley. The tour then returns to **Cusco**.

[This Sacred Valley tour with Moray and the Maras salt mines (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/ollantaytambo-l93095/from-cusco-sacred-valley-and-moray-salt-mines-t317787/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher) continues to Pisac after Ollantaytambo and returns to Cusco. The full circuit takes **12:00–12:30**. Budget approximately 30–45€ for the tour, with archaeological admissions and Maras charged separately unless included in the selected option.

## Day 24: Cusco

Start at **San Pedro Market** for the fruit, bread, cheese and cooked-food stalls. Walk back through **Plaza San Francisco**, then visit the **Museo Histórico Regional** in the house associated with Inca Garcilaso de la Vega. Its displays connect the region’s archaeological history with colonial Cusco. Continue to **Plaza Regocijo** and the **Museo de Arte Contemporáneo** for a smaller art stop.

Both museums are included in the integral tourist ticket, which is still within its ten-day validity. Allow around **1:00–1:30** for the regional museum and **0:30–0:45** for the art museum. The market and central streets are free.

In the afternoon, walk south along **Avenida El Sol** to the **Pachacútec Monument**, around **0:30 from Qorikancha**. The monument has a small historical display and a viewpoint above the busy roads of modern Cusco; its admission is also on the tourist ticket.

Take the overnight bus from **Cusco’s Terminal Terrestre** to **Puerto Maldonado**. Maldonado Tours lists a **19:00** departure, Palomino **20:00** and Transzela **21:00**. The journey takes 9–11 hours, with tickets from 50 soles (12,80€). Arrival is at the Puerto Maldonado terminal, where the lodge operator can arrange the start of the Amazon booking.

## Day 28: Amantani

![Lake Titicaca](images/blog/peru10.jpg)

From **Juliaca**, my route continued to **Puno**, then across **Lake Titicaca** to **Amantani**. I had met a Peruvian family during the Amazon trip and they invited me to their home on the island. It became one of the best side trips of the journey.

The public boat from **Puno’s main harbour**, at the lake end of Avenida Titicaca, traditionally departs around **08:00–08:30** and takes roughly 3–4 hours. The official published visitor guide gives 30 soles (7,70€), but that is an older reference rather than a verified 2026 fare.

Another route to Amantani goes via **Capachica and Chifrón**. From Puno, a colectivo to Capachica takes about **1:00–1:30**, followed by a walk of approximately **0:30** to Chifrón harbour and a shared launch of roughly **0:45–1:00** to Amantani. The shared boats leave when full, with no fixed timetable. Allow approximately **10–15€ per person** as a working budget for the shared road-and-boat stages.

On the island, walk through the villages and agricultural terraces, then climb towards the **Pachamama and Pachatata viewpoints**. The paths connect the settlements with the two hilltops, with wide views across the lake towards the mountains. Allow 2–3 hours for a hill walk and return, depending on the village where you are staying. The island is around 3 800 m, with the summits above 4 000 m.

I stayed with the family. An independent stay can be arranged directly with a host, agreeing meals and transport together.
