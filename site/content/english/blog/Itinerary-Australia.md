## Day 1: Alice Springs

Alice Springs airport is about 15 kilometres from town. I thought the shuttle was ridiculously expensive, so I hitchhiked. It took a while to find someone willing to stop; walking that far with the 40°C heat and the millions of flies (Plus a large backpack) wasn’t much of an alternative, tbh.

Alright, once in town, you can visit the **Royal Flying Doctor Service Museum** and walk up **Anzac Hill** to see Alice Springs and the mountains around it.
I was too lazy to visit the museum, as I'm bored of them, but I went up the hill to gaze upon the city and the surrounding desert, it's quite a nice viewpoint, you get to stretch your legs and adapt to the heat.

Then, you will spot the next spot we aim for: The **Telegraph Station**.
You might also spot the **Stuart Town Gaol**, the casinos and some kangaroos while walking along the center. You have to go through and take the time for a walk there, if you do have the time. Not that interesting, but part of the city.

So, the Telegraph Station It is farther north along the Todd River, it takes a bit of time to walk, assure you have plenty of water, especially if going during summer where afternoon temps goes often well above 40°C.

> Be careful at night, the natives wake up and they are experts at stone throwing and knifing.
> There is a private mercenary corps from eastern europe defending the city, especially around the casinos. Farther out becomes dangerous.
> Fetal alcohol syndrome is common place, be wary, be careful.

If you didn't book any tour, or rented any car/camper van, now is the time to do so.
Uluru and other attractions in the desert are hundreds of km away (Uluru is 460 km away from town!)

> Note, I went from Alice Springs, but technically, there is an airport in Yulara, much closer. But back then it was crazy expensive and flight were not common. 
> If you arrive in Yulara, you can ignore this Alice Springs portion and enjoy the attractions over two days instead of 3 or have a return to Alice Springs to go onwards.

## Days 2 and 3: Kata Tjuta and Uluru

I booked a three-day camping tour from Alice Springs, with pickup in town.

Our guide was **David**, the best guide I ever had (But I can't remember the company, sadly, it might not even exist know), he had so many stories, so much knowledge, a crazy life before working as a guide in australia (Dude was a bus driver in India, he played music in the evenings...
I paid the equivalent of 300€ for the three days, now it's closer to 600€. Good times.

Anyway, Uluru is around 450 kilometres away, so much of the first day was spent driving. We stopped at a camel/ostrich farm along the way and that was it. What a long day!
It started early (don't have the time), stopped at the farm for breakfast at 7:30, ended up in Uluru at 15:30.

We walked at **Kata Tjuta**, watched the sunset at **Uluru**, then had dinner at the campsite. That was my first night sleeping in a swag.

I cannot find the exact tour I booked in 2020. The next day, you have to wake up early. It's time to get to know **Ayer's rock** / **Uluru**. And see the sunrise, it's much better than the sunset, believe me I did both!

The walk around **Uluru** is around 10 kilometres for the full circuit. 
Doesn't seems like it, but with the heat, it's a lot.
I found it much more interesting up close: you see the caves, the markings and waterholes, see more life (Wildlife, tourists, natives...)

It will take two or three hours to see everything. Then a hour for the **Uluru-Kata Tjuta Cultural Centre**, to get to know even more.

After that we went to the Olgas, saw wild horses on the way, then to a camping for sleeping; It was a long drive to the camp. Lot of driving around as you see.

> That night, a spider bit me while I was sleeping in the swag.
> I ended up with a red lump the size of a golf ball around my elbow. That was scary, to say the least. Be careful folks.

## Day 4: Kings Canyon

![Kata Tjuta](images/blog/australia1.jpg)

The last day of the tour was for the **Kings Canyon Rim Walk**. The steep climb at the start is the hardest part, after that, you walk between sandstone domes and along the canyon, with a detour down to the **Garden of Eden**.

The minibus left early so we could get to see the sunrise, then we hiked all the way up (The hardest part) and spent around 3 hours in the canyon.

After the walk, we drove back to Alice Springs, 5 more hours of driving.

Then I got to visit Alice Springs a bit more, mostly shopping and resting for my last evening.

Resting up is important especially after close to 18 hours of driving
over three days.

> My honest review, 6 years later. A lot of driving, not that many things to be seen. It was around 3 hours of touristic attractions each day, not much more than that.
> But actually it was much more than that. The roads, spotting the wildlife, the stories, the food we made during camp, the camping we did with all the setup, the music, the pool (yes we had some pool time for the first night!).
> The journey was part of Uluru, flying in and out same day would not have been this fun. Renting a campervan would mean the same amount of adventures, for sure.

## Day 5: Darwin

![Flooded woodland after the heavy rain](images/blog/australia3.jpg)

Take a flight from **Alice Springs** to **Darwin**, only around two hours.
I recommend doing it here, as Alice Springs doesn't yield that much more things to do. I flew in the morning, as most flights leave at that time.

So, for the afternoon, I recommend a walk along **Smith Street** and down to the **waterfront**, where you can go to the wave lagoon and the **WWII Oil Storage Tunnels**.
Darwin ain't that fun, but if you have time, see the **Crocosaurus Cove**, It's a great way to kill the time!
Honestly, that should fill the whole afternoon, the city is quite large afterall.

## Day 6: Kakadu National Park (Day 1)

![A crocodile in Darwin](images/blog/australia2.jpg)

> My day trip to Kakadu was cancelled because of heavy rain. I stayed in Darwin and visited the **Museum and Art Gallery of the Northern Territory**, the **George Brown Darwin Botanic Gardens** and **Crocosaurus Cove** instead.
> Since it's an itinerary, I'm going with what I was supposed to do.

For **Kakadu**, rent a car or book a tour from Darwin. The drive to Jabiru takes roughly three hours.

Stop at **Mamukala Wetlands** for the bird hide, then head to **Ubirr** for the rock paintings. Walk up to the lookout above them for the view over the Nadab floodplain. Spend the night around Jabiru or Cooinda.

Flooding can close the road to Ubirr; check the [park’s access report](https://kakadu.gov.au/news/access-and-alerts/) before setting off.

## Day 7: Kakadu National Park (Day 2)

From Cooinda, take a **Yellow Water cruise** to see the wetlands, birds and crocodiles. The nearby **Warradjan Aboriginal Cultural Centre** has exhibits on Aboriginal life in Kakadu. You can find [both in the Yellow Water area](https://kakadu.gov.au/discover/regions/yellow-water-ngurrungurrudjba/).

Then visit the rock paintings at **Burrungkuy (Nourlangie)** before driving back to Darwin. Allow around three hours for the return, plus stops.

> I spent this day in **Charles Darwin National Park** instead. You can see the termites mounds, see the old bunkers and get a day in the nature, it's a miniature, worse Kakadu.
> Take [**bus 5 towards Casuarina**](https://nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1562505/route-5-tt-darwin-to-casuarina-october-2025.pdf) from **Harry Chan Avenue, Bay E**, to [**Coonawarra Road after Menmuir Street (stop 186)**](https://transitapp.com/en/region/darwin/darwinbus/bus-5). Walk along Coonawarra Road and Bowen Road, then cross Tiger Brennan Drive to the park entrance. The bus runs on weekdays, except public holidays (checked October 2026).

### One more day in Darwin: Charles Darwin National Park

**Charles Darwin National Park** has old WWII bunkers, walking and mountain-bike trails, mangroves and a lookout over Darwin.

The [park entrance](https://nt.gov.au/parks/find-a-park/charles-darwin-national-park) is off Tiger Brennan Drive, opposite Bowen Road in Winnellie, around 5,5 kilometres from the centre. You can cycle or drive there; the [bus directions are under Day 7](#day-7-kakadu-national-park-day-2).

### Litchfield National Park

Add a day from Darwin for the waterfalls, swimming holes and termite mounds.

[This full-day Litchfield tour from Darwin (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/darwin-l357/litchfield-national-park-t37261/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher) includes the return transport.

### Nitmiluk and Katherine Gorge

**Katherine Gorge**, in **Nitmiluk National Park**, and **Edith Falls** can be visited on a long day trip from Darwin: around 14 hours on most organised trips. If you have time, spend a night in Katherine instead.
