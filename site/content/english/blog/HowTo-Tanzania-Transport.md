## Main transport logic

Tanzania is not one transport system. The north works around **Kilimanjaro International Airport**, **Arusha**, safari vehicles and road transfers. The coast works around **Dar es Salaam**, ferries and domestic flights. Long road journeys are common, but distances are large and night travel is not a good default.

For most independent travellers, use buses for city-to-city moves, ferries or flights for Zanzibar, and an operator vehicle for safaris. Use trains only where the route actually matches your plan.

Plan Tanzania by regions rather than by map distance. **Arusha/Moshi**, **Dar es Salaam/Zanzibar**, **Kilwa**, **Mwanza/Kigoma** and the **southern parks** do not connect quickly with each other. If you only have one or two weeks, choose a north-plus-Zanzibar route or a coast-plus-south route, not both.

## Arrival points

**Kilimanjaro International Airport (JRO)** is the main airport for Arusha, Moshi and the northern safari circuit. The airport lists taxis and a shared airport shuttle outside the terminal to Moshi or Arusha. Air Tanzania also lists shuttle pickup points in Arusha and Moshi for its passengers.

**Julius Nyerere International Airport (DAR)** is the main airport for Dar es Salaam, the south and many domestic connections. Use taxis, hotel transfers or ride-hailing where available. Leave margin for Dar traffic.

**Abeid Amani Karume International Airport (ZNZ)** is the airport for Zanzibar. It is useful after safari if a domestic flight is available from your route.

If your trip starts with safari, flying into **JRO** is usually cleaner than arriving at DAR and crossing the country by road. If your trip is Zanzibar and coast-heavy, DAR or ZNZ makes more sense.

**Route:** Kilimanjaro Airport to Arusha  
**Best option:** Airport shuttle, taxi or pre-arranged transfer.

**Departure point:** Kilimanjaro International Airport terminal area.  
**Arrival point:** Arusha hotel, bus area or safari operator office.  
**Booking method:** Airport shuttle, airline shuttle where applicable, hotel or operator transfer.  
**Important notes:** If you arrive late, book a transfer in advance.

**Route:** Arusha to Moshi  
**Best option:** Bus, minibus or arranged transfer.  
**Typical duration:** About 2 to 3 hours.  
**Departure point:** Arusha bus area or hotel pickup.  
**Arrival point:** Moshi centre or hotel.  
**Booking method:** Local bus offices, accommodation or transfer operator.  
**Important notes:** Useful before Kilimanjaro foothill walks or if your flight uses JRO.

**Route:** Arusha to northern safari parks  
**Best option:** Safari vehicle with licensed operator.  
**Typical duration:** Varies by park and itinerary; expect early departures.  
**Departure point:** Arusha hotel or operator office.  
**Arrival point:** Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Ngorongoro, Serengeti or campsite/lodge.  
**Booking method:** Safari operator.  
**Important notes:** Do not plan park entry as a normal public-transport trip.

**Route:** Dar es Salaam to Zanzibar  
**Best option:** Ferry or flight.  
**Typical duration:** Ferry timings vary by vessel and sea conditions; flights are short but add airport time.

**Arrival point:** Zanzibar ferry terminal in Stone Town or ZNZ airport.  
**Booking method:** Ferry company website or office, airline website, agency or hotel.  
**Important notes:** Zan Fast Ferries operates between Dar es Salaam, Zanzibar and Pemba. Check the current sailing before going to the port.

**Route:** Zanzibar ferry terminal to beaches  
**Best option:** Taxi, shared transfer or dala-dala if travelling light.

## Rail in Tanzania

The railway is improving, but it does not replace the normal safari-and-coast route.

**TRC** operates passenger rail and the new SGR corridor between Dar es Salaam, Morogoro and Dodoma. The official TRC site has live booking/search tools and current rail notices. Use it when your route follows that corridor.

**TAZARA** operates the Tanzania-Zambia line. TAZARA announced the resumption of cross-border passenger service in February 2026, with the Mukuba service between Dar es Salaam and New Kapiri Mposhi. It is useful for a slow overland trip to Zambia, not for Zanzibar or the northern safari circuit. TAZARA advises booking well in advance.

For rail, verify the exact departure station. Dar es Salaam has different transport nodes, and a taxi transfer across the city can take longer than expected. Do not place a ferry, flight and train connection too close together.

## Domestic flights

Domestic flights are useful when road transport wastes days: Dar es Salaam to Zanzibar, Dar to Kilimanjaro/Arusha, Dar to Mwanza, Dar to Kigoma, and safari-airstrip connections when budget allows. Air Tanzania lists domestic destinations including Dar es Salaam, Zanzibar, Kilimanjaro, Arusha, Mwanza, Kigoma and others. Precision Air also lists domestic routes including Zanzibar, Kilimanjaro and Serengeti.

Check luggage rules carefully. Small aircraft and safari-airstrip flights may have stricter baggage limits than normal trunk routes.

Safari-airstrip flights are convenient but not interchangeable with normal city airports. Confirm the exact airstrip, baggage limit, pickup vehicle and lodge/operator coordination before booking.

## Local transport

In Dar es Salaam and Arusha, taxis, ride-hailing where available, hotel transfers and local buses all exist, but comfort varies. In Zanzibar, use taxis, shared transfers or dala-dalas depending on budget and tolerance for slow travel.

For ferries and airports, keep your passport accessible. For buses, buy from an office or a clearly identified operator when possible, and avoid last-minute night departures if you can.

## Bus terminals and timing

For long-distance buses, identify the correct terminal or operator office before the travel day. Dar es Salaam has several departure areas depending on route. Arusha and Moshi departures are easier to handle, but early departures still require a taxi or hotel-arranged transfer.

Buy from a proper office when possible and keep the luggage tag. On long routes, choose daytime travel. Night buses may exist, but they are not the best default for visitors dealing with road safety, stops and arrival logistics.

## Safari transport questions to ask

Before paying a safari operator, confirm:

- pickup and drop-off city;
- whether JRO pickup is included;
- whether park fees, meals and water are included;
- vehicle type and group size;
- final drop-off after Serengeti or Ngorongoro;
- what happens if roads or weather change the route.

This matters because the safari vehicle is not just transport. It controls park access, timing, meals and where you sleep.
