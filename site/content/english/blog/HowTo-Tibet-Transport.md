## The rule that decides everything

Foreign travellers need organised travel and permits for the Tibet Autonomous Region. Independent travel is not the normal option. China Tibet Online states that foreign tourists must be organised by travel agencies with confirmed routes and need a Tibet Travel Permit. For border or non-open areas, additional permits may apply.

In practice, your agency controls the route, guide, vehicle, hotel registration and checkpoints. Transport planning is therefore not only a question of trains and buses.

Before you pay for flights or trains, confirm the exact permit package with the agency. A simple Lhasa permit is not the same as permission for Everest, Gyirong, Ngari or other sensitive border areas. If the permit or route is not approved, the transport booking does not solve the problem.

Useful rule: plan the route first, then book the transport that matches the approved route.

## Getting to Lhasa

**Route:** Mainland China to Lhasa by train  
**Best option:** Train if you want the plateau rail experience and have time.  
**Typical duration:** Long-distance; varies heavily by origin.  
**Departure point:** Cities such as Xining, Chengdu, Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou or others depending on schedule.  
**Arrival point:** Lhasa Railway Station.  
**Booking method:** 12306 or a rail booking platform, plus permit handling through your agency.  
**Important notes:** You still need the Tibet permit to board. Do not book assuming a train ticket alone is enough.

The train is usually chosen for the experience and gradual climb, not because it is the fastest. **Xining** is the most common practical gateway for the Qinghai-Tibet railway, while Chengdu, Beijing, Shanghai and Guangzhou services are much longer. Use 12306 for live schedules and your agency for permit handling.

**Route:** Mainland China to Lhasa by air  
**Best option:** Flight if time is limited.  
**Typical duration:** Depends on origin; common gateways include Chengdu, Chongqing, Xian and Kunming.  
**Departure point:** Mainland China airport.  
**Arrival point:** Lhasa Gonggar Airport.  
**Booking method:** Airline/OTA plus permit handling through your agency.  
**Important notes:** Flying saves time but gives less gradual altitude exposure.

Flights are common from **Chengdu**, **Chongqing**, **Xian**, **Kunming** and other Chinese cities depending on schedule. They are useful if you have limited time, but the first day in Lhasa should stay light. Do not land and go straight into Potala stairs or a high-pass road day.

**Route:** Kathmandu / Nepal to Tibet  
**Best option:** Agency-arranged route only, by air or overland when open and permitted.  
**Typical duration:** Depends on route, border status and permit processing.  
**Departure point:** Kathmandu airport or Nepal border route.  
**Arrival point:** Lhasa or Gyirong/Tibet-side route depending on itinerary.  
**Booking method:** Tibet/Nepal agency coordination.  
**Important notes:** Rules change and border areas need additional paperwork. Confirm current crossing status before building the trip around it.

## Lhasa airport to city

Lhasa Gonggar Airport is far from central Lhasa. A Tibet government airport guide lists the civil aviation airport bus between Lhasa city and the airport, with the Lhasa city departure point at **No. 1 Niangre Road, Chengguan District** and airport pickup outside **Gate 5 of Terminal 3**. It lists about 70 minutes for the city-airport route and ticketing through the Lhasa airport express mini-program or ticket windows.

Your tour agency may include airport pickup. Confirm this before arrival; it is usually easier than solving the bus after a high-altitude flight.

If pickup is not included, ask the agency whether you are allowed to use the public airport bus independently or whether the guide must meet you. Rules and agency practice can differ; do not assume a normal free-arrival model.

## Moving around Lhasa

Inside Lhasa, expect a mix of walking, taxis and tour vehicles. The main visitor circuit is compact enough to combine **Potala Palace**, **Jokhang**, **Barkhor**, **Sera**, **Drepung** and **Norbulingka** over two or three days.

Do not plan Lhasa like a low-altitude city break. Keep the first day light, avoid running up stairs, and leave buffer time if you feel unwell.

The old-city area around Jokhang and Barkhor is walkable. Potala viewpoints, Sera, Drepung and Norbulingka usually need a vehicle, taxi or agency transfer. Even short distances can feel slower at altitude, so avoid back-to-back visits that require climbing stairs.

## Road route to Shigatse and Everest

**Route:** Lhasa to Shigatse via Yamdrok and Gyantse  
**Best option:** Guided vehicle arranged by the agency.  
**Typical duration:** Full travel day with stops.  
**Departure point:** Lhasa hotel.  
**Arrival point:** Shigatse hotel.  
**Booking method:** Included in tour arrangements.  
**Important notes:** This is a checkpoint and itinerary-controlled route, not a casual self-drive.

This route normally includes high passes, Yamdrok Lake viewpoints, Gyantse and arrival in Shigatse. It is a transport day with sightseeing, not a relaxed lake excursion. Carry layers, sunglasses, water and snacks in the vehicle.

**Route:** Lhasa to Namtso  
**Best option:** Guided vehicle if your itinerary and season allow.  
**Typical duration:** Long day or overnight depending on road, weather and plan.  
**Departure point:** Lhasa hotel.  
**Arrival point:** Namtso visitor area or approved stop.  
**Booking method:** Agency-arranged.  
**Important notes:** High altitude, weather and road conditions matter. Seasonal access can change.

**Route:** Shigatse to Everest Base Camp / Rongbuk area  
**Best option:** Guided vehicle.  
**Typical duration:** Long travel day; often split depending on itinerary and rules.  
**Departure point:** Shigatse or nearby overnight stop.  
**Arrival point:** Everest/Rongbuk area or designated tourist base area.  
**Booking method:** Included in tour arrangements with required permits.  
**Important notes:** Weather, altitude, road conditions and permit scope matter. Conditions can change quickly.

Expect checkpoints and long stretches without much flexibility. If the itinerary includes Everest, ask where you sleep, what heating is available, whether oxygen is carried, and what happens if weather closes views or roads.

**Route:** Everest area to Gyirong / Nepal border  
**Best option:** Guided vehicle if your itinerary exits to Nepal.  
**Typical duration:** Long mountain road day or split route.  
**Departure point:** Everest/Rongbuk or Tingri area.  
**Arrival point:** Gyirong or border area.  
**Booking method:** Agency-arranged with border-area paperwork.  
**Important notes:** Border rules are sensitive and can change. Confirm current crossing status before building the trip around it.

## Booking sequence

1. Choose the approved route with an authorised agency.
2. Confirm which permits are required for that route.
3. Book entry transport only when the agency tells you the permit timing works.
4. Keep buffers before international flights, especially after Everest or border routes.

The agency may ask for passport and visa scans before applying for permits. Processing time can vary, and holidays can slow things down.

## What to carry on transport days

Carry passport, permit copy if provided, warm layers, water, snacks, sunglasses, basic medication and power bank. For long road days, do not pack everything in the trunk if you will need it before the next hotel.

If you are prone to motion sickness, prepare before Yamdrok, Gyantse, Everest or Gyirong routes. High altitude plus winding roads is a poor place to discover the problem.

## What not to assume

- Do not assume you can rent a car and drive yourself.
- Do not assume a hotel booking and train ticket are enough for entry.
- Do not assume all Tibetan cultural regions have the same rules: Tibetan areas in Yunnan, Sichuan, Qinghai and Gansu are not the same as the Tibet Autonomous Region.
- Do not plan tight onward flights after an Everest or border route.
- Do not assume public buses can replace the agency vehicle for foreign travellers.
- Do not assume road closures or permit changes will be solved on the day.
