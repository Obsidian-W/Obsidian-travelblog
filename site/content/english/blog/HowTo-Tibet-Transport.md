## The rule that decides everything

Foreign travellers need organised travel and permits for the Tibet Autonomous Region. Independent travel is not the normal option. China Tibet Online states that foreign tourists must be organised by travel agencies with confirmed routes and need a Tibet Travel Permit. For border or non-open areas, additional permits may apply.

In practice, your agency controls the route, guide, vehicle, hotel registration and checkpoints. Transport planning is therefore not only a question of trains and buses.

Before you pay for flights or trains, confirm the exact permit package with the agency. A simple Lhasa permit is not the same as permission for Everest, Gyirong, Ngari or other sensitive border areas. If the permit or route is not approved, the transport booking does not solve the problem.

Useful rule: plan the route first, then book the transport that matches the approved route.

## Getting to Lhasa

**Route:** Mainland China to Lhasa by train  
**Best option:** Train if you want the plateau rail experience and have time.  
**Typical duration:** Long-distance; varies heavily by origin.  
**Departure point:** Cities such as Xining, Chengdu, Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou or others depending on schedule.  
**Arrival point:** Lhasa Railway Station.  
**Booking method:** trip.com, plus permit handling through your agency.  
**Important notes:** You still need the Tibet permit to board. Do not book assuming a train ticket alone is enough (Apparently, you can get in trouble for that)

The train is usually chosen for the experience and gradual climb, not because it is the fastest. **Xining** is the most common practical gateway for the Qinghai-Tibet railway, while Chengdu, Beijing, Shanghai and Guangzhou services are much longer. **Route:** Mainland China to Lhasa by air  
**Best option:** Flight if time is limited.  
**Typical duration:** Depends on origin; common gateways include Chengdu, Chongqing, Xian and Kunming (And even Shangri-La!)
**Departure point:** Any mainland China airport.  
**Arrival point:** Lhasa Gonggar Airport.  
**Booking method:** Airline/OTA plus permit handling through your agency.  
**Important notes:** Flying is the quickest way to reach Lhasa from mainland China.

Flights are common from **Chengdu**, **Chongqing**, **Xian**, **Kunming** and other Chinese cities depending on schedule. They are useful if you have limited time.

**Route:** Kathmandu / Nepal to Tibet  
**Best option:** Agency-arranged route only, by air or overland when open and permitted.  
**Typical duration:** Depends on route, border status and permit processing.  
**Departure point:** Kathmandu or Gyirong (depends on the agency?)
**Arrival point:** Lhasa or Gyirong route depending on itinerary.

## Lhasa airport to city

Lhasa Gonggar Airport is far from central Lhasa. A Tibet government airport guide lists the civil aviation airport bus between Lhasa city and the airport, with the Lhasa city departure point at **No. 1 Niangre Road, Chengguan District** and airport pickup outside **Gate 5 of Terminal 3**. It lists about 70 minutes for the city-airport route and ticketing through the Lhasa airport express mini-program or ticket windows.

Your tour agency may include airport pickup. Confirm this before arrival so that you do not pay for a transfer you will not use.

If pickup is not included, ask the agency whether the guide must meet you or whether you can use the public airport bus. The bus runs between Terminal 3 and **No. 1 Niangre Road** in central Lhasa, takes about 70 minutes, and can be booked through the Lhasa Airport Express mini-program or at the airport ticket window. At Terminal 3, use the stop outside **Gate 5**.

## Moving around Lhasa

Inside Lhasa, use walking and public buses where the permit and agency arrangements allow independent movement. Confirm routes in Amap on the day because stop names and services can change. Use the Chinese name of the destination when searching. The main visitor circuit is compact enough to combine **Potala Palace**, **Jokhang**, **Barkhor**, **Sera**, **Drepung** and **Norbulingka** over two or three days.

The old-city area around Jokhang and Barkhor is walkable. For Potala viewpoints, Sera, Drepung and Norbulingka, use public buses or confirm an agency transfer.

## Road route to Shigatse and Everest

**Route:** Lhasa to Shigatse via Yamdrok and Gyantse  
**Best option:** Guided vehicle arranged by the agency.  
**Typical duration:** Full travel day with stops.  
**Departure point:** Lhasa hotel.  
**Arrival point:** Shigatse hotel.  
**Booking method:** Included in tour arrangements.  
**Important notes:** This is a checkpoint and itinerary-controlled route, not a casual self-drive.

This route normally includes high passes, Yamdrok Lake viewpoints, Gyantse and arrival in Shigatse. It is a transport day with sightseeing, not a relaxed lake excursion. Carry layers, sunglasses, water and snacks in the vehicle.

**Route:** Lhasa to Namtso  
**Distance and time:** Roughly 177 kilometres and 2.5 hours each way from Lhasa, excluding stops.  
**Public transport:** There is no fixed public bus from Lhasa to Namtso. Foreign visitors travel in the vehicle provided by the agency, followed by the scenic-area shuttle where required.  
**Season:** May to September is the principal visiting period. Winter access is less reliable.

**Route:** Shigatse to the Qomolangma tourist base area and Rongbuk  
**Typical schedule:** Two days return from Shigatse. Official regional itineraries travel to the visitor base and Rongbuk on the first day, stay near the mountain, then return through the Gyawula viewpoint on the second.  
**Public transport:** Foreign visitors use the agency vehicle specified in the approved itinerary. Public intercity buses do not provide access through the route's checkpoints or inside the scenic area.  
**Visitor boundary:** Tourist access ends in the designated base area near Rongbuk. The protected core above Rongbuk, including the climbers' camp, has been closed to ordinary tourism since 2018.  
**Overnight:** Tours use accommodation near the mountain or in a nearby settlement. The exact property and room type should appear in the tour contract.

**Route:** Everest area to Gyirong / Nepal border  
**Best option:** Guided vehicle if your itinerary exits to Nepal.  
**Typical duration:** Long mountain road day or split route.  
**Departure point:** Everest/Rongbuk or Tingri area.  
**Arrival point:** Gyirong or border area.  
**Booking method:** Agency-arranged with border-area paperwork.  
**Important notes:** Border rules are sensitive and can change. Confirm current crossing status before building the trip around it.

## Permits and transport bookings

The authorised agency applies for the permits using copies of your passport and Chinese visa. It must also give you the permit document or copy required for boarding the flight or train to Lhasa. Keep the agency's deadline for sending those documents when choosing the entry date.

## What to carry on transport days

Carry passport, permit copy if provided, warm layers, water, snacks, sunglasses, basic medication and power bank. For long road days, do not pack everything in the trunk if you will need it before the next hotel.

The roads around Yamdrok, Gyantse, Everest and Gyirong include long winding sections, so carry any motion-sickness medication you normally use.

## What not to assume

- Do not assume you can rent a car and drive yourself.
- Do not assume a hotel booking and train ticket are enough for entry.
- Do not assume all Tibetan cultural regions have the same rules: Tibetan areas in Yunnan, Sichuan, Qinghai and Gansu are not the same as the Tibet Autonomous Region.
- Do not plan tight onward flights after an Everest or border route.
- Do not assume public buses can replace the agency vehicle for foreign travellers.
- Do not assume road closures or permit changes will be solved on the day.
