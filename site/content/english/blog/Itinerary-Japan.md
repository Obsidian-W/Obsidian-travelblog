## Day 1: Tokyo

![Akihabara at night](images/blog/japan2.jpg)

Start at **Senso-ji** in Asakusa. Enter through Kaminarimon, follow the shops along Nakamise to the temple and walk behind the main hall to Asakusa Shrine.

Take the Ginza Line to **Ueno** for Shinobazu Pond and Ueno Toshogu, with its gilded shrine buildings. The **Tokyo National Museum** has samurai armour, Buddhist sculpture and woodblock prints; allow two or three hours for its Japanese collection. Continue one stop on the JR Yamanote Line to **Akihabara** for the electronics shops and arcades.

## Day 2: Tokyo

Begin at **Meiji Shrine**, reached through the wooded grounds beside Harajuku Station. The large wooden torii and rows of donated sake barrels are on the approach to the shrine courtyards.

Walk back through Harajuku towards Omotesando, then take the Yamanote Line one stop to **Shibuya** for the crossing and Hachiko statue. Continue on the same line to **Shinjuku**. The Metropolitan Government Building is west of the station; Omoide Yokocho's narrow restaurant alleys are beside its north-west side.

## Day 3: Tokyo

Begin around the outer market at **Tsukiji**, walk through Ginza and continue to the **Imperial Palace East Gardens**. Take the Chiyoda Line north for **Nezu Shrine**, then walk through Yanaka before the shops close.

Nezu Shrine has a hillside path beneath rows of red torii. From there, walk east to Yanaka Cemetery and the shopping street at Yanaka Ginza; Nippori Station is nearby for the JR journey back.

## Day 4: Nikko

![Toshogu in Nikko](images/blog/japan1.jpg)

![Details inside the Nikko shrine complex](images/blog/japan1b.jpg)

Take a morning **Tobu limited express from Asakusa to Tobu-Nikko**, around **two hours**. From the station, take the local bus to Shinkyo and walk through Rinno-ji, **Toshogu**, Futarasan Shrine and Taiyuin. Return to Tokyo on the same railway.

The limited express requires a reserved seat and a supplement to the basic Tobu fare. [This Nikko day tour from Tokyo also visits Kegon Falls and Lake Chūzenji (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/tokyo-l193/nikko-kegon-falls-lake-chuzenji-toshogu-temple-t862525/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

Inside Toshogu, continue beyond Yomeimon to the main shrine, the sleeping-cat carving and the staircase to Tokugawa Ieyasu's tomb. Futarasan Shrine and Taiyuin stand farther west along the same forest road.

## Day 5: Hakone

The **Odakyu Romancecar** takes about **1:20** from Shinjuku to **Hakone-Yumoto**. From there, take the mountain railway to Gora, the cable car to Sounzan and the ropeway over **Owakudani** to Lake Ashi. The lake boat and bus complete the circuit back to Hakone-Yumoto. Return to Tokyo for the night.

At Owakudani, walk the open volcanic paths and try the black eggs sold around the ropeway station. Cross Lake Ashi by boat, get off near Hakone Shrine and walk to the lakeside torii before taking the bus back.

## Day 6: Kanazawa

![Kenrokuen in Kanazawa](images/blog/japan8.jpg)

Take the **Hokuriku Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kanazawa**, around **2:30**. Walk through Omicho Market, Kanazawa Castle and **Kenrokuen**, then continue to the Higashi Chaya district.

Kenrokuen has ponds, stone lanterns and old pines supported by wooden props. Across the Asano River, **Higashi Chaya** preserves wooden teahouse façades; cross back for the smaller riverside lanes of **Kazuemachi**.

## Day 7: Shirakawa-go

![Shirakawa-go](images/blog/japan3.jpg)

Take the [Hokutetsu highway bus](https://www.hokutetsu.co.jp/highway-bus/takayama) from Kanazawa Station's west exit, bay 4, to **Shirakawa-go**. The journey takes about 1:15 and requires a reservation. Walk among the gassho-style houses and climb to the Shiroyama viewpoint, then return to Kanazawa by bus.

**Wada House** is open to visitors and shows the upper floors beneath the steep thatched roof.

## Day 8: Kyoto

Take the **Hokuriku Shinkansen from Kanazawa to Tsuruga**, then change to the **Thunderbird limited express for Kyoto**. Allow around **two hours** for the journey. From Kyoto Station, **bus 86 or 206** stops at **Gojozaka**, below **Kiyomizu-dera**. Climb to the temple's wooden terrace, then walk down through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka to Yasaka Shrine, Gion and the Shirakawa canal.

[A guided evening walk through Gion is available here (affiliate link)](https://www.getyourguide.com/kyoto-l96826/kyoto-gion-magical-night-walking-tour-with-geisha-trivia-t659135/?partner_id=H1N6CGH&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Day 9: Kyoto

![Fushimi Inari](images/blog/japan4.jpg)

Take the JR Nara Line to Inari for **Fushimi Inari**, then return through Kyoto Station and continue by bus to **Kinkaku-ji**.

Start Fushimi Inari early and continue beyond the first dense rows of gates towards the upper paths. After Kinkaku-ji, continue west to **Ryōan-ji** and **Ninna-ji**. Ryōan-ji has the famous fifteen-stone rock garden; Ninna-ji has palace rooms, gardens and a five-storey pagoda.

## Day 10: Nara

Take a **Kintetsu train from Kyoto to Kintetsu-Nara**, around **0:40–0:50**; this station is closer to the park than the JR station. From the station, visit Kōfuku-ji, continue to the Great Buddha Hall at Tōdai-ji, then follow the path past Nigatsudō to the lantern-lined approach of Kasuga Taisha. Return through the pond pavilion at Ukimido and the old merchant streets of Naramachi, then take the train back to Kyoto.

## Day 11: Osaka

![Osaka Castle](images/blog/japan6.jpg)

![Central Osaka at night](images/blog/japan5.jpg)

Take a **JR Special Rapid from Kyoto to Osaka**, around **0:30**, or a **Hankyu train from Kyoto-Kawaramachi to Osaka-Umeda**, around **0:45**. Visit Osaka Castle, then use the metro for Shinsekai, Kuromon Market and **Dotonbori**. Return to Kyoto after the evening in Namba.

Osaka Castle's interior is a modern museum; the exterior and park were the stronger part for me. Save Dotonbori for after dark and return on one of the late trains to Kyoto.

## Day 12: Himeji

Take the **Shinkansen from Kyoto to Himeji**, around **0:45–1 hour**. Himeji Station has luggage storage for the walk to **Himeji Castle** and **Koko-en Garden**. Afterwards, continue by **Shinkansen from Himeji to Hiroshima**, roughly **an hour**.

The castle route climbs through a series of defensive gates before entering the wooden keep. **Koko-en**, beside the moat, is divided into enclosed gardens with ponds, waterfalls and a tea garden.

## Day 13: Hiroshima

Take the tram from Hiroshima Station to the **Atomic Bomb Dome**. Walk through Peace Memorial Park past the Children's Peace Monument and Memorial Cenotaph to the **Peace Memorial Museum**, whose exhibits document the bombing and its effects on the city's inhabitants. Hiroshima Castle is about 20 minutes north of the park; **Shukkeien** is another 15 minutes east of the castle.

## Day 14: Miyajima

![Miyajima from Mount Misen](images/blog/japan7.jpg)

Take the **JR Sanyo Line from Hiroshima to Miyajimaguchi**, around **0:30**, then the **ten-minute ferry to Miyajima**. Visit Itsukushima Shrine, then climb or take the ropeway towards Mount Misen. Return to Hiroshima and take the **Shinkansen to Hakata**, around **an hour**.

Check the tide time for the shrine's torii, then walk through Daisho-in before starting up Mount Misen. The ropeway ends below the summit: there is still an uphill walk past the mountain's temple buildings to the observation platform.

## Day 15: Fukuoka

Walk around **Hakata**, Canal City and the Kushida Shrine area. The onward crossing leaves from Hakata Port International Terminal, reached by bus from Hakata Station or Tenjin.

Add Tocho-ji and the temple streets between Gion and Hakata Station, then eat around the yatai stalls when they open in the evening.

## With more days

### Stay overnight in Nikko

Stay overnight for the bus farther uphill to **Lake Chūzenji** and **Kegon Falls**, keeping the shrine walk for the first day. The bus from **Tobu-Nikko to Chūzenji Onsen** takes around **0:50**; Kegon Falls is a short walk from the bus terminal.

### Give Kyoto another two days

Keep one day for **Arashiyama**, its riverside, **Tenryū-ji** and the lanes north of the bamboo grove. Use the other for **Nijō Castle**, its painted palace rooms and gardens, then the paths beside the **Philosopher's Walk**, **Nanzen-ji** and its brick aqueduct.

### A full day in Fukuoka

Take the **Nishitetsu train from Fukuoka-Tenjin to Dazaifu**, changing at **Futsukaichi**, around **0:40**. Visit **Dazaifu Tenmangū**, its wooded shrine grounds and the **Kyushu National Museum** behind it. Back in Fukuoka, **Ōhori Park** and the ruins of **Fukuoka Castle** make another city walk west of Tenjin.
